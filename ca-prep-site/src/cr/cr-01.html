What corporate reporting is, and who uses it

TermMeaning
Financial reportingIdentifying, measuring and communicating economic information so others can make decisions and assess management's stewardship: recording transactions, grouping them, and presenting periodic results in set formats.
Corporate reportingWider: also covers auditors' reports, assurance reports, internal audit, environmental reports, integrated reporting, corporate governance, CSR and narrative reports such as the management commentary.
Management accountingInternal reports for managers, in any format or frequency. The line blurs when external reporting drives decisions, for example a bonus based on reported profit, or loan covenants based on reported ratios.
A complete set of IFRS statementsStatement of financial position · statement of profit or loss and OCI (one or two statements) · statement of changes in equity · statement of cash flows · notes (accounting policies, supporting detail, items not recognised such as contingent liabilities).
Bangladesh requirementThe Companies Act 1994 requires every company to prepare accounts each year that give a true and fair view under IFRS. The Financial Reporting Act 2015 requires PIEs to prepare IFRS accounts.

Fair presentation (IAS 1): accounts must present fairly, meaning faithfully represent transactions; this normally follows from applying IFRS. A departure from IFRS is allowed only in extremely rare cases where compliance would be so misleading that it conflicts with the objective of financial statements.

Exam skill: challenge management's judgement

Directors may choose treatments that suit them (a higher profit for a bonus, legal form over substance). The examiner expects you to spot the incentive, apply the right IFRS, and say what the correct treatment is, even when there is no single "right" answer.

Users and their decisions
Users: present and potential investors, employees, lenders, suppliers, customers, government and agencies, the public. Decisions: buy, hold or sell shares; assess stewardship; assess ability to pay employees; assess security for loans; set tax policy; decide distributable profits; national statistics; regulation.
InformationShowsMain statement
Financial positionResources controlled, financial structure, liquidity and solvency, ability to adaptStatement of financial position
Financial performanceProfit and comprehensive income; helps predict future cash generationP/L and OCI; changes in equity
Changes in financial positionOperating, investing and financing cash flows; least affected by judgement, so seen as more reliableStatement of cash flows

The objective of general purpose financial statements is to give information useful to a wide range of users for economic decisions; focusing on investors' needs usually meets most users' needs. Statements also show management's stewardship, but cannot give all the information needed to judge it. Users increasingly also want sustainability and ESG information.

The role of the audit and the expectation gap

ISA 200 para. 3: "The purpose of an audit is to enhance the degree of confidence of intended users in the financial statements. This is achieved by the expression of an opinion by the auditor on whether the financial statements are prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework."

The audit gives shareholders a check on directors' stewardship and supports trust in the wider economy, alongside directors' duties and corporate governance. All companies in Bangladesh must be audited.

Why only reasonable assurance (ISA 200)Judgement and uncertainty in financial reporting · selective testing · limits of internal control · evidence is persuasive, not conclusive · impractical to test every item · collusion and misrepresentation · the auditor's work is full of judgement.
True and fairTrue: not false, matches reality; follows standards and law; correctly taken from records that reflect real transactions. Fair: reflects the commercial substance and is free from bias. The auditor's defence is showing due skill and care, and that judgements were reasonable on the evidence at the time.

Expectation gap: the public expects audits to say whether a company will survive or is a good investment; audits are not designed to do this. KAMs and longer reports try to narrow the gap, but failures such as Carillion keep it wide.

Acceptance or continuance (legal, ethical, practical); engagement letter Plan: understand the entity, evaluate controls, assess risk, set materiality Strategy and plan Test controls (with sampling) Substantive work: analytics and tests of detail Evaluate results, final analytics Report, plus other reporting duties

The whole audit is done with professional scepticism (ISA 200; Chapter 5).

Legal duties of directors and auditors in Bangladesh

Directors' report CA 1994 s.184

Must be attached to every balance sheet laid at the general meeting
(1) State of the company's affairs · amount proposed for reserves · recommended dividend · material changes and commitments between the year end and the report date
(2) Changes during the year in the nature of the business, in subsidiaries or their business, and in the classes of business the company has an interest in
(3) Fullest information and explanation on every reservation, qualification or adverse remark in the auditor's report
(4) Signed by the chairman if authorised, otherwise by the directors who sign the balance sheet (s.189)
(5) Fine up to Tk 5,000 for each offence

Extra disclosures for listed companies BSEC Corporate Governance Code

Business review

Industry outlook; segment or product performance; risks; COGS, gross and net margin; extraordinary gains or losses; variance between quarterly and annual results; deviation from last year; 5-year key data.

Funds and transactions

Related party transactions and their basis; use of IPO, RPO and rights proceeds; explanation if results fall after a public offer; reasons if no dividend.

Directors' statements

Accounts present fairly; proper books kept; suitable, consistent policies and prudent estimates; IAS/IFRS followed (departures disclosed); internal control sound and monitored; no significant going concern doubt (or reasons).

Governance data

Board meetings and attendance; shareholding pattern (related parties, directors, CEO, CS, CFO, head of internal audit and families, top 5 executives, holders of 10% or more); directors' pay; profile of directors being appointed or reappointed.

Loans to directors CA 1994

A company may not lend to, guarantee, or give security for a loan to: a director; a firm where a director is a partner; a private company where a director is a director or member; or a public company whose managers act on a director's instructions.

Exception

Allowed only for a banking company, a private company that is not a subsidiary of a public company, or a holding company lending to its subsidiary, and only if approved by the board and the general meeting and shown specifically in the balance sheet. The loan may never exceed 50% of the paid-up value of the director's own shares. Breach: fine up to Tk 5,000 or 6 months' simple imprisonment, and joint liability to repay. This also applies to book debts that began as loans.

Auditor's powers and duties CA 1994 s.213

5

Standards, regulators and public interest entities

The IAASB (set up by IFAC) issues ISAs and quality management standards. In Bangladesh the FRC adopts ISAs and ethical standards; ICAB also issues ethical guidance. An auditor may depart from an ISA only in exceptional cases, and must justify it. Recent IAASB projects: ISA 701 (KAMs, 2015), other information, estimates, risk assessment (ISA 315), group audits (ISA 600) and audit evidence.

Public interest entity: Financial Reporting Act 2015 s.2(8) and FRC Gazette notifications (unofficial translation)
A. Any entity meeting one of these: (1) bank company (Bank Company Act 1991); (2) an issuer of securities that reports to BSEC; (3) financial institution; (4) microfinance institution; (5) insurer; (6) annual revenue above Tk 500 million (Tk 50 crore) in the previous year (SRO-34); (7) at the previous year end: at least 50 employees (Rule 2023), total assets above Tk 300 million (SRO-35), and liabilities excluding equity above Tk 100 million (SRO-36)
B. Also included if they meet A: state-owned companies or commercial bodies; statutory authorities; NGOs doing voluntary work in the private sector; similar institutions

Other entity of public interest (OEPI): a newer ethics term for entities that are not PIEs but still have public interest aspects, such as a pension fund.

Correction to the Audit and Assurance key

The ICAB Audit and Assurance answer key quoted PIE limits of Tk 5 crore revenue, Tk 3 crore assets and Tk 1 crore liabilities. The workbook's FRC wording gives Tk 500 million (50 crore), Tk 300 million (30 crore) and Tk 100 million (10 crore). Use the workbook figures.

Other current pressures: going concern after COVID-19 and geopolitical shocks (balancing scepticism with supporting business); political, economic, social (climate, diversity) and technological (big data, blockchain, AI) change. Be ready to discuss these developments clearly.

Audit quality management and documentation

Quality is managed at the firm level (ISQM 1: risk assessment, governance and leadership, ethics, acceptance and continuance, engagement performance, resources, information and communication, monitoring and remediation) and on each audit (ISA 220 Revised, led by the engagement partner). Professional bodies also set standards and monitor firms. "Quality control" was replaced by "quality management" (ISQM 1 and 2, ISA 220 Revised).

UK FRC findingsLesson
2017 thematic review on quality procedures"Lines of defence" good practice; more senior review of audit work needed
2018 audit culture reviewCulture = values, attitudes and behaviours. Put ethical principles first; good audits serve society; reward good work, not just punish bad; use root cause analysis; use independent non-executives to monitor culture.
2020 enforcement review and Developments in auditEthical and auditing standards not always followed; not enough evidence; weak scepticism; too little challenge of management on goodwill impairment and going concern; group teams not showing how they assessed component work; weak internal reviews
2021 Developments in auditShare of audits needing improvement still unacceptable
ISA 230 para. 8: document enough "to enable an experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the audit, to understand" the nature, timing and extent of procedures, the results and evidence, significant matters and conclusions, and significant professional judgements.
Example: TrucksToGo Ltd (reviewing a junior's work)

The junior: (1) relied on the FD's oral confirmation that there are no other non-current assets; (2) could not inspect many sampled vehicles because they were on the road, so instead traced vehicles on site back to the register; (3) accepted the vehicle manager's word that damaged vehicles (engines missing) were "under repair"; (4) concluded all assets are correctly stated and valued.

ProblemAction
Oral representation is weak, internal evidence for completenessGet it in the written representation letter; also trace assets seen and purchase invoices to the register
Floor-to-register tests completeness, not existenceInspect sampled vehicles when they return, or check registration documents, tracking or insurance records
Damaged vehicles: impairment and rights not testedCheck ownership documents; assess impairment (IAS 36) and whether they will be repaired or scrapped
Conclusion not supportedValuation not tested at all: recalculate depreciation, review useful lives; document the work and a supported conclusion

AreaKey points
Management's dutyEnsure the entity complies with laws and regulations, using policies, monitoring, training, legal advisers, codes of conduct and a register of significant laws
Auditor's dutyGet sufficient evidence on compliance with laws that directly affect material amounts and disclosures (tax, pensions, company law); for other laws (licences, environment, health and safety), enquire of management and inspect correspondence with regulators; stay alert to non-compliance
If non-compliance is foundUnderstand the act and its circumstances; evaluate the effect on the accounts; discuss with management and TCWG (unless that would tip off); get legal advice if needed; reconsider management's representations
Reporting to management and TCWGPromptly; go to the audit committee or higher if management is involved
