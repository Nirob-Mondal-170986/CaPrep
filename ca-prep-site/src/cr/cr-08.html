1

Completion review and uncorrected misstatements

Completion steps: governance and quality issues → review the financial statements → completion procedures → report to the board → the auditor's report. For listed entities the report cannot be dated until the engagement quality review is complete (ISA 220).

Overall review (by a senior person)Compliance with law and IFRS (checklists); policies appropriate, consistent and disclosed; substance over form; consistent with what the audit found; any new matters; bias in presentation; final analytical procedures on adjusted figures.
ISA 450Accumulate every misstatement except those clearly trivial (a much smaller amount than "not material"; document the threshold). Types: factual (no doubt), judgemental (unreasonable estimates or policies), projected (from samples). As the total nears materiality, do more work or ask management to adjust.
Schedule of uncorrected misstatements: reading it

Last year's closing inventory was understated by £21,540 and it is still uncorrected. How does it affect this year?

It reverses: last year's profit was understated, and this year's opening inventory is too low, so this year's profit is overstated by the same £21,540. The cumulative balance sheet effect is nil. Prior-year items that reverse cancel out; items that grow each year (an unprovided debt, obsolete stock) build up. Show both the profit or loss and the balance sheet effect, for the current and prior year.

Subsequent events (ISA 560, IAS 10)

When foundAuditor's duty and action
Year end → date of auditor's reportActive duty. Procedures close to the report date: ask management, read board minutes and the latest management accounts, follow up items open at year end. Check adjusting events are adjusted and non-adjusting ones disclosed
Report date → statements issuedNo duty to look, but if told: discuss, decide if amendment is needed. If amended: extend procedures and issue a new report dated no earlier than the new approval. If not amended: report not yet released → modify the opinion; already released → tell management not to issue, and if issued, act to prevent reliance (take legal advice)
After issueNo duty to look. If a fact existed at the report date: discuss; if revised, audit the revision, check that everyone is informed, and issue a new report with an emphasis of matter or other matter paragraph referring to the revision note. If management does nothing, act to prevent reliance (legal advice)
Interactive question: Extraction (oil company)

Year end 31 Dec 20X7. The report was signed, and the statements authorised, on 28 March 20X8. On 1 April a major oil leak is found.

Accounting: if the leak existed at 31 December (even unseen) → adjusting event and provision. If it started after year end → non-adjusting but material, so disclose. If after 28 March → a 20X8 matter. An expert is needed to date the leak. Auditor: no duty to look after signing, but the timing raises questions about concealment and other information withheld. If the statements are not yet issued, consider amendment; if amended, do more procedures and issue a new report.

3

Going concern (ISA 570 Revised)

Management must make an explicit assessment. If liquidation or ceasing to trade is intended or unavoidable, the going concern basis is not used (IAS 10); use a break-up basis with impairments and onerous contract provisions, disclosed under IAS 1.

Auditor's evaluationStay alert throughout. Evaluate management's process, assumptions and plans with scepticism. If the assessment covers less than 12 months from the year end, ask management to extend it. Ask about events beyond that period. A history of profits and easy finance needs less detailed work.
When doubt existsEvaluate management's plans · test the cash flow forecast (reliable data, supported assumptions) · consider new information · get written representations on plans and whether they are feasible.

Procedures: analyse and discuss forecasts and interim accounts; read loan agreements for breaches; read board and committee minutes; ask lawyers about claims; confirm support from related or third parties and their ability to pay; plans for unfilled orders; subsequent events; confirm facilities; regulatory reports; support for planned asset sales; sensitivity analysis on key assumptions.

ConclusionAuditor's report
Basis appropriate, no material uncertaintyUnmodified
Material uncertainty, adequately disclosedUnmodified + separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section (not a KAM; the KAM section cross-refers)
Material uncertainty, not adequately disclosedQualified or adverse

Communicate to TCWG (unless they all manage the entity): whether there is a material uncertainty, whether the basis is appropriate, whether disclosure is adequate, and the effect on the report. A significant delay in approving the accounts may itself be a going concern sign.

Revised standard

The workbook mentions the 2023 exposure draft. The IAASB has since issued ISA 570 (Revised 2024), which moves the 12-month period to start from the date of approval of the financial statements, for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2026. For current exams, use 12 months from the year end and mention the change. (Approval date is already the UK rule.)

Comparatives (ISA 710) and written representations (ISA 580)

Corresponding figuresPrior-period figures are part of this year's statements; the opinion covers the current period only (usual in Bangladesh and the UK). Check the policies are consistent and the amounts agree with last year (or are properly restated).
Comparative financial statementsThe opinion refers to each period presented. Representations needed for every period covered.

Corresponding figures are mentioned in the report only when: (a) last year's modification is unresolved, so modify this year too (refer to both periods, or explain the effect on comparability); or (b) a material misstatement is found in last year's unmodified figures and not restated, so qualify or give an adverse opinion. If last year was unaudited, say so in the report and still do ISA 510 work.

Written representationsRules

Other specific onesWhere evidence cannot be obtained otherwise, e.g. intentions: plans affecting asset values; actual and contingent liabilities; title and charges on assets; laws and contracts; all known control deficiencies; subsequent events; related parties; fraud
LimitsSupport other evidence but are never enough on their own
Date and formA letter addressed to the auditor, dated as near as possible to, but not after, the auditor's report
Doubtful or refusedRe-assess management integrity and all evidence. If the general representations are unreliable or refused → disclaimer
Interactive question: Leaf Oil (which go in the letter?)

(1) No adjusting subsequent events. (2) Big revaluation gain based on directors' belief in a coming boom; market survey shows no rise. (3) Directors say the company owns 75% of a new subsidiary. (4) Directors say 500 gallons of oil in Warehouse B belong to Leaf Oil.

(1) Yes (required by ISA 560). (2) No: this is a disagreement over a misstatement, not something to "represent away". (3) No: check the companies registry. (4) No: get documentary evidence of ownership.

6

Key audit matters (ISA 701)

Key audit matters: matters that, in the auditor's judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the current period, selected from matters communicated with TCWG.
1 All matters communicated with TCWG 2 Those needing significant audit attention: higher risks, significant judgements, significant events 3 Those of most significance in the audit

Step 3 factors: interaction with TCWG; importance to users; complex or subjective policies; size and nature of misstatements; audit effort; difficulty applying procedures; control deficiencies; whether several areas are affected.

Each KAM saysWhy it was among the most significant · how it was addressed (procedures, judgements) · reference to the related disclosure note. The introduction states there is no separate opinion on these matters.
RulesNot a modification and not a substitute for one, or for missing disclosure · modified-opinion matters and going concern material uncertainties go in their own sections (the KAM section cross-refers) · may be omitted only in very rare cases (law, or harm outweighs public interest) · "no KAMs" must be stated · document how KAMs were chosen.

Typical KAMs: goodwill impairment (judgemental assumptions; valuation expert used) and revenue recognition (bundled product and service contracts, risk of recognising too early).

Modified opinions, emphasis of matter and other matter (ISA 705, 706)

CauseMaterial, not pervasiveMaterial and pervasive
Material misstatement (disagreement)Qualified: "except for the effects of…"Adverse: do not give a true and fair view
Cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence (scope limitation)Qualified: "except for the possible effects of…"Disclaimer: unable to form an opinion
Pervasive: not confined to specific items; or, if confined, a substantial part of the statements; or, for disclosures, fundamental to users' understanding.

Scope limitations arise from circumstances beyond the entity's control (records destroyed), the timing of the auditor's work (appointed after the count), or management (refuses access). Try alternative procedures first. A pervasive limitation imposed by management after acceptance: withdraw if possible, otherwise disclaim. Do not accept an engagement that would need a disclaimer from the start. A disclaimer may also be given for multiple interacting uncertainties. The heading changes to "Basis for Qualified/Adverse/Disclaimer of Opinion".

Emphasis of matterA matter properly presented or disclosed that is fundamental to understanding: unusual litigation or regulatory uncertainty; a major subsequent event; early adoption of a pervasive new standard; a major catastrophe; revised accounts (ISA 560). Opinion not modified. Not used if the matter is already a KAM.
Other matterA matter not in the financial statements that is relevant to understanding the audit, the auditor's responsibilities or the report, e.g. prior period audited by another auditor or unaudited, or restrictions on use. Not used for a KAM.

Other information (ISA 720) and other reports (ISA 800, 805, 810)

Other information: financial and non-financial information in the annual report other than the financial statements and auditor's report (directors' report, chair's statement, climate disclosures, KPIs).

The auditor reads it for material inconsistency with the financial statements or with knowledge gained in the audit, and for anything otherwise misstated. No opinion is given on it. Arrange to get it before the report date; if it comes later, get a written representation that it will be provided.

Problem foundAction
Financial statements need changing and management refusesQualified or adverse opinion
Other information (received before the report) needs changing and is refusedTell TCWG; describe the uncorrected misstatement in the "Other Information" section, or withdraw
Found after the report dateIf corrected, check it and how users are told; if not, take legal advice

"Other Information" section: management is responsible; what was received; the opinion does not cover it; the auditor's duties; and either "nothing to report" or a description of the uncorrected misstatement.

StandardKey points
ISA 800: special-purpose frameworksTax basis, cash basis, regulator or contract rules. Agree the terms; state the purpose and any restriction on use; emphasis of matter on the basis of accounting; opinion "in accordance with" that basis
ISA 805: single statements or elementsItems are interrelated, so check the work is practicable; give separate opinions; no unmodified opinion on a single statement if the full set has an adverse opinion or disclaimer (an element may be possible if not a major part, published separately and not prohibited)
ISA 810: summary financial statementsOnly if the full statements were audited. Opinion: "consistent in all material respects" or "a fair summary". If the full report is adverse or a disclaimer, give no opinion on the summary. If the summary is inconsistent and not fixed → adverse
XBRL taggingNot required in Bangladesh; in the UK the tagging is not audited and is not "other information". Tagging services to an audit client raise ethical issues
Exam focus

Reporting questions follow a pattern: (1) accounting issue → (2) is it material (calculate)? → (3) misstatement or lack of evidence? → (4) pervasive? → (5) the exact report wording or section (qualified "except for", MURGC section, EoM, KAM). Commit to one conclusion. For going concern, list indicators with numbers and give specific procedures. Practice: Russell, Truckers, Extraction, Branch plc, SafeAsHouses plc.
