1

Events after the reporting period (IAS 10)

Adjusting eventsGive more evidence about conditions that existed at the year end: a customer's insolvency revealing an irrecoverable debt; sale of inventory below cost; settlement of a court case; final cost or proceeds of assets bought or sold before the year end; profit-sharing or bonus amounts for the year; discovery of fraud or errors.
Non-adjusting eventsConditions that arose after the year end: fire or flood, major acquisitions or disposals, share issues, large falls in market value, new legal commitments, announced restructuring. Disclose the nature and estimated effect if material.

Dividends declared after the year end are not a liability (no obligation at the year end); disclose them. Going concern: if after the year end management decides to liquidate or cease trading (or has no realistic alternative), the going concern basis cannot be used. The IAS 10 period runs to the date the statements are authorised for issue (disclose that date and who authorised it), not the AGM.

Provisions, contingent liabilities and contingent assets (IAS 37)

Provision: a liability of uncertain timing or amount. Recognise when there is a present obligation (legal or constructive) from a past event, an outflow is probable, and a reliable estimate can be made.
Likelihood of outflowObligation is presentObligation is only possible
Probable (more likely than not)Provide (or disclose a contingent liability in the rare case of no reliable estimate)Disclose contingent liability
PossibleDisclose contingent liabilityDisclose contingent liability
RemoteNothingNothing
MeasurementBest estimate: expected value for large populations (warranties), most likely outcome for a single obligation (adjusted for other outcomes). Discount when the time value is material, using a pre-tax rate reflecting the liability's risks (or a risk-free rate with risk-adjusted cash flows, but don't count risk twice). Unwinding the discount is a finance cost.
Not providedFuture operating losses (no past event). Costs the entity could avoid by its own future actions (e.g. fitting equipment to meet a new law: provide only for fines for not complying). Contingent assets: disclose only if probable. Reimbursements: separate asset only if virtually certain, capped at the provision.

Disclosure: a reconciliation of movements for each class (including unwinding and rate changes), a description, uncertainty and expected timing. In extremely rare cases where disclosure would seriously prejudice a dispute, give only the general nature and explain why.

Onerous contracts, restructuring and climate commitments

AreaRule
Onerous contractUnavoidable costs exceed expected benefits. Provide for the lower of the cost of fulfilling and the penalty for exiting. Costs of fulfilling include incremental costs and an allocation of other directly related costs (2022 amendment). Test related assets for impairment first. Leases are mostly under IFRS 16 now; only short-term or low-value leases can create an IAS 37 onerous provision
RestructuringConstructive obligation only when there is a detailed formal plan (business, locations, employees affected, costs, timing) and it has been announced to those affected, or started. Include only direct costs (redundancy, termination of contracts); exclude retraining or relocating continuing staff, marketing, new systems, and future operating losses
Climate commitmentsNew laws create an obligation for fines or for obligations already triggered, not for spending the entity could avoid. A public net-zero pledge may create a constructive obligation if specific, announced and with a pattern of honouring pledges. Disclose contingent liabilities; consider asset lives and impairment
DecommissioningPresent value of dismantling and restoration added to the asset's cost (IAS 16) and depreciated; unwind the discount each year; changes in estimate adjust both asset and provision
Interactive question: restructuring in two stages

Plan approved 1 Dec 20X5 and announced in detail on 2 Dec: Stage 1 closes 3 lines in 20X6 (3,000 redundancies; 500 transfers). Stage 2 (4 more lines, 3,500 jobs in 20X7) was only "probable" until confirmed in detail on 1 Nov 20X6. A "take or pay" contract runs to the end of 20X7 for 100,000 tonnes per line per year.

Stage 1: provide in 20X5 for redundancy, termination and close-down staff costs. Stage 2: provide in 20X6 (when detailed). Costs of moving staff, plant and inventory to continuing operations are expensed when incurred. Test all affected assets for impairment in 20X5 (announcement is an indicator). The take-or-pay contract becomes onerous for the tonnes the closed lines cannot use, from when they stop until the end of 20X7.

Announced 25 Sept 20X7; it always honours public commitments. Payments: £20m (Sept 20X8), £25m (Sept 20X9), £30m (Sept 20Y0); rate 8%; factories have 30 years left; spending enhances the factories.

Constructive obligation from the announcement. 20 × 0.926 + 25 × 0.857 + 30 × 0.794£63.765m

Auditing provisions, litigation and claims

QuestionProcedures
Complete list?Schedule of provisions and contingencies with opening balance, movements and closing balance; consider the business (warranties, restoration, legal) for missing ones
Present obligation?Correspondence; discussion with directors (have they created a valid expectation?); announcements, minutes and contracts
Probable outflow?Payments after the year end; letters from lawyers, banks, customers, insurers and suppliers before and after year end; how similar past provisions were settled; possible reimbursement
Amount?Recalculate, including discounting; compare with later payments and past experience; challenge management's estimate (ISA 540)
Disclosure?Contingent liabilities and assets correctly classified and described; reconciliation provided
Litigation (ISA 501)Ask management and TCWG; read minutes and lawyers' correspondence; examine the legal expenses account; talk to in-house lawyers. If claims exist or may exist, seek direct communication with the entity's external lawyers: a letter prepared by management and sent by the auditor, listing the claims and management's assessment, asking the lawyer to confirm or correct. Update near the report date. Meet the lawyer only with management's permission.
RefusalIf management refuses permission to contact the lawyers, or the lawyer will not reply, and there is no other evidence → qualified opinion or disclaimer for lack of evidence.
Interactive question: Newthorpe Engineering (dismissed MD)

The MD was dismissed for gross misconduct with no compensation, claims unfair dismissal and 2 years' salary under a service contract. The accounts show a "resignation", with no provision or disclosure.

Accounting: provide if the claim is probable to succeed; otherwise disclose a contingent liability (nature, estimated effect, uncertainties, possible reimbursement) unless remote. Accrue legal costs. "Resignation" is misleading. Procedures: read the service contract (maximum claim, grounds for dismissal); review the disciplinary hearing and compliance with employment law; correspondence for admissions of liability; lawyers' view on the chance of success and likely costs; insurance cover; written representations.

Exam focus

Always run the three recognition tests in order and conclude: provide / disclose / nothing. Watch for: constructive obligations from public statements; restructuring announced after the year end (non-adjusting); future losses (never provided); discounting and unwinding as finance cost; decommissioning capitalised into PPE. For audit, the lawyer's letter and post-year-end payments are the strongest evidence.
