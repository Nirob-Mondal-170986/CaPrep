Share-based Payment

IFRS 2: scope and terms; equity-settled transactions (direct and indirect measurement, vesting conditions, mid-year grants, modifications, cancellations); cash-settled transactions; settlement choices; group arrangements; disclosures and EPS effect; distributable profits and buying back own shares; and audit focus.

1

Scope and terminology

Share-based payment: the entity receives goods or services and pays with its own (or a group company's) equity instruments, or with cash based on the price of those instruments.
TypeAccounting
Equity-settled (share options, free shares)Dr expense (or asset) / Cr equity
Cash-settled (share appreciation rights, phantom shares)Dr expense / Cr liability
Choice of settlementDepends on who has the choice (topic 4)

Out of scope: shares issued to acquire a business (IFRS 3); transactions with employees as shareholders (rights issues open to all); contracts for non-financial items under IFRS 9. A purchase paid with shares worth a fixed £ amount is a financial liability (IAS 32), not IFRS 2.

Key terms: grant date (both parties agree the terms); vesting period (conditions must be met); vesting conditions = service conditions and performance conditions (market or non-market); intrinsic value = share price − exercise price; fair value of an option includes time value (option pricing model, e.g. Black-Scholes, binomial).

Measurement basisDirect: goods or services from non-employees at their fair value (if reliable), at the date received. Indirect: employee services, and where the direct value is unreliable, at the fair value of the equity instruments granted at grant date.
Why expense it?Options have a real cost (dilution and value given up); employees provide services that are consumed, so it is an expense. It reduces earnings but makes companies with different pay mixes comparable.
2

Equity-settled: vesting conditions

Cumulative expense at each year end = number of instruments expected to vest × grant-date fair value × proportion of vesting period passed. Charge for the year = cumulative − previous cumulative.
ConditionEffect
Vests immediatelyExpense in full at grant
Service and non-market performance (profit, sales, cost targets)Update the estimate of instruments expected to vest each year; true up to actual. If not met, cumulative expense reverses
Market conditions (share price targets)Built into grant-date fair value; expense recognised if service is given, even if the market target is missed. No reversal
Non-vesting conditions (e.g. employee stops saving in a scheme)Built into fair value; if the employee or entity fails one, treat as a cancellation
Worked example: 400 employees × 100 options, £20 fair value, 3 years

Expected leavers 20% (year 1), 25% (year 2); actual leavers 20, 25, 10 (55 in total).

Year 1: 400 × 100 × 80% × £20 × 1/3£213,333
Year 2: 400 × 100 × 75% × £20 × 2/3 = 400,000 cumulative£186,667
Year 3: 345 × 100 × £20 = 690,000 cumulative£290,000
Worked examples: Sally (non-market) and Jeremy (market)

Sally: 10,000 options at £21; cost-cutting target looked achievable in year 1, unlikely in year 2, met in year 3. Jeremy: 10,000 options at £18 with a 25% a year share price growth target; served 3 years; growth only 24%.

Sally: year 1 70,000; year 2 reverse (70,000); year 3 210,000£210,000 total
Jeremy: 10,000 × £18 ÷ 3 each year, despite missing the market target£60,000 a year

Mixed conditions (revenue £1bn and share price £50): expense if the non-market and service conditions are met, regardless of the share price. If revenue is missed or staff leave, reduce for those.

Mid-year grants, modifications and cancellations

Worked example: Yarex (grant on 1 November)

100,000 options to each of 50 managers; 3-year service; 75% expected to stay; grant-date fair value £2 (later values £2.40 and £3.80 are irrelevant).

Total: 37.5 × 100,000 × £2 = £7.5m; £2.5m a year£7.5m
Year 1 (2 months): 2.5m × 2/12£416,667
Year 2 (no change in estimate)£2.5m
EventTreatment
Modification that increases fair value (repricing)Keep the original grant-date charge; add the incremental fair value (after − before modification) over the remaining vesting period
Modification that reduces fair valueIgnore; continue the original charge (the entity cannot reduce the cost)
Cancellation or settlement (by entity or employee) during vestingAccelerate the unrecognised charge immediately. Payment to employees = buy-back of equity, up to fair value at that date; any excess → P/L
Vested options not exercised or lapsedNo reversal; may transfer within equity
Cancel and replaceTreat as a modification if the new grant is identified as a replacement
Worked examples: repricing and cancellation

(1) 20 managers × 1,000 options at £20 over 4 years (£100,000 a year). On 1 July of year 2 the exercise price is cut; fair value goes from £2 to £11. (2) 23 expected managers × 2,000 options at £33 over 3 years. Cancelled halfway through year 2 with 24 managers in post; fair value then £60; compensation £63 per option.

(1) Extra cost 20,000 × (11 − 2) = 180,000 over 30 months; year 2: 100,000 + 6 × 6,000£136,000
(2) Accelerate: 2,000 × 24 × 33 = 1,584,000 − 506,000 already charged£1,078,000
Compensation 2,000 × 24 × 63 = 3,024,000: up to fair value (× £60) → equity£2,880,000
Excess over fair value → P/L£144,000
4

Cash-settled and choice of settlement

Cash-settled: recognise a liability as services are received; remeasure at fair value at each reporting date and on settlement, with changes in P/L. Vesting conditions affect the number expected to vest as for equity-settled. After vesting, keep remeasuring until exercised; cash paid = intrinsic value.

Worked example: 500 employees × 100 cash SARs, 3-year vesting

Expected to vest: 405 (year 1), 400 (year 2); 403 actually vest. Fair values £14.40, £15.50, £18.20, £21.40; exercised: 150 at £15 (end of year 3), 140 at £20 (year 4), 113 at £25 (year 5).

Year 1: liability 405 × 100 × 14.40 × 1/3194,400
Year 2: liability 400 × 100 × 15.50 × 2/3 = 413,333; expense218,933
Year 3: cash 225,000 + liability 253 × 100 × 18.20 = 460,460; expense272,127
Year 4: cash 280,000; liability 113 × 100 × 21.40 = 241,820; expense61,360
Year 5: cash 282,500; liability nil; expense (total £787,500 = cash paid)40,680
Employee choosesA compound instrument: liability component = fair value of the cash route; equity component = fair value of the share route − cash route (at grant date). Liability remeasured; equity not. If shares are chosen, transfer the liability to equity. Example: 8,000 shares (fair value £19) or cash for 7,000 shares (£21 at grant): equity £5,000 (152,000 − 147,000); liability rises to 7,000 × £42 = £294,000; total equity at the end £299,000.
Entity choosesCash-settled if the entity has a present obligation to pay cash (cannot issue shares, stated policy or past practice of paying cash); otherwise equity-settled. Cash paid on an equity-settled award = buy-back from equity; any excess value → P/L.

Group arrangements: the entity receiving the services recognises an expense even if a parent grants the shares or pays. Equity-settled in the subsidiary's accounts if the parent settles in its own shares (Cr equity: capital contribution); cash-settled if the subsidiary must pay cash or deliver its parent's shares that it has to buy.

5

Disclosures, EPS and distributable profits

IFRS 2 disclosures: description of each scheme; option movements (outstanding at start, granted, forfeited, exercised with weighted average share price, expired, outstanding and exercisable at end) with weighted average exercise prices; range of exercise prices and remaining life; how fair value was measured (model and inputs: share price, exercise price, volatility, life, dividends, risk-free rate, early exercise); total expense (separately for equity-settled); liability carrying amount and vested intrinsic value. KMP awards also fall under IAS 24.

EPS: fixed-term employee options count as options in diluted EPS from grant date (add unrecognised IFRS 2 cost to the exercise price); performance-based awards are contingently issuable shares (Chapter 11).

Distributable profits (workbook, UK rules)Dividends only from accumulated realised profits less accumulated realised losses. Extra depreciation on revalued assets can be treated as realised. Public companies must also keep net assets at least equal to called-up share capital + undistributable reserves (share premium, capital redemption reserve, net unrealised gains). E.g. capital £800k + premium £200k; net assets £1.25m → maximum dividend £250k.
Buying back sharesReasons: return surplus cash, support share price and EPS, buy out dissenting or retiring shareholders, adjust gearing, provide shares for employee schemes. Under UK law it must be from distributable profits or a fresh issue (or capital for private companies), with a capital redemption reserve to protect creditors.
Bangladesh point

The workbook's distributable profit and buy-back rules are the UK Companies Act 2006. In Bangladesh, the Companies Act 1994 and the articles (Schedule I, Table A) allow dividends only out of profits, and BSEC rules govern how listed companies declare and pay dividends. General buy-back of own shares has not been permitted under the Companies Act 1994 (redeemable preference shares aside). Check the current Act and BSEC rules before answering.

Audit focus

RiskProcedures
Completeness of schemesRead board and remuneration committee minutes, directors' contracts and scheme rules; ask HR; check for awards by the parent or group companies
ClassificationEquity vs cash-settled, who has the choice, past practice of cash settlement
Fair valueAgree the model and inputs (volatility, life, dividends, risk-free rate) to market data; use a valuation expert where complex; check the grant date (when terms were agreed)
Vesting estimates (management bias)Compare expected leavers with staff turnover history; test performance forecasts against budgets; check true-up to actual
Calculations and eventsRecompute the charge, modifications, cancellations and cash-settled remeasurement; agree exercises to the share register and bank
DisclosureIFRS 2, IAS 24 (directors' awards) and EPS dilution
Exam focus

Use a year-by-year table: number expected to vest × fair value × time fraction = cumulative; charge = difference. Use grant-date fair value for equity, year-end fair value for cash. Then say Dr/Cr (equity or liability) and the effect on EPS and gearing. Watch for directors wanting to avoid the expense (e.g. calling it equity-settled when the company always pays cash).
