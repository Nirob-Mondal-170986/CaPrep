Financial Instruments: Presentation and Disclosure

IAS 32: what a financial instrument is, the debt/equity line (fixed-for-fixed, contingent settlement, own-share obligations), compound instruments and offsetting. IFRS 7: disclosures about the significance of financial instruments and the credit, liquidity and market risks they create.

1

Definitions and the basic test

Financial instrument: any contract that gives rise to a financial asset of one entity and a financial liability or equity instrument of another.
TermIncludes
Financial assetCash; equity of another entity; a contractual right to receive cash or another financial asset, or to exchange on favourable terms; certain contracts settled in own shares
Financial liabilityA contractual obligation to deliver cash or another financial asset, or to exchange on unfavourable terms; a contract settled in a variable number of own shares
Equity instrumentA residual interest in assets after deducting all liabilities

Not financial instruments: physical assets, prepayments and deferred income (they bring goods or services, not cash), and obligations created by law rather than contract (income tax, constructive obligations).

Liability or equity: the key questionDoes the issuer have an unconditional right to avoid delivering cash? If not → liability. Substance beats legal form: redeemable preference shares (redeemable at 20% premium, 5% coupon) are liabilities and their dividends are finance costs.
Settled in own sharesFixed number of shares for a fixed amount of cash → equity. A variable number equal to a fixed value (shares worth £6,000, or worth 200 ounces of gold) → liability, because the holder bears no equity risk.

The harder debt/equity cases

FeatureClassification
Contingent settlement (pay cash if an index, profit or rate event happens, outside both parties' control)Liability (no unconditional right to avoid), unless the trigger is only liquidation or is not genuine
Dividends only payable if ordinary dividends are paid; non-redeemableEquity (payment is at the issuer's discretion)
Shares the entity must repurchase on a set event (e.g. end of a director's contract)Liability at PV of redemption amount; discretionary dividends go to finance cost when declared
Obligation to buy back own shares (even if conditional on the holder, e.g. a written put)Liability at PV of the redemption amount, taken out of equity
Contingent convertible bonds (CoCos) that convert if the bank's capital ratio fallsAnalyse terms: often a compound or hybrid instrument with liability and equity features
Worked examples: Acquittie, High Growth Bank and Emporium

(1) Non-redeemable preference shares; dividend only if ordinary dividends are paid. (2) Equipment paid for with ordinary shares worth £5m one year after delivery. (3) Directors must buy special £1 shares, repurchased at nominal value when their contract ends. (4) Cumulative 7% preference shares redeemable if bank profit rises by over 50%. (5) Emporium's bonus "rights" can be sold back to the company for fixed cash by 10 November (year end 31 October).

(3) Mandatory repurchaseLiability

Current development

The IASB's Financial Instruments with Characteristics of Equity (FICE) project (exposure draft 2023) proposes clarifications to IAS 32, e.g. on contingent settlement, obligations to buy own shares and shareholder discretion. It is not yet effective; mention it as a current issue only.

Compound instruments, treasury shares and offsetting

Split accounting: value the liability first (PV of interest and capital at the market rate for similar debt without conversion); equity = proceeds − liability. Never remeasured. Issue costs are split in proportion. A bond converting into a variable number of shares equal to its value is wholly a liability.

Interactive question: convertible bond with issue costs

5,000 8% bonds at £10 (proceeds £50,000), convertible after 2 years; market rate 10%; issue costs £1,000.

Equity: 50,000 − 48,240£1,760
Costs split 48,240 : 1,760 → liability £965, equity £35£1,000
Net liability 47,275; new effective rate 11.19%: year 1 interest£5,290
Liability after 2 years£50,000
Treasury sharesDeducted from equity. No gain or loss in profit or loss on buying, selling or cancelling own shares. Disclose the number held.
OffsettingOnly when the entity has a currently enforceable legal right to set off and intends to settle net (or simultaneously). A floating-rate loan and a pay-fixed swap that together create fixed-rate debt are not offset; disclose the link.
4

IFRS 7: significance for position and performance

Objective: help users judge how significant financial instruments are to the entity's position and performance, and the nature and extent of risks and how they are managed. Applies to all entities and all financial instruments (except interests in subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures, employee benefit plans, insurance contracts, share-based payment). Group disclosures into classes suited to their nature; reconcile to the balance sheet.

AreaMain disclosures
CategoriesCarrying amounts of assets at amortised cost, FVOCI (debt and equity) and FVTPL (mandatory and designated); liabilities at amortised cost and FVTPL
Own credit riskLiabilities designated at FVTPL: change in fair value due to the entity's own credit risk (shown in OCI) and how it was measured
Reclassifications and derecognitionBusiness model changes and amounts; transferred assets not derecognised and continuing involvement
CollateralAssets pledged and collateral held that can be sold or repledged
Profit or loss and OCINet gains or losses by category; interest income and expense (effective interest); fee income; impairment losses (expected credit losses)
Hedge accountingRisk management strategy; amount, timing and uncertainty of future cash flows; effect on position and performance
Fair valueFair value of each class for comparison with carrying amount, plus IFRS 13 hierarchy disclosures (Levels 1–3)
5

IFRS 7: nature and extent of risks

Credit risk

A counterparty fails to pay. Disclose maximum exposure, collateral, credit quality, the expected credit loss model and a reconciliation of loss allowances.

Liquidity risk

Difficulty meeting obligations from financial liabilities. Disclose a maturity analysis of undiscounted contractual cash flows and how liquidity is managed.

Market risk

Fair value or cash flows change with market prices: currency, interest rate and other price risk. Disclose a sensitivity analysis for each, with methods and assumptions.

Qualitative: for each risk, the exposures, how they arise, objectives, policies and processes for managing them, measurement methods and changes from last year. Quantitative: summary data based on what is reported to key management, plus the minimum disclosures above and concentrations of risk (by geography, currency, counterparty or industry). Entities may use value-at-risk instead of a simple sensitivity analysis if that is how they manage risk.

Exam focus

Expect the debt/equity question inside a long case: apply the "unconditional right to avoid paying cash" and "fixed for fixed" tests, give the classification and the effect on gearing and covenants (reclassifying preference shares as debt can breach a covenant), then the correcting entry. For audit, link classification to management bias (keeping debt off the balance sheet) and to completeness of IFRS 7 risk disclosures.
