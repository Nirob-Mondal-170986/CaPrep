1

Functional and presentation currency

Functional currencyThe currency of the primary economic environment where the entity operates. Primary indicators: the currency that mainly influences sales prices (and the country whose competition and regulation set them), and the currency that mainly influences labour, material and other costs. Secondary: currency of financing and of retained receipts. For a foreign operation, also: autonomy from the parent, share of activity with the parent, whether its cash flows affect the parent directly, and whether it can service its own debts.
Presentation currencyThe currency the financial statements are shown in; can be any currency. If different from the functional currency, translate using the foreign operation method (topic 3). A change in functional currency is applied prospectively from the date of change, translating all items at that date's rate.
2023 amendment

From 1 January 2025, IAS 21 requires an entity to judge whether a currency is exchangeable into another; if not, it estimates the spot rate that an orderly market would use and discloses the effects. Relevant where currency controls restrict conversion.

Translating a foreign operation

ItemRate
Assets and liabilities (including goodwill and fair value adjustments)Closing rate
Income and expensesRates at transaction dates (average if it approximates)
Share capital and pre-acquisition reservesHistorical rate at acquisition (not retranslated)
Resulting exchange differencesOCI → translation reserve; NCI share to NCI

Why differences arise: (1) opening net assets retranslated from last year's closing rate to this year's; (2) profit translated at average but added to net assets at closing; (3) goodwill retranslated each year.

Worked example: Darius and 100%-owned Xerxes (set up for €100 = £25)

Xerxes: profit after tax €80; net assets €300 at the start and €380 at the end. Rates: €2 = £1 at the start, average €1.60, €1 = £1 at the end.

Profit at average: 80 ÷ 1.6£50
Closing net assets at €1: 380; opening at €2: 150Increase £230
Exchange gain to OCI: 230 − 50£180
Group post-acquisition reserves from Xerxes: 380 − 25£355
Total comprehensive income: 150 (group profit) + 180£330
Context example: goodwill in a foreign currency

ABC buys 80% of DEF for £10m (€24m at €2.40); net assets at fair value €9m; NCI at share.

Goodwill: 24.0 + 1.8 − 9.0 = €16.8m ÷ 2.40£7.0m

4

Consolidation issues

IssueTreatment
Net investment loans (long-term intra-group loans with no settlement planned)Exchange differences go to P/L in the individual accounts, but on consolidation they are moved to OCI (they offset the translation of net assets). If the loan is in a third currency, the difference stays in P/L. E.g. £100,000 loan to a euro subsidiary; € moves from 3.0 to 2.5: subsidiary's €50,000 gain (£20,000) is reclassified to OCI
Intra-group trading balancesMonetary items, so exchange differences stay in group P/L (they reflect real currency exposure). Eliminate balances after retranslation; remove unrealised profit using the rate at the transaction date
Different reporting datesUse the rate at the foreign operation's reporting date, adjusting for significant rate changes up to the group date
DisposalRecycle the cumulative translation reserve (group share) to P/L as part of the gain; partial disposal of a subsidiary without losing control: reattribute to NCI
Negative translation reserveAllowed

Disclosures: exchange differences in P/L (excluding FVTPL instruments); net differences in OCI and a reconciliation of the translation reserve; reasons if the presentation and functional currencies differ, or the functional currency changes. Cash flows: translate foreign currency cash flows at the dates of the flows (or average); show the effect of rate changes on cash as a separate reconciling line.

5

Hyperinflation (IAS 29)

IndicatorsCumulative 3-year inflation approaching or above 100%; people keep wealth in non-monetary assets or a stable foreign currency; prices quoted in foreign currency; credit prices include expected inflation; interest, wages and prices linked to an index. (Venezuela's inflation passed 1,000,000% in 2019.)
RestatementRestate the statements in the measuring unit current at the reporting date using a general price index. Monetary items: not restated. Non-monetary items at cost: × (index now ÷ index at acquisition), capped at recoverable amount. Items already at current value: not restated. Equity and income and expenses: restated from their dates. Gain or loss on the net monetary position → P/L (net monetary assets lose; net monetary liabilities gain).
Example: restating machinery

Bought for $H2,000 when the index was 400; index now 1,000; half its life used.

Cost: 2,000 × 1,000/400$H5,000
Accumulated depreciation: 50%$H2,500

Consolidating a hyperinflationary subsidiary: restate under IAS 29 first, then translate all amounts (including income and expenses and comparatives) at the closing rate. Comparatives in a stable presentation currency are not restated.

6

Audit focus: foreign operations

RisksWrong functional currency; wrong rates or methods (IAS 21 misapplied); hyperinflation not adjusted; weak control over distant subsidiaries; political instability; currency controls blocking dividends; going concern; local tax errors; different local GAAP needing conversion.
ProceduresAgree rates to independent sources (central bank); recompute translation (closing for balance sheet and goodwill, average for income if stable); check equity treatment is consistent; check elimination of intra-group balances and classification of net investment loans; recompute the translation reserve and NCI; check IAS 29 was applied before translation; review disclosures; use component auditors and tax specialists.
Exam focus

FX usually appears inside the consolidation question: translate the subsidiary (closing, average, historical), retranslate goodwill each year, and split the exchange difference between group and NCI in OCI. In single-entity questions, classify each item as monetary or non-monetary before choosing the rate. Explain where each difference goes and why.
