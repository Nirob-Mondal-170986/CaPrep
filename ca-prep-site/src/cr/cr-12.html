PPE, inventories and intangibles (revision)

IAS 16 PPERecognise if benefits are probable and cost is reliable (same test for later spending). Cost = price less discounts + directly attributable costs + estimated dismantling and restoration. Income from incidental operations during construction goes to profit or loss. Exchanges at fair value unless no commercial substance. Cost model or revaluation model (whole class, kept up to date; more often when inflation is high).
IAS 38 intangiblesIdentifiable (separable or contractual/legal) non-monetary asset without substance. Research expensed; development capitalised once all criteria are met (never retrospectively). No internally generated goodwill, brands or customer lists. Revaluation only with an active market. Finite life: amortise from available for use, nil residual (usually). Indefinite life: no amortisation, annual impairment test and life review.
Revaluation movementTreatment
IncreaseTo profit or loss to the extent it reverses a previous decrease charged to profit or loss; the rest to revaluation surplus (OCI)
DecreaseAgainst any revaluation surplus on that asset (OCI) first; the rest to profit or loss
Extra depreciation on revalued amountMay be transferred each year from revaluation surplus to retained earnings (within equity, not through profit or loss)

Impairment (IAS 36)

Impairment loss = carrying amount − recoverable amount; recoverable amount = higher of value in use and fair value less costs of disposal

When to test: whenever there are indicators (external: market value falls, adverse changes in technology, law or markets, higher interest rates, market cap below net assets; internal: damage, obsolescence, restructuring, worse performance). Annually for goodwill, indefinite-life intangibles and intangibles not yet in use. Value in use: pre-tax cash flows from approved budgets (max 5 years unless justified), current condition (no future improvements or restructuring not yet committed), no financing or tax flows, pre-tax market discount rate.

Cash-generating unitsThe smallest group of assets with largely independent cash inflows. A pizza oven or a theme-park monorail is not a CGU; a restaurant branch or a commuter monorail is. An internal network with an external active market for its output is a CGU even if output is used internally.
Allocating a lossRevalued asset: OCI first, then profit or loss. CGU: goodwill first, then other assets pro rata, but not below the highest of each asset's fair value less costs of disposal, value in use, and zero. Then depreciate over the remaining life.

Investment property (IAS 40)

Investment property: land or buildings (owned or a right-of-use asset) held to earn rentals or for capital appreciation, not for own use or for sale in the ordinary course of business.
SituationClassification
Part owner-occupied, part letSplit if the parts could be sold or leased separately; otherwise investment property only if the owner-occupied part is insignificant
Ancillary services to tenantsInvestment property only if the services are insignificant (serviced offices with reception, cleaning and secretaries → IAS 16)
Let to another group companyInvestment property in the owner's own accounts; owner-occupied (IAS 16) in the group accounts
Under construction for future letting; vacant but held to letInvestment property
Held for sale, or built for third partiesIFRS 5, IAS 2 or IFRS 15, not IAS 40
MeasurementInitially at cost including transaction costs (legal fees, transfer taxes). Not included: start-up costs, abnormal waste, losses before full occupancy. Then cost model (IAS 16, with fair value disclosed) or fair value model (IFRS 13, changes in profit or loss, no depreciation) for all investment property.
Transfers on change of useIP → owner-occupied or inventory: fair value becomes deemed cost. Owner-occupied → IP at fair value: revalue under IAS 16 at the change date (gain to OCI). Inventory → IP at fair value: difference to profit or loss. Under the cost model, transfers are at carrying amount.

Replacing parts (cost model): derecognise the old part's carrying amount (use the new cost as a proxy if unknown, e.g. £1.2m × 25/30 = £1m) and capitalise the new part. Under the fair value model, take out the old part's value (lift £100,000) and add the new cost (£600,000): £3m → £3.5m. Damaged property at fair value: write down to fair value (no deduction of selling costs for IAS 40 fair value assets under IFRS 5); recognise insurance compensation only when receivable.

Agriculture (IAS 41) and exploration (IFRS 6)

Biological asset (IAS 41)Agricultural produce at harvest (IAS 41)Product after processing (IAS 2)
Dairy cattle · sheep · trees in a forest · cotton plantsMilk · wool · felled logs · cottonCheese · yarn · timber · clothing
MeasurementBiological assets at fair value less costs to sell at each year end (cost only if fair value is unreliable at first recognition, and never back to cost later). Produce at fair value less costs to sell at harvest, which becomes its IAS 2 cost. All gains and losses (including on initial recognition, e.g. birth of a calf) go to profit or loss. Costs to sell = commissions, levies and transfer taxes; transport to market is deducted in arriving at fair value instead.
Bearer plantsPlants used only to grow produce for many years (tea bushes, rubber trees, oil palms, grape vines) are IAS 16 PPE: cost until mature, then depreciated. Their produce (tea leaves, latex) stays in IAS 41 until harvest. Highly relevant for Bangladesh tea estates.
Worked examples: livestock

(1) Market price £60; transport £1; commission 2%; sales levy 3% (purchase levy 1% is irrelevant to a seller). (2) Herd of 5 at £200 each; 1 bought at £212; year-end value £230 each.

(1) Fair value 60 − 1 = 59; less 1.20 and 1.80£56.00
(2) 1,000 + 212 + change = 6 × 230 = 1,380Gain £168

IFRS 6 (oil, gas and minerals): lets entities keep existing policies for exploration and evaluation spending (after getting legal rights, before feasibility is shown). Measure at cost, then the cost or revaluation model; classify as tangible or intangible consistently. Impairment indicators are modified: rights expired, no further spending planned, no viable finds, or development unlikely to recover the cost. Once feasibility is shown, test for impairment and reclassify (development comes under IAS 16/38).

Insurance contracts (IFRS 17)

Insurance contract: the insurer accepts significant insurance risk from the policyholder by agreeing to compensate them if an uncertain future event (whether, when or how much) adversely affects them.
In scopeLife, funeral, medical, disability, credit, travel insurance; fixed-fee repair services (boiler repair) as payment in kind; cover for claims whose total cost is still unknown.
Not IFRS 17Manufacturer warranties (IFRS 15/IAS 37) · employee benefits · residual value guarantees by a lessee · financial guarantees under IFRS 9 · contingent consideration · policies the entity holds as policyholder. Financial risk alone is not insurance risk.

General measurement model: applied to portfolios split into groups; recognise from the earlier of the start of cover and the first premium due. Initial liability = fulfilment cash flows (probability-weighted, discounted, risk-adjusted) + contractual service margin (CSM), the unearned profit released as service is given. Profitable groups: no day-one profit. Onerous groups: loss in profit or loss immediately, CSM nil. Later: liability for remaining coverage + liability for incurred claims. A simpler premium allocation approach exists for short contracts. Effects: new profit pattern, more volatility, more judgement, big system changes.

Bangladesh point

IFRS 17 is effective internationally from 2023. Bangladesh insurers report under IDRA rules and adoption has been slow. Check the current FRC and IDRA position before saying IFRS 17 applies to a Bangladesh insurer.

Auditing assets and investment property

General: PPE for over- and understatement, valuation and impairment; intangibles for creative capitalisation (overstated assets, understated expenses); inventory for quantity (attend the count) and valuation (cost vs NRV).

Interactive question: Propertyco (fair value model)

A: head office. B: right-of-use property sublet to an outside company. C: bought this year for £3m including legal fees, vacant, tenant sought. D: owned and let to an outside company.

A is owner-occupied, so IAS 16, not fair value through profit or loss. B, C and D are investment property (a right-of-use asset can qualify; vacant property held to let qualifies). C starts at £3m cost, then fair value. Procedures: understand the controls and process for fair values; check the IFRS 13 basis; evaluate external valuers; test DCF inputs against leases and market rates; review supporting documents; check disclosures.

Exam focus

Asset questions mix classification (IAS 16 vs 40 vs IFRS 5 vs IAS 2), measurement and audit. Impairment is the most common: show the recoverable amount calculation, allocate goodwill first, gross up for NCI when it is measured at share of net assets, then give audit procedures on the cash flow forecast and discount rate (Chapter 6, topic 3).
