Questions 4 and 5 and parts of Questions 1 to 3 are written answers: risks and procedures, first-year audits, ethical threats, fraud, group audit, IT and cyber, opinions, and governance. This chapter gives the answer layout, ready lists for each type with the past-paper source, and short outlines for the topics that have not yet been tested.

The three layouts that earn the marks

Question typeLayoutExample heading in the official answer
Risks and proceduresRisk (with the fact from the scenario) → assertion or account affected → likelihood and impact (High, Medium, Low) → specific procedures"Risk of misstatement: Revenue. Impacted accounts: … Likelihood: High. Audit procedures: …" (Nov 2024 GSL)
EthicsIssue (fact) → threat named → principle affected → safeguard or action"Staff member holding shares in NFL. Threat: self-interest. Safeguard: remove from the engagement." (Nov 2025)
Evaluate or adviseConclusion first → reasons (3 to 5 bullets) → action or recommendation"Conclusion on the extent of reliance on internal control" (Nov 2025 Tara)
Three habits

(1) Use the facts: quote the number, the name, the date. Generic lists get few marks. (2) One idea per bullet: a risk, a reason, an action. (3) Match marks to length: 4 marks is about 4 specific points.

Five threatsSelf-interest (fees, shares, loans) · Self-review (auditing your own work) · Advocacy (acting for the client) · Familiarity (long or close relationship) · Intimidation (pressure or threat).

3

First-year (initial) audit

Set in 8 of 13 sessions (NFL, Kingpin, Lifeline, Spring, Miratex, IBL, CETL, Shapla). The question gives a mix of acceptance faults, opening balances, a predecessor's modified opinion and ethics.

Before acceptingClient integrity (owners, PEPs, reputation, reason for change); firm competence and resources; independence; fees and time; communicate with the predecessor (client's permission); valid appointment (Companies Act 1994, Form 23B); engagement letter. Quality-control approval.
Opening balances (ISA 510)Check last year's closing balances were brought forward; read the predecessor's report and working papers (with permission); consistent accounting policies; test balances that matter (inventory count roll-back, receivables confirmations, PPE, loans); consider the effect of a predecessor's modification.
Planning extrasMore time; higher inherent risk (no knowledge of the client); understand processes, systems and controls; professional scepticism over management claims of "stable practices" (NFL); consider key audit matters early.
Predecessor qualified the opinionFind out if the cause is resolved. If the opening balance or comparatives are still misstated and material, modify your opinion on the current period or the comparatives; if resolved, still disclose in comparatives where needed (ISA 710).

5

Fraud (ISA 240)

Fraud trianglePressure (targets, loan covenants, listing, personal debt) · Opportunity (weak controls, management override) · Rationalisation ("temporary", "everyone does it"). Add the scenario fact next to each.
Auditor's dutiesResponsible for reasonable assurance; maintain scepticism; presume fraud risk in revenue and management override (test journals, estimates, unusual transactions); discuss within the team; inquire of management and TCWG; respond with unpredictable procedures.
Controls to suggestSegregation of duties; approvals and limits; vendor master data checks; independent reconciliations; rotation and leave; whistleblowing line to the audit committee; background checks; surveillance; regular surprise counts; tone at the top and code of conduct.
If fraud is suspectedGet more evidence; discuss with the appropriate level of management (not the suspected person); inform TCWG; consider legal and reporting duties against confidentiality; assess integrity and consider withdrawing; do not tip off. Covid-19 (Mar 2022): remote work, weaker controls, new suppliers, fake documents.
6

Group audit (ISA 600)

Responsibilities (Nov 2022 answer)The group auditor expresses the opinion on the consolidated statements; understands the structure and identifies significant components; sends instructions; communicates regularly; reviews working papers; evaluates the consolidation. ISA 600 (Revised) applies to periods from December 2023: the group partner is responsible for all the work.
Before accepting a group engagementUse of component auditors (competence, independence, quality); effect of the structure on risk; components' business and regulatory environment; service organisations; group-wide controls; complexity of consolidation; access to those charged with governance, component management and component auditors' documentation; whether the necessary work can be done.
Group audit instructions (Mar 2024 list)Recent business updates (plans for IPO); procedures being done by the group auditor; materiality for each component; communication timetable; risks identified; independence and ethics confirmations; reporting formats; related parties and intra-group items; subsequent events; how to report misstatements.
Key risks in consolidated statementsMisstatements in component statements (different auditors); different accounting policies (cost vs revaluation); intra-group items and PURP; goodwill and impairment; foreign translation; fair values at acquisition; disposals and gains; consolidation errors.
7

IT, ERP, cyber security, AI and outsourcing

IT general controlsAccess (user IDs, passwords, privileged access, leavers) · change management (testing, approval, segregation of developers and users) · operations (backup, recovery, job scheduling) · security (firewalls, patching). Application controls: input validation, approval workflow, interface checks, automated calculations (depreciation, lease schedules).
New ERP go-live (Nov 2025 Tara)Risks: data migration errors, incorrect mapping, weak access control, no segregation, manual journals from depots, interfaces not reconciled, no parallel run, user training. Conclusion: limited or no reliance on controls; extend substantive testing, use data analytics on all transactions, test migration reconciliations, test access reports.
Cyber security audit (Nov 2022)An independent assessment of the cyber risks an organisation faces and the controls and resources it has (security, planning, information and communication). It aims to identify weaknesses, assess compliance, and improve protection. Auditor's link: risk of misstatement from data breach, ransomware, unauthorised changes, and going-concern effects.
Artificial intelligence in the audit (May 2026)Benefits: full-population testing, pattern detection, faster documentation. Risks: confidential client data entered into public tools, unreliable or "invented" output, bias, lack of explainability, over-reliance, weak documentation. Needs: firm policy, approved tools, human review, professional scepticism, training, consent, data protection.
Service organisation (ISA 402)Understand the services and the controls at the service organisation; obtain a Type 1 or Type 2 assurance report and assess its scope, period and exceptions; consider complementary user-entity controls; if no report, test at the entity or visit. Example: payroll by HR Solutions (Jul 2024).
Expert (ISA 620) and management's specialistEvaluate competence, capabilities, objectivity; agree the scope; assess the work (data, assumptions, methods); consider use of the report in the audit; the auditor stays responsible for the opinion. Property valuer (Fine Vehicles, Mar 2023); actuary (employee benefits).

Audit opinion, KAM and reporting

Nature of the problemMaterial but not pervasiveMaterial and pervasive
Financial statements are misstatedQualified ("except for")Adverse
Unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidenceQualified ("except for possible effects")Disclaimer
Key audit matters (ISA 701)Matters of most significance in the audit, chosen from those discussed with TCWG. Each KAM: what it is, why it was a KAM, how it was addressed. Likely KAMs: revenue, inventory valuation, receivable impairment, goodwill, going concern (only if a material uncertainty, then a separate section), related parties, climate. Not used for disclaimers.
Other paragraphsEmphasis of matter: a matter already disclosed that is fundamental. Other matter: a matter not presented that is relevant. Material uncertainty related to going concern (ISA 570). Other information (ISA 720): read the directors' report and the chairman's statement; if inconsistent with the audited figures (NFL's profit margin stated 14% against about 9% in the statements), ask for correction and, if not corrected, report.
Prior-year qualification (ISA 710)

If last year's qualification is resolved, current opinion is unaffected (but consider comparatives). If unresolved and still material, the current report is modified for the current period figures affected, and the comparatives paragraph explains. In Jul 2023 (SBCL) the official answer adds that once the evidence is obtained the company should record the provision, so check what the prior-year qualification was about.

Governance, internal control, AML and quality

Conclusion on reliance on controlsLayout: Conclusion (reliance: none, limited, full) → Reasons (control environment, IT environment, segregation, monitoring) → Impact on the audit approach (more substantive testing, analytics, larger samples, year-end work). Add improvements: segregation, approvals, access, an internal audit function reporting to an audit committee, whistleblowing.
Audit committee and boardIndependence of directors; audit committee oversight of internal audit and the external auditor; related-party approval; reporting lines (e.g. the CFO answering to the MD only); the BSEC Corporate Governance Code requirements for listed companies; the role of the Head of Internal Audit and Compliance.
Anti-money-laundering programme (Nov 2022)Risk assessment; appoint a money-laundering reporting officer (MLRO); customer due diligence and identification of beneficial owners; enhanced due diligence for PEPs and high-risk clients; staff training; internal reporting and reporting suspicious matters to the BFIU (Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit); record keeping; do not tip off; independent testing of the programme.
Quality management (ISA 220, ISQM 1)Firm-level system: governance and leadership, ethics and independence, acceptance, resources, engagement performance, information, monitoring. Engagement partner is responsible for quality on the engagement; engagement quality review for listed clients; consultation on difficult matters; supervision and review; peer review (Nov 2024).

Climate, ESG and sustainability reporting

Tested in May 2026 (Shapla: IFRS S1 and S2), Mar 2025 (Green Eco Resort) and Jul 2023 (NDL). See Chapter 26 for the full theory.

IFRS S1 and S2: four pillarsGovernance (board oversight, management's role) · Strategy (risks, opportunities, effect on business model and finance, resilience via scenario analysis) · Risk management (how risks are identified, assessed, integrated) · Metrics and targets (greenhouse-gas emissions scope 1, 2 and 3, targets, progress). S2 climate: physical and transition risk.
Audit linksClimate risk can cause impairment, shorter asset lives, new provisions, going concern doubt, and a risk that the climate statements in the directors' report are inconsistent with the financial statements (ISA 720). Procedures: understand the entity's exposure, review forecasts and impairment models for climate assumptions, test provisions, read other information, use an expert where needed. Sustainability assurance: ISAE 3000, ISAE 3410 and ISSA 5000.
11

Review engagements and due diligence (ISRE 2410)

Interim review (Nov 2023 TFL)Limited assurance, conclusion in the negative form ("nothing has come to our attention"). Mainly enquiries and analytical procedures; no tests of controls or detailed substantive testing. Before starting: agree terms in the engagement letter, understand the entity, check the framework (IAS 34). Request from management: management accounts, budgets, bank reconciliations, minutes, schedule of significant changes, legal letters.
Analytical procedures to proposeRevenue by segment (e.g. airline contracts, stores, made-to-measure) against budget and last year; gross margin by line; expenses by nature; receivables and inventory days; cash-flow trend; explanations for each fluctuation; reconcile changes to volume and price.
12

Not yet tested: outlines to learn now

My judgement

These are gaps in the 13 papers. Learn the outline of each (5–6 lines) so you can score even if the question is new.

TopicOutline for a 4 to 6 mark answer
Going concern (ISA 570)Management's assessment covers at least 12 months from the year end. Indicators: losses, negative cash flow, loan defaults, loss of key customer or licence, litigation, supplier credit withdrawn. Procedures: forecasts and assumptions, covenants, bank facilities, post year-end results, support letters, written representations. Outcomes: no uncertainty; material uncertainty (adequate disclosure → unmodified with a separate section; inadequate → qualified or adverse); inappropriate basis → adverse.
Accounting estimates (ISA 540 Revised)Understand how management makes the estimate (method, model, data, assumptions, expert); test methods, data and assumptions; compare with an independent estimate or subsequent events; assess management bias and estimation uncertainty; evaluate disclosures. Examples: ECL, impairment, provisions, defined benefit, fair value.
Laws and regulations (ISA 250, NOCLAR)Auditor obtains understanding of the legal framework; procedures for laws with direct effect (tax, companies law); if non-compliance is suspected: inquire, discuss with management and TCWG, assess the effect on the opinion, consider reporting to authorities under the Code (disclosure override of confidentiality) or withdrawing.
Communication with those charged with governance (ISA 260, 265)Scope and timing, significant findings, independence confirmation, uncorrected misstatements, significant deficiencies in internal control in writing, fraud suspicions, KAMs. Layout of a management letter: finding, risk, recommendation, management response.
Written representations (ISA 580)Confirm management's responsibilities and key assertions (completeness of information, related parties, litigation, subsequent events). They never replace other evidence. If management refuses: reconsider integrity; disclaim if integrity is in doubt.
Subsequent events (ISA 560)Events between year end and the audit report date: adjusting (settlement of a court case, inventory sold at a loss, customer bankruptcy) and non-adjusting. Procedures: review minutes, latest management accounts, enquiries, legal letters. Facts discovered after the report date: discuss, consider amending.
Using internal audit (ISA 610)Evaluate objectivity, competence and systematic approach; decide the extent to use; direct assistance only with safeguards; never take sole reliance for significant risks.
Audit evidence, sampling, confirmations (ISA 500, 530, 505)Sufficient and appropriate evidence; reliability hierarchy (external over internal, direct over inquiry); sampling risk, stratification; confirmation controls and non-response procedures.
Sustainability assurance (ISSA 5000)Acceptance: suitable criteria, competent team and experts, accessible data; limited vs reasonable assurance; procedures for emissions data (meter reads, emission factors, site visits); report wording.
Data analyticsTest entire populations (journals, duplicates, gaps); needs reliable data and testing of the data's completeness; document how exceptions were followed up.
