1

Business strategy and industry analysis

Understand how the business makes money before reading the numbers. Industry analysis (five forces): rivalry, threat of new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power, which set the industry's profit potential. Competitive strategy: cost leadership or differentiation (or focus); the ratios should fit the strategy (a cost leader has low margins and high turnover; a differentiator has high margins and high spending on brand and R&D). Corporate strategy: how a group creates value from its business mix (shared resources, synergies), or destroys it.

2

Accounting analysis and quality

Judge how far the accounts reflect the economics: identify the key accounting policies for the business's success factors (revenue for a contractor, inventory for a retailer, R&D for pharma), the flexibility management has, how aggressive the choices are compared with peers, the quality of disclosure, and red flags (unexplained policy changes, profit far above cash flow, large year-end transactions, growing receivables or inventory, big write-offs, qualified or late audit reports, related party deals).

Sources of distortionRigid rules that ignore substance; estimates and forecasts; management incentives (bonuses, covenants, share price, tax). An audit improves quality but does not guarantee it: the auditor only gives reasonable assurance and judgements remain.
Example: aircraft livesLufthansa depreciated aircraft over 12 years (15% residual); British Airways over 20 years (8% residual). Part of the difference may be real (aircraft type, use, replacement policy), part policy choice (Lufthansa's rates matched tax rules). Analysts adjust only for the policy part.

4

Normalised earnings and cash alternatives

Sustainable operating profitRevenue, cost of sales and operating expenses after standardising policies
Tax on itReported tax adjusted for finance costs, one-offs and deferred tax unlikely to crystallise
Non-recurring itemsDiscontinued operations, disposal gains, impairments, restructuring, redundancy, start-up costs, unusual provisions, fair value and FX gains, after tax

Earnings can be managed, so analysts build sustainable earnings and compare with cash measures (operating cash flow, free cash flow = operating cash − maintenance capex − interest − tax; EBITDA with caution, since it ignores capex and working capital). Under IFRS 18 (from 2027) companies must reconcile their own "management-defined performance measures" (adjusted profit) in the notes, which helps this analysis.

Data analysis: data must be relevant, reliable, timely and complete; check sources (internal, external, structured or not), definitions and sample sizes; look for trends, outliers and correlations, but do not assume causation. Exam scenarios often include data analytics outputs that need interpreting with scepticism.

6

Management commentary

Narrative reporting explains the numbers through management's eyes. IFRS Practice Statement 1 (non-mandatory; the IASB has been updating it, so check the current version) sets out principles: management's view, supplementing and complementing the statements, forward-looking information, and being understandable, relevant, supportable, balanced and comparable. Typical content: nature of the business; objectives and strategy; resources, risks and relationships; results and prospects; KPIs.

Making it compulsory: forBetter information for investors; consistency; forward-looking view; links to sustainability reporting; accountability of directors.
AgainstCost; boilerplate text; commercially sensitive information; liability for forecasts; hard to audit (auditors only read it under ISA 720); may be used for spin.

In Bangladesh, BSEC's Corporate Governance Code requires a directors' report with management's discussion and analysis (results, risks, comparatives, outlook) for listed companies.

7

Audit focus: fraud (ISA 240)

Fraud: an intentional act by management, TCWG, employees or third parties using deception to gain an unjust or illegal advantage. Error is unintentional.
Fraudulent financial reportingFalsifying records or documents; misrepresenting or omitting transactions or disclosures; misapplying accounting rules on purpose; often by management override (fictitious journals, biased estimates). Aggressive earnings management is likely after bad market reactions, with bonus targets, to cut tax, or to meet covenants or funding conditions.
Misappropriation of assetsEmbezzling receipts, stealing inventory or data, paying fictitious suppliers, personal use of assets; often small by staff, larger by management, and hidden by false records.

Responsibilities: management and TCWG must prevent and detect fraud. The auditor must obtain reasonable assurance that the statements are free of material misstatement, including from fraud, keeping scepticism throughout. Fraud triangle: incentive or pressure, opportunity, rationalisation (attitude).

StageRequirements
Risk assessmentTeam discussion; enquiries of management, TCWG and internal audit about fraud risks and known frauds; unusual relationships from analytics; fraud risk factors. Revenue recognition is a presumed fraud risk (rebuttable); management override is always a risk
ResponsesExperienced staff; unpredictability; test journal entries and year-end adjustments; review estimates for bias (look back at last year's); examine the business reason for significant unusual transactions
Evaluation and representationsIs a misstatement a sign of fraud? Reassess management's reliability; written representation that management is responsible for fraud prevention and has disclosed all known or suspected fraud
ReportingTell management promptly; tell TCWG if management or key control staff are involved or the fraud is material; report control deficiencies; consider external duties
WithdrawalIf continuing is in doubt (e.g. management fraud), consider withdrawing where law allows; take legal advice
Interactive question: Sellfones plc (fraud risk factors)

A competitor has failed under margin pressure; 10 new divisional managers with tough targets and big bonuses, while shop staff Christmas bonuses are cut; a new inventory system with problems "fixed" by a chief accountant who never takes leave; a share issue after the year end and a request to sign 6 weeks early; gross margin up from 29% to 33% with a £30m property gain; sale and leasebacks.

Bonus pressure on divisional managers → overstated sales, understated costs, inventoryFraudulent reporting
Disgruntled shop staff (bonus cut) → theft of stock or cashMisappropriation
Chief accountant refusing leave + new system → concealment possibleOpportunity
Share issue and early deadline → pressure to flatter results; less audit timeIncentive
Margin rising in a falling market; property gains → revenue, cut-off, sale and leaseback accountingRed flags
Bangladesh and current issues

Under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2012 and the Money Laundering Prevention Rules 2019, chartered accountants are reporting organisations: report suspicious transactions to BFIU, and do not tip off the client. The IAASB has also been revising ISA 240 to strengthen scepticism, fraud procedures and transparency in the auditor's report; check whether the revised standard is in force for your exam.

Exam focus

FSA 2 questions mix analysis with judgement: adjust the numbers for distortions you can see, explain the effect on key ratios, and say what it means for the user. When the scenario hints at manipulation, identify the fraud risk factors, link each to a financial statement area, and give targeted procedures (journals, estimates, cut-off, unusual transactions). Expectation gap: auditors are not guarantors against fraud, but must design work that could detect material fraud.
