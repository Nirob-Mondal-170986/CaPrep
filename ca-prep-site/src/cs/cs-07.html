1

The whole report: layout

  1. Title block: Draft report to the Board of [Company], from [your firm], date; disclaimer (draft; based on information supplied; not an audit; assumptions in Section X).
  2. Executive Summary (one block per requirement, with numbers and recommendations: Chapter 5).
  3. Assumptions used (short list).
  4. Section 1 (a)(i): adjustments, adjusted financial evaluation, non-financial evaluation, conclusion, recommendation.
  5. Section 2 (a)(ii): SWOT, conclusion, recommendation.
  6. Section 3 (b)(i): NPV of the options, justification, fit with mission and vision, conclusion, recommendation.
  7. Section 4 (b)(ii) and (iii): financing analyses (loan, IPO, other), non-financial factors, conclusion, recommendation.
  8. Section 5 (c)(i): ethical issues; Section 6 (c)(ii): four-pillar sustainability, each with conclusion and recommendation.
  9. Annexures: adjusted statements, mix and ratio tables, NPV workings, share premium workings, loan table.
Same ending in every section

Close each section with Conclusion (3 to 5 bullets with numbers) and Recommendation to the board (3 to 4 actions). This repeats in the suggested answers and covers the C&R and AJ marks.

Requirement (b)(i): NPV of the options and recommendation

  1. Discount rate: CAPM = Rf + β (Rm − Rf). Use the government bond coupon (or the T-bill rate stated). Round and state it.
  2. Cash flows table per option: revenue (with growth), direct expenses, operating expenses, taxable profit, tax at the stated rate (or none if the board says so), capital expenditure, free cash flow, terminal value (g given), total cash flow, discount factor, present value, NPV.
  3. Check and test: assumptions, terminal value share, timing of capital spending, working capital omitted, sensitivity.
  4. Mission and vision: table of the vision elements against each option (fit, partial, no fit) with evidence.
  5. Justify the choice by NPV, fit, risk, capability, stakeholders, ESG; recommend one option with conditions.

Marks lost

(1) No explanation of the discount rate. (2) Forgetting tax when the board says to apply it (or applying it when the board says to ignore it). (3) Wrong timing of the capital outflow. 5

Bank loan viability

  1. Terms: amount, interest rate, grace period, repayment pattern, covenants (for example debt to equity ≤ 4:1; penalty rate if exceeded), security, default penalty, fees (commitment fee), board seats and personal guarantees.
  2. Test the covenant now and after the loan: current debt to equity (Luna 2.34:1) against the limit.
  3. Cash flow table after financing: EBIT, finance expense, taxable profit, tax (note tax relief on losses), capital expenditure, loan drawdowns and repayments, free cash flow, terminal value if used, NPV.
  4. Conclude: viable or not; when the shortfall appears; what the company must do (more equity, longer tenor, a smaller loan).
Result in the Luna case

Loan 1.4 billion at 12.5%, 2-year grace, equal repayment over the last 3 years, interest on the outstanding balance.

Interest year 1: 600m × 12.5%75.0m
Interest years 2 and 3: 1,400m × 12.5%175.0m
Principal repayments years 3 to 5: 1,400m ÷ 3466.7m a year
Free cash flow in year 3 after loan service(482.8)m
NPV of the loan-financed optionnegative (−2.9bn): not viable

Cash from the new plant in years 3 to 5 does not cover repayments of 466.7m a year, so the company would need to fund repayments from the rest of the business or default (penalty 2%). Debt to equity would also rise towards the 4:1 covenant.

Non-financial factors for a loan: loss of flexibility, restrictive covenants, personal guarantees, bank representatives on the board, reporting duties, security over assets, interest rate risk, relationship with existing lenders, effect on credit rating, no dilution of control.

IPO: BSEC share premium and number of shares

Method used in the suggested answers

In the past answers, BSEC is assumed to consider NAV, EPS and sector P/E with equal importance. Estimate a price by each method, take the simple average, and compare with the price proposed by the board. Face value is BDT 10 per share, so premium = price − 10.

MethodCalculationLuna example
NAV-based priceAverage market price of comparables × (company NAV per share ÷ average NAV of comparables)198.8 × 117.90 ÷ 59.1 = 397
EPS-based priceAverage market price × (company EPS ÷ average EPS of comparables)198.8 × 3.73 ÷ 2.34 = 316
Sector P/E priceSector P/E ratio × company EPS46.5 × 3.73 = 173
Acceptable priceAverage of the three(397 + 316 + 173) ÷ 3 = 295; premium 285
Check on the NAV definition

In the Luna and PaperCraft suggested answers (which I checked against the figures), "NAV per share" is calculated from total assets ÷ shares (for Luna: 23,673,877,410 ÷ 200,790,000 = 117.90); the Apon, Astha, Infinity and Bijoy answers use the same "total asset" wording, while Splash used net assets. NAV is normally net assets (total assets − total liabilities) ÷ shares (for Luna the adjusted equity of 5,114m gives about 25.5). Markers follow the key, so state your definition clearly and, if time allows, show the effect of the other definition in one line. The Luna answer also shows EPS as 3.73 in one place and 1.59 in another; use the figure that matches your own adjusted profit.

EPS and dilution (PaperCraft style)Basic EPS = profit ÷ shares (before); after the issue use the new share count (weighted if issued part-way through the year) and add the profit from the new investment if asked. Dilution = fall in EPS and in ownership for existing holders; compare the funding need with what the IPO raises.
Non-financial factors for an IPOListing costs and timeline; disclosure and governance duties; independent directors; BSEC approval risk; market conditions and demand; dilution of control; family ownership; valuation expectations; reputation; quarterly reporting pressure.

Share exchange ratio (from the Biponi answer)

Book value: Biponi NAV per share 18.68; issue 1,346.4m shares at ratio 1:0.91 for Digipay's 1,225m shares.

Discounted cash flow: values per share Digipay 61, Biponi 1,035. Shares to issue = 1,225m × 61 ÷ 1,035 = 72m; ratio 1:17. The DCF ratio issues fewer shares, so it is better for Biponi's shareholders, but it depends on forecasts (growth 5% and 7%, discount rates 13% and 14%), so the risk is higher.

Sale and leaseback (worked pattern)

Sell land for 150m; lease back at 20m a year for 10 years; cost of funds 12%.

PV of rentals = 20m × 5.650 = 113m. Net benefit = 150 − 113 = 37m (before tax). Compare with a loan of 150m at 12%, check the land's expected price growth and lease renewal, and show the funding gap that remains.
