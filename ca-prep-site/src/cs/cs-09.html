Four-pillar sustainability checklist

HumanTraining and skills; health and safety policy and accident record; fair pay and conditions; retention, succession and culture; diversity; employee voice; product safety for users; leadership capacity for growth. Gap to look for: no evidence on retention, succession or satisfaction.
SocialBenefit to households and communities; employment and local suppliers; affordability and access; consumer protection; supply chain labour standards; CSR and donations; relations with regulators; public health. Gap: weak monitoring of suppliers; no community engagement.
EconomicProfitability and cash flow; dependence on imports or a few customers; contribution to tax, jobs, exports and local value-added; investment and growth; resilience to currency and duty changes; fair dealing with suppliers. Gap: thin liquidity, high gearing, one-product risk.
EnvironmentalEnergy and water use; emissions; waste, recycling and take-back; effluent and pollution; clean-up liabilities; raw materials; climate and flood risk; environmental certificates (such as ISO 14001); compliance. Gap: no environmental policy or measurement.

Method: for each pillar list 3 or 4 points from the exhibits (strength then gap), show how the new investment affects it, give a conclusion (strong, adequate, weak) and a recommendation (policy, target, reporting). Remember that investors, lenders and a prospectus need credible sustainability information, and IFRS S1 and S2 style reporting is the direction of travel.

Exam focus

Build a one-page sheet for 8 sectors with 5 ethical issues and 3 points per pillar. In the exam, take the sector's facts from Exhibits 7 and 8 first and add this knowledge second. Finish every part with a conclusion and a recommendation that the board can act on.
