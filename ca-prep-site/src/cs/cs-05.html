The Executive Summary

1

What it is and why it matters

The Executive Summary (ES) is a short, stand-alone précis of the report for busy board members, many of whom may read only this part. Leaving it out is a serious mistake. The Marking Key has six mark-earning areas in the ES, two for each of the three requirements, plus the overall criteria: report structure (from the firm, disclaimer, headings) and report language (formal, tactful, ethical).

  • Do not add new ideas. The ES must only summarise what is in the main report.
  • No separate "conclusion" heading. The whole ES is a conclusion.
  • Credit is given for key issues, key numbers and results, judgements, conclusions and recommendations under each requirement, and for an "appropriate summary of the report section" (not a copy of paragraphs).
2

What a good Executive Summary contains

ElementContent
Key issuesThe main issues under each requirement, in priority order with a reason for the order
Financial and strategic analysisThe key numbers: adjusted profit, adjusted ratios, NPV of each option, share price and premium, number of shares, loan viability. Stated in non-technical language
JudgementsThe assumptions and the confidence in them ("positive NPV, but dependent on 4% long-term growth")
ConclusionsUnambiguous and supported by the points above
RecommendationsWhat the board should do, with next steps
Length and formAbout 10–15% of the report. Use short headed paragraphs or bullets, one block per requirement (a), (b), (c), in the same order as the report. Bold the decisions.
Good against poorGood: main points under each heading with figures and a recommendation. Poor: an index of the report ("in section 2 we discuss..."); no figures; no priorities; copied text; leaves out ethics.
3

Skeleton you can reuse

Executive Summary layout (fill with the case facts)

One short block per requirement, each with issue, number, judgement and action.

  1. Purpose and basis. One line: advice to the board on [company] based on draft statements and exhibits; draft; assumptions stated in Section X.
  2. (a) Performance and position. Draft profit adjusted from [X] to [Y] after [number] issues (main ones); liquidity weak (current ratio [..]); gearing [..]; cash conversion [..]; SWOT headline: strongest strength, biggest weakness, key opportunity and threat; overall view: [adequate or weak] and why.
  3. (b) Options and financing. NPV of option 1 [..] and option 2 [..] at [CAPM]%; recommended option [..] because NPV and fit with mission; funding: bank loan [viable or not, why], IPO at maximum acceptable BSEC price [..] with [..] new shares and [..]% ownership after issue; recommended funding and conditions.
  4. (c) Ethics and sustainability. Main ethical issues (financial reporting, safety, compliance, governance) and the actions; sustainability across the four pillars with the weakest pillar and the improvement needed.
  5. Next steps. What the board should approve, what further information is needed, and the timetable.
Common mistakes

(1) Not writing it. (2) Writing it first, then running out of time to finish the report. (3) No numbers. (4) Introducing a new recommendation that is not in the body. (5) Copying paragraphs. (6) Leaving out requirement (c). (7) Not saying what to do.
