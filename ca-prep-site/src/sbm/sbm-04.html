Performance management and budgeting

Performance management sets objectives, measures results, compares with targets, finds the causes of differences and takes action: a feedback loop (single-loop corrects actions; double-loop questions the objectives themselves). It covers strategic, tactical and operational levels.

Traditional budgetingBeyond Budgeting
PlanningAnnual fixed plan, top-down targetsRolling forecasts, relative targets (against peers and last period)
ControlVariance against budgetContinuous review, trust and empowerment
RewardsBonus for meeting budget (can cause gaming)Team and relative performance rewards
ResourcesAnnual allocation, "use it or lose it"Resources released as needed

Criticisms of traditional budgets: time-consuming, quickly out of date, encourage padding (budgetary slack) and short-term thinking, discourage change, focus on cost not value, linked to bonuses so people game targets.

Information for strategic decisions

LevelDecisionsInformation
StrategicLong term, directionExternal, forward-looking, summarised, uncertain
TacticalMedium term, resource useMix of internal and external, periodic
OperationalDay to dayInternal, detailed, accurate, frequent
SystemsExecutive information systems (summaries and drill-down for top managers), management information systems (regular reports), decision support systems (what-if models). Strategic information systems give advantage by changing products or processes.
Challenge for accountantsManagement accounting information must be externally oriented (market, competitors) and future oriented (forecasts), as well as internal and historical. Data analytics supports this (Chapter 8).

Performance measurement

First decide what to measure: link to critical success factors (CSFs: the few things the organisation must do well) and to objectives. Good measures are relevant, measurable, timely, controllable and understood.

Financial measureFormula (typical)
Profit marginProfit ÷ revenue
Return on capital employed (ROCE)Profit before interest and tax ÷ (equity + long-term debt)
Asset turnoverRevenue ÷ capital employed
Residual incomeProfit − (capital × required return)
Economic value addedNOPAT − (capital × WACC)
Cash flow measuresOperating cash flow, free cash flow
Leading and lagging indicatorsLagging measures results already achieved (profit, market share). Leading measures drivers of future results (customer satisfaction, order book, training hours). Use both.
Non-financial measuresAdvantages: forward-looking, closer to operations, hard to manipulate by accounting. Disadvantages: many measures cause overload, may conflict, harder to compare, may not link to profit. The performance measurement manifesto asks for measures linked to strategy, balanced and few.

Value for money (VFM) in the public and not-for-profit sector: economy (inputs at lowest cost for quality), efficiency (output per unit of input), effectiveness (objectives achieved).

The balanced scorecard (Kaplan and Norton)

Four perspectives, with objectives, measures, targets and actions for each.

PerspectiveQuestionExample indicators
FinancialHow do we look to shareholders?Revenue growth, ROCE, cash flow, cost per unit
CustomerHow do customers see us?Satisfaction, retention, market share, complaints, delivery time
Internal business processWhat must we excel at?Cycle time, defect rate, productivity, on-time delivery
Learning and growthCan we keep improving?Training, staff turnover, innovation rate, IT capability

The perspectives are linked (better learning gives better processes, which satisfy customers, which raises profit). A strategy map shows these cause-and-effect links. Problems: conflicting measures, too many indicators, no clear weighting, hard to quantify, needs top commitment, can become a reporting exercise.

Assurance and performance indicators: an accountant can give assurance on the accuracy and fairness of reported KPIs, which makes them more credible to stakeholders.

4

Rewards, behaviour and performance

Executive pay should attract and keep good people, link pay to long-term performance and be set by an independent remuneration committee. A package mixes several parts.

ElementPurpose and risk
Basic salaryFixed income; not linked to performance
Performance-related bonusRewards meeting targets; risk of short-termism and manipulation of results
Shares and share optionsAlign with shareholders; options can reward risk-taking; "underwater" options lose motivation. Expense under IFRS 2 (grant-date fair value over the vesting period)
Benefits in kindCar, housing, insurance; visibility and tax
PensionLong-term reward; defined benefit creates large liabilities and affects strategic decisions (IAS 19)
Behaviour and targetsPeople focus on what is measured. Targets motivate if they are achievable, accepted and controllable. Poor design causes gaming (delaying costs, cutting maintenance), narrow focus and conflict. Judge managers only on what they can control (responsibility accounting).
Pay at all levelsPerformance-related pay can raise effort but may reduce teamwork and encourage short-term action. Fairness, clear measures and a link to strategy matter.

CSR, ESG and integrated reporting

Organisations measure CSR and ESG performance with quantitative indicators (emissions, energy, water, waste, accidents, staff turnover, diversity) and qualitative ones (policies, stakeholder views), with targets. Some disclosures are mandatory (for example emissions and employee information in many countries' strategic reports) and others voluntary.

Integrated reporting (IR)Explains how an organisation creates value over time using six capitals: financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, natural, social and relationship. Benefits: joined-up thinking, long-term focus, better investor information. Costs: new data and judgement.
Natural capitalInclude the cost and value of natural resources (water, forests, biodiversity) in decisions: dependency and impact, risk and opportunity.
FrameworkUse
UN Sustainable Development Goals (17)Global goals that organisations link their activities and targets to; SDG Compass guides business
TCFD and IFRS S2Climate risk disclosure: governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets
IFRS S1General sustainability-related disclosures
Integrated reporting frameworkValue creation story across capitals
Assurance

Assurance over ESG data (limited or reasonable) adds credibility and reduces greenwashing. The accountant must understand the criteria, data systems and controls. Bangladesh: the Bangladesh Bank sustainable finance rules and the DSE reporting guidance are relevant local context.

Exam focus

Common requirements: evaluate performance using financial and non-financial measures from the data; design a balanced scorecard with indicators; criticise a budget system; advise on pay and its behavioural effects; discuss ESG measures and assurance.
