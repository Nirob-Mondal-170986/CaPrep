Financial strategy decisions

DecisionQuestionKey points
InvestmentWhere to invest?Which projects, acquisitions, capacity; appraisal (Chapter 17); capital rationing; fit with strategy
FinancingHow to pay for it?Equity or debt; gearing; cost of capital; maturity; covenants; control and risk (Chapter 14)
DividendHow much to return?Pay out surplus; signals; shareholders' tax and income needs; need for retained funds; legal limits

Factors affecting the choice of strategy: corporate objectives, stage of the life cycle (growth firms need funds and pay less; mature firms generate cash), risk appetite, access to markets, cost of finance, tax, regulation, owners' wishes and the economy.

Strategic cash flow planningForecast cash for the long term, not just the year: investment needs, debt repayments, dividends, cyclical swings. Plan funding sources and reserves in advance; test scenarios.
Strategic fund managementManage surplus and short-term funds (investment of cash), debt maturity profile, banking relationships and liquidity buffers (Chapter 18).

Finance as business partner: finance people help line managers with decisions: modelling options, challenging assumptions, measuring performance, managing risk and communicating with investors. This needs commercial awareness, communication, and data skills.

Corporate reporting implications of investment and financing

DecisionReporting standard and effect
Buying shares in another entityControl: consolidate (IFRS 10). Significant influence: equity method (IAS 28). Joint arrangement: IFRS 11. Less: financial asset (IFRS 9)
Acquiring a businessIFRS 3: fair values, goodwill, acquisition costs expensed
Buying assetsIAS 16 (PPE), IAS 38 (intangibles), IAS 40 (investment property), IFRS 16 (leases), impairment (IAS 36)
Issuing debt or equityIAS 32 classification (liability or equity), IFRS 9 measurement, effective interest, covenants and disclosure (IFRS 7)
Dividends and share buybacksDistributions reduce equity; buybacks reduce shares and EPS denominator

Financing choices change gearing, interest cover and EPS and may affect covenants and credit rating. Choice of structure (lease or buy, debt or equity) changes reported ratios, so analysts adjust for them.

Financial crises, inflation and interest rates

Global financial crisis (2007–09)Collapse of credit markets after excessive lending and complex products. Effects: tight credit, falling demand, bank failures and rescues, stricter regulation, lower confidence, impairment and going-concern concerns for businesses.
Inflation and rising rates (2021–2023)Higher input and wage costs, margin pressure, higher borrowing cost for floating rate debt, lower asset values, higher discount rates (lower valuations), pressure on cash flow and covenant headroom.

Business responses: keep liquidity, review gearing and fixed-rate cover, hedge, pass on prices, cut cost, diversify funding, stress-test plans. Eurozone, Brexit: currency union and exit changed trade, regulation and currency risk for firms dealing with Europe; they are examples of political risk and uncertainty.

Other current issues in finance

Access to creditSmall firms often cannot borrow on good terms after banks tighten lending. Alternatives: asset finance, invoice finance, supply-chain finance, peer-to-peer lending, crowdfunding, private equity, government schemes.
Dark pool tradingPrivate trading venues where large orders are matched without showing prices first. Benefits: less market impact for big investors. Concerns: lack of transparency, fairness, price discovery.
Islamic finance principleMeaning and products
No interest (riba), no excessive uncertainty (gharar), no gambling, only permitted (halal) activitiesReturns must come from real trade, ownership and risk sharing
MurabahaBank buys the asset and sells to the customer at a disclosed mark-up, payable later
IjaraLeasing: the bank owns the asset and leases it, often with a transfer option
Mudaraba and MusharakaProfit sharing partnerships (one provides capital and the other skill, or both provide capital)
SukukAsset-backed certificates that pay a share of asset income instead of interest (Islamic bonds)
Bangladesh note

Bangladesh has several Islamic banks and Islamic windows, so the principles above are relevant to local scenarios. Accounting follows IFRS; substance over form decides classification.

5

Social responsibility, sustainability and green finance

  • Corporate responsibility and citizenship: responsibility to society beyond profit; "corporate citizen" with rights and duties.
  • Sustainability: long-term thinking about environmental and social impact. Costs: compliance, change; benefits: efficiency, reputation, access to finance.
  • Green finance: finance for environmental benefit. Green bonds raise funds for green projects, with reporting on use of proceeds; sustainability-linked loans and bonds change the interest rate according to sustainability targets. Risk: greenwashing.
  • Control systems and management accounting: collect environmental data (energy, waste, water, carbon), environmental management accounting, life-cycle costing.
  • Reporting: environmental and social reporting, integrated reporting, TCFD and ISSB standards. Assurance over environmental information (limited or reasonable) supports credibility.
Exam focus

Typical tasks: advise the board on financing a strategy (debt vs equity, dividend), explain reporting effects of an investment, comment on the effects of inflation or a crisis, explain Islamic or green finance products, or discuss the finance function's role.
