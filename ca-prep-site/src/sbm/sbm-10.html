1

Strategic HRM and the employer of choice

Human resource management (HRM) acquires, develops, motivates and keeps the people the strategy needs. Strategic HRM links people policies to business objectives, instead of acting only as personnel administration.

Roles of HRMWorkforce planning; recruitment and selection; training and development; performance management; reward; employee relations; health and safety; legal compliance; culture and change support; diversity and wellbeing.
Employer of choiceA reputation that attracts and keeps talented people: fair pay and benefits, development, purpose and values, flexibility, safe and inclusive culture, career paths, good leadership. Benefits: lower recruitment and turnover cost, higher productivity and innovation.

3

Appraisal and performance management

Purposes of appraisal: assess performance and potential, agree objectives, find training needs, decide pay and promotion, motivate, and plan succession. For it to work: clear objectives linked to strategy, fair and consistent measures, regular feedback (not once a year), and trained appraisers.

HR and performance managementSet targets that cascade from corporate objectives; coach and review during the year; reward results and behaviour; deal with under-performance. Use balanced measures: results, skills and values.
Target setting issuesTargets that are too hard demotivate; too easy waste effort. Make them specific, agreed, controllable and linked to critical success factors. Beware of gaming and of ignoring quality, ethics and teamwork.
4

Remuneration and reward

A reward management model covers the total reward: pay, bonus, benefits, recognition, development and work conditions. Set reward levels with reference to the market, the job's value, performance, affordability and fairness.

Benefits of linking reward to performanceMotivation, focus on objectives, attract high performers, share success. Aligns staff with shareholders when linked to long-term value.
Adverse consequencesShort-term focus, gaming or fraud, unfairness, lower teamwork, stress, high cost in good years, demotivation when targets are missed.
Accounting linkKey rule
IAS 19 defined benefit plansLiability = present value of obligation − fair value of plan assets; service cost and net interest in profit or loss; remeasurements in OCI (Corporate Reporting Chapter 32)
IAS 19 defined contribution plansExpense = contribution payable for the period
IFRS 2 share-based paymentEquity-settled: grant-date fair value over the vesting period. Cash-settled: liability remeasured to fair value each year
Remuneration reportExecutive pay policy and amounts disclosed; shareholders vote (advisory or binding); links to listing rules and governance codes
5

HRM, change management, acquisitions and ESG

  • Change agents: HR supports leaders by planning, communicating, training and managing resistance. Models of change (Lewin, Kotter: Chapter 3) guide the process.
  • Internal consistency: HR policies (selection, training, reward, appraisal) must support each other and the new strategy: do not reward individual targets if teamwork is needed.
  • Leadership and change: visible, consistent leadership gives credibility. Involve people, listen, and show quick wins.
  • Acquisitions: HR due diligence (liabilities such as pensions, key staff, culture), retain talent, harmonise pay and conditions, manage redundancies and communication. Many deals fail because of poor people integration.
  • HR and ESG: diversity and inclusion, health and safety, human rights in the supply chain, ethical culture, employee volunteering, links of pay to ESG targets, and workforce data for ESG reporting.
Exam focus

Typical tasks: advise on HR implications of a chosen strategy; design a reward scheme and discuss its behavioural effects; evaluate flexible working; manage people in a change or acquisition; comment on pay and accounting. Link every point to the scenario's strategy.
