Strategy models applied to a case

RequirementLayout that scores
Five forcesTable: force → strong or weak → evidence from the case → effect on profit. Finish with an overall industry attractiveness verdict and one strategic action (Chapter 1). Used for Liza Pharma and the SaaS company.
PESTEL or PESTOne line per factor with a case fact, marked opportunity (O) or threat (T), then pick the top 2 to 3 factors with the biggest effect and say why. Used for Fit Footwear, the land deal, bottled water.
SWOTFour short lists, each point with a case fact, ranked. End with 2 to 3 strategies that match (use strength to seize opportunity; fix weakness that exposes threat).
Strategic options (alternative scenarios)Table: option → description → pros → cons → risk → verdict, then a recommendation using suitability, acceptability and feasibility (Chapter 2).
Generic strategy, business-level strategyName the strategy (cost leadership, differentiation, focus, hybrid, blue ocean), show evidence from the case (price, features, target market), then the functional actions that support it (marketing, operations, HR, IT).
Competitive advantage of a firm or nationList the sources: efficiency, quality, responsiveness to customers, innovation (the four building blocks) or for a country Porter's diamond: factor conditions, demand conditions, related and supporting industries, firm strategy and rivalry (plus government and chance). Link each to case facts.
Growth through takeover or diversificationDefine the type: horizontal (same stage, same industry), vertical backward or forward (supplier or customer), conglomerate (unrelated). Then advantages (scale, market power, security of supply, risk spreading) and disadvantages (cost, integration, culture, regulation, lack of expertise).
2

Change, leadership, governance and risk answers

Managing change (force-field and process)Forces for (new technology, competitors, customers, regulation, poor performance) against forces against (fear, skills, cost, culture, habit). Plan: reduce restraining forces first. Then Lewin (unfreeze, change, refreeze) or Kotter's eight steps, with specific actions for the manager in the case.
Leadership theories evaluatedFor each relevant theory: idea → application to the situation → strength or limit. Then recommend a style and actions on engagement and motivation (listen, involve, clear goals, recognition, development).
Governance critiqueTable: weakness in the case → principle breached → recommendation (independent directors, split of chair and CEO, committees, evaluation, whistleblowing, disclosure). For a scandal (Volkswagen): culture and pressure, weak oversight, no speaking-up, auditor and regulator failure, consequences and lessons.
Risk questionsRisk register table: risk → cause → likelihood → impact → response (transfer, avoid, reduce, accept) → owner. For "risk culture": tone at the top, clear appetite, training, reporting without blame, rewards that include risk, monitoring.
Business risk (fitness centres example)Linked financial statement risk
Members leave; price competitionRevenue decline; impairment of assets; going concern if cash falls
Membership fees paid in advanceRevenue recognised too early (IFRS 15 contract liabilities)
Heavy equipment and pools; safety accidentsImpairment or wrong useful lives; provisions for claims; insurance recoveries
Loans and leases to open centresCovenant breach (debt becomes current), lease liability measurement
Energy price risesCost growth; margin pressure; onerous contract risk

For the question "link each business risk to a financial statement risk": name the statement line, the assertion (existence, completeness, valuation, classification, cut-off) and what could be misstated.

Merger, takeover price and valuation

EPS effect of an acquisition at a premium

Acquirer A: 100 million shares, earnings 500 million (EPS 5), share price 75 (P/E 15). Target T: 40 million shares, price 40, earnings 100 million. A offers a 35% premium and pays in new A shares.

Offer price per T share 40 × 1.3554
Cost of the deal 54 × 40m2,160m
New A shares 2,160 ÷ 7528.8m
Combined earnings 500 + 100600m
New EPS 600 ÷ 128.8m shares4.66 (down from 5.00)
Earnings needed to keep EPS at 5: 5 × 128.8644m
Synergy needed (after tax)44m a year

The premium and the lower P/E of the target (40 ÷ 2.5 = 16 before premium, 21.6 after) make the deal dilute EPS. The board must show real synergies exceed the premium. If paying in cash, compare the after-tax finance cost with the earnings acquired.

QuestionAnswer outline
Why pay a premium?Control and synergies are worth more than the market price of a minority stake; to win against other bidders; shareholders will not sell at market price; compensation for lost independence
Offer price range (OHP and ABC, Paper 10)Maximum OHP can pay = market value of ABC (100,000 shares × 30 = 30.0m) + synergy gains 10.0m + saving of overpayment 3.2m = 43.2m, so Tk 43.20 a share. Minimum ABC's management will accept for its 50% holding = market value of its holding 15.0m + PV of lost remuneration 3.2m = 18.2m ÷ 500,000 shares = Tk 36.40. Negotiate within 36.40 to 43.20.
Do the target's shareholders gain from a share swap? (Power and Summit, Paper 11)Power's value before = earnings 5.8m × P/E 10 = 58m. Combined earnings (10m + 5.8m) × 1.08 (8% synergy) = 17.06m; at P/E 9 value 153.54m. Power holders get 30m of 70m shares: 153.54m × 30/70 = 65.8m, so 7.8m better off. Also say increasing EPS alone is not a valid reason to merge.
Forward price and arbitrage (cost of carry, Paper 10)Forward = spot + carrying cost. Spot 2,000, carrying cost 9% a year: 6 months 4.5% gives 2,090. If the 3-month futures price (2,250) is above spot + 2.25% carry (2,045): buy spot, sell futures and lock in 205 a share risk free.
Why is the CEO eager?Growth, size and status, pay linked to size, empire building, defence against being taken over, pressure from the market (agency problem)
Maximum offer priceValue to the acquirer = target's standalone value + PV of synergies − costs of integration. Maximum price per share = that value (less net debt) ÷ number of shares. Pay less than this so some value stays with your own shareholders (Chapter 12)
Reliability of synergy estimatesSynergies are forecasts: check evidence, timing, one-off costs, customer loss after merger, staff loss, culture clash, regulatory limits; usually cost synergies are more certain than revenue synergies
Hostile takeover and sponsorsBid made directly to shareholders against the board's will; defences: persuade shareholders, find a white knight, poison pill; sponsors may refuse to transfer shares to protect control or because of price disputes or lock-in rules
Why alliances failDifferent goals, mistrust, culture clash, unequal contributions, weak governance, loss of know-how to the partner, unclear exit

Foreign exchange: exposure, hedging, netting and swaps

1
Identify the exposure: receipt or payment, currency, date, amount 2
Name the risk type (transaction, translation, economic) 3
Calculate each hedge in the home currency 4
Compare and recommend 5
Add non-calculation points: cost, flexibility, counterparty, policy

Quotes: a bank buys the currency at the lower rate and sells at the higher rate. For a receipt of foreign currency you sell it (bank buys: lower rate). For a payment you buy it (bank sells: higher rate). Always state which rate you used. Check the direction of the quote: Paper 3 quoted rates as US dollars per taka (for example $0.008854 = Tk 1), so taka received = dollars ÷ that rate (USD 56 million ÷ 0.008754 = Tk 6,397 million with a forward of 0.008754). Invert if you prefer taka per dollar, but be consistent.

Option on a USD receipt

Exporter will receive USD 1m. Put option to sell USD at 110 costs 1.2 per USD. Forward rate 111.5.

If spot at expiry is 105: exercise at 110 − premium 1.2108.8 per USD
If spot at expiry is 118: let lapse, sell at 118 − 1.2116.8 per USD
Forward (fixed whatever happens)111.5 per USD

The option sets a floor of 108.8 and keeps the upside. The forward gives certainty at 111.5. Compare the results at the rates in the question and say which is better at each rate. Premium is paid at the start (consider its time value).

Multilateral netting (Tasnia Global pattern)

Intercompany balances (USD million): A owes B 5 and C 3; B owes C 4 and A 1; C owes A 2 and B 1.

A: pays 5 + 3 = 8; receives 1 + 2 = 3net pays 5
B: pays 4 + 1 = 5; receives 5 + 1 = 6net receives 1
C: pays 2 + 1 = 3; receives 3 + 4 = 7net receives 4
Check: −5 + 1 + 40

Gross transfers were 16; after netting A pays 5 (1 to B, 4 to C). Benefits: lower bank charges and conversion costs, less exposure, fewer transfers, quicker settlement. Needs a central treasury, consistent currency and local law permitting it.

Official method (Paper 1): convert every balance into BDT first (USD at 112, CNY at 15.5, AED at 30.5), then net each subsidiary: China net payable 1.64m, Indonesia 0.33m, UAE 12.92m, Qatar net receivable 14.89m (total payables equal total receivables). Bangladesh point: netting of export and import payments generally needs Bangladesh Bank approval; payments abroad also carry tax and VAT deducted at source, so deduct and deposit these first, then net the rest, or apply for special approval.

HedgeLayout of the calculation
ForwardAmount × forward rate (use the correct side of the quote)
Money market (receipt)Borrow PV = amount ÷ (1 + foreign borrowing rate for the period) → convert at spot → deposit at the home rate: result × (1 + home deposit rate). For a payment: buy PV of the amount at spot and deposit abroad, financed by home borrowing
FuturesNumber of contracts = exposure ÷ contract size; profit or loss on closing out; basis difference; compare with the spot cost
OptionShow outcome in each of the spot scenarios given, net of premium; choose the better of exercise or lapse
Currency swapExchange of principal and interest. Official layout (Paper 13): notional USD 20m at Tk 96.5 = Tk 1,930m; dollar rate 12%, taka rate 15%. Each interest date: pay the lender USD 2.4m, receive USD 2.4m from the counterparty, pay the counterparty Tk 289.5m (15% × 1,930m). At maturity: pay lender USD 20m, receive USD 20m, pay Tk 1,930m. Result: the company has swapped a dollar loan into a fixed taka loan and removed currency risk. Also note counterparty credit risk
Forward price (arbitrage)Forward = Spot × (1 + cost of carry for the period) for commodities (add storage, subtract convenience yield) or Spot × (1 + ihome) ÷ (1 + iforeign) for currency. If the market forward differs, buy the cheap and sell the dear and lock in a risk-free profit (arbitrage)

Direct risk reduction methods (no derivative needed): invoice in your own currency, match foreign income and costs, borrow in the foreign currency, leading and lagging, netting, price adjustment clauses, diversify suppliers and markets, hold foreign currency accounts.

Financing, dividends, sukuk, lease or buy

Homemade dividend (Mr. Hasan pattern)

An investor holds 1,000 shares; dividend 2 per share; wants income of 5,000; ex-dividend price 248.

Dividend received 1,000 × 22,000
Shortfall 5,000 − 2,0003,000
Shares to sell 3,000 ÷ 24812.1, so about 12 shares

If the investor wanted less income than the dividend, he would reinvest the surplus in shares. In a perfect market the investor can create any income pattern, so dividend policy does not change his wealth (Modigliani and Miller). Official answer (Paper 5): wanted BDT 4,000 at a price of BDT 400, so sell 4,000 ÷ 400 = 10 shares. Tax and costs break this in practice.

Sukuk price

Sukuk of 100 face value, 5 years, expected profit payments 7% a year, investors require 8%.

Profit payments 7 × 5-year annuity factor at 8% (3.9927)27.95
Principal 100 × 0.680668.06
Price per 10096.01

Price below par because the profit rate is below the required return. Attractive features: asset-backed, no interest (riba), risk sharing, ethical screening, diversification for Islamic investors. Accounting depends on the structure; most are financial liabilities at amortised cost with profit payments as finance cost (substance over form). Official variant (Paper 5, ijara sukuk): price = PV of the annual lease payments only, e.g. 160 × 5-year annuity factor at 5% (4.33) = 692.8. The dealer that buys the sukuk records them at cost if there is no market price at year end.

QuestionAnswer outline
Financing mix memo (equity or debt)To, from, subject. Project cash flow stability and size; existing gearing and interest cover; cost of each source; control and dilution; tax relief; flexibility and covenants; market conditions; recommendation with a target gearing range and a timetable (Chapter 14).
Why not a source of financeGive one reason (cost, dilution, repayment pressure, security, control) and two other factors (gearing, availability, flexibility, tax).
Musharaka against MudarabaMusharaka: both bank and customer contribute capital; profit is shared as agreed and loss in proportion to capital (the bank can take part in decisions). Mudaraba: bank gives all capital, entrepreneur gives skill, profit shared, loss borne by the bank unless negligence. The bank may prefer Musharaka for a share in decisions and lower moral hazard.
IPO risksPricing (under or over), failure to sell, market conditions, cost, disclosure and governance burden, loss of control, short-term pressure. Reduce with an underwriter, timing, roadshow, advisers, compliance preparation.
Thematic bonds (orange against blue)Green (environment), blue, orange, social, sustainability-linked. Features: use of proceeds, reporting, external review. Risk: greenwashing; use independent verification and audit. Official comparison (Nov–Dec 2025): an orange bond funds women's empowerment (women-owned SMEs, jobs, financial inclusion; SDG 5 and SDG 8; social focus; beneficiaries women and girls); a blue bond funds protection and sustainable use of oceans and water (marine biodiversity, coastal resilience, sustainable fisheries; SDG 14 and SDG 13; environmental focus; coastal communities and ecosystems).
Lease or buy (bottled water pattern)

Buy: outlay 1,000, resale 100 after 4 years. Lease: 300 a year for 4 years (ignore tax). Discount rate 10%.

PV of lease 300 × 3.1699951.0
PV of buying: 1,000 − 100 × 0.6830931.7
Buying is cheaper by19.3

Official pattern (Paper 13, 15% discount rate, tax 25%): lease payment 52,000 a year (in advance, years 1 to 5 as shown), tax saving 25% × 52,000 = 13,000 a year one year later; PV of net cost 136,418 against buying 141,758, so lease. Always show both columns, use the after-tax cost of debt or the rate given, and state the decision. Other factors: maintenance, flexibility, obsolescence, cash available, risk of residual value, effect on gearing (a lease liability is also recorded under IFRS 16).

Reporting and performance calculations

IFRS 8: reportable segments

A segment is reportable if it meets any one 10% test: revenue (including inter-segment), profit or loss, or assets. Revenue of A 40, B 25, C 20, D 9, E 6 (total 100). Profits: A +12, B +8, C −5, D +1, E −2.

Revenue test: 10% of 100 = 10 → A, B, C qualify; D (9) and E (6) do not
Profit test: the greater of combined profits of profitable segments (12 + 8 + 1 = 21) and combined losses (5 + 2 = 7) is 21; 10% = 2.1. C (5) qualifies; D (1) and E (2) do not
Coverage: reportable segments must cover at least 75% of external revenue: A + B + C = 85%OK

A segment that met the tests last year but not this year may still be reported if management judges it important. Others are combined in "all other segments".

Official answer (Paper 2, seven segments): total revenue 40,740, so 10% = 4,074; profit test base 4,140 (profits exceed losses of 630), so 10% = 414; assets 38,860, so 10% = 3,886. Metals, Furniture, Glassware and Sporting goods passed; their external revenue was only 55.7% of 28,520. Because the 75% external revenue test failed, further segments were added in order of external revenue (Farming equipment, then Home appliances, then Plumbing) until coverage reached 75% even though they missed the 10% tests.

Contribution and segment margin statementRevenue − variable costs = contribution margin; − traceable (avoidable) fixed costs = segment margin (a good measure of a segment's performance); − common fixed costs = operating profit. Prepare by market, then by product, in columns with a total. Use it to judge whether to drop a segment: drop only if its segment margin is negative and costs are really avoidable.
Earnings-manipulation indices (Beneish type)DSRI (receivable days index) >1: revenue may be pushed. GMI (gross margin index = old margin ÷ new margin) >1: margin falling, pressure to manipulate. AQI (asset quality index) >1: more costs capitalised. SGI (sales growth) high: pressure. DEPI (depreciation index) >1: slower depreciation raises profit. LVGI (leverage) >1: covenant pressure. TATA (accruals to assets) high: profit not backed by cash. Say what each movement implies and what to check. Official reading (Paper 6): Days Sales in Receivable Index up, Asset Quality Index up and Leverage Index up = manipulation more likely; Gross Margin Index below 1 and Depreciation Index below 1 = manipulation less likely.
Short accounting partsAnswer outline
Software or platform as a service (PaaS)Official answer (Paper 3): the customer does not control the platform or tools (the supplier keeps the intellectual property), so access is a service contract expensed as incurred: not an intangible asset (IAS 38 needs identifiability and control), not PPE (no control of servers), not a lease (no right to direct the use of an identified asset: IFRS 16). The CRM software the company develops itself may be an intangible asset if the IAS 38 development criteria are met.
Impairment of foreign units (INTRACO, Paper 13)IAS 36: estimate cash flows in the currency in which they arise, discount at a rate for that currency (its own risk-free rate, inflation and risk), then translate the present value at the spot rate at the date of the value-in-use calculation. One discount rate for all cash-generating units is wrong because risk differs by unit; the home 10-year bond rate does not match foreign inflation.
Cash-settled share appreciation rightsLiability at fair value at each year end × proportion of the vesting period elapsed; expense is the movement; remeasure until settlement (Corporate Reporting Chapter 32).
CryptocurrencyNot cash or a financial asset (no contractual right). Usually an intangible asset (IAS 38, cost or revaluation if an active market) or inventory if held for sale in the ordinary course (IAS 2). Bangladesh Bank restrictions are a legal risk.
Joint arrangement (Paper 2, Rangs Renewables)Joint control exists when decisions need unanimous consent (here 70/30 ownership but equal directors and unanimity). Classify by rights and obligations, not ownership: if the parties take all the output in proportion (7:3), cannot sell to third parties freely, and pay prices that make the vehicle break even, they have direct rights to assets and obligations for liabilities, so it is a joint operation (account for the share of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses). Otherwise it is a joint venture (equity method). Check control (IFRS 10) first.
Deferred taxTemporary difference × tax rate; DTA only if profit is probable (Corporate Reporting Chapter 32).
IFRS 7 risksCredit, liquidity and market risk: nature, extent, how managed, with sensitivity analysis.
Fair value (IFRS 13)Exit price in the principal market; hierarchy of inputs (Level 1 to 3).
Transfer price rangeMinimum for the selling division = marginal cost + opportunity cost (with spare capacity: marginal cost; without: the external price). Maximum for the buying division = what it can pay (external buying price or net marginal revenue). A deal exists only if minimum ≤ maximum. Official pattern (Paper 12): where the transfer saves division A selling overhead of 50 and saves B rework of 40, a price that shares the saving equally (375) is fair to both, or take the midpoint of the range (between 380 and 420 = 400). A fair price for both managers' evaluation is often a negotiated price within the range or the market price.
Performance evaluation answersProblems with narrow targets: gaming, short-termism, unfairness (uncontrollable items), demotivation, conflict. Features of a good system: agreed, controllable, balanced (financial and non-financial), consistent with strategy, regularly reviewed, linked to fair reward and feedback.
