Rights issue example

Current share price 200. The company offers 1 new share for every 4 held at 150.

4 shares at 200 + 1 new share at 150 = 950 for 5 shares950
Theoretical ex-rights price (TERP) = 950 ÷ 5190
Value of a right per new share = 190 − 15040
Value of rights per existing share = 40 ÷ 410

A shareholder who neither takes up nor sells the rights loses value. The discount does not cost shareholders anything if they exercise or sell. Accounting: equity issued at the proceeds net of issue costs.

Cost of equity (CAPM)Ke = Rf + β × (Rm − Rf). Example: Rf 5%, market return 11%, β 1.2 → Ke = 5% + 1.2 × 6% = 12.2%. β measures systematic risk (sensitivity to the market). Alternative: dividend growth model Ke = D₁ ÷ P₀ + g.
Portfolio theoryCombining securities that do not move together reduces unsystematic (specific) risk. Systematic (market) risk remains and is what CAPM prices. Investors need return only for systematic risk.

Stock markets and market efficiency

A stock exchange provides a place to raise capital (primary market) and to trade shares (secondary market). Listing needs a minimum size, history, free float and disclosure. In Bangladesh the main exchanges are the Dhaka Stock Exchange (DSE) and the Chittagong Stock Exchange (CSE), regulated by the BSEC. Smaller or growth markets exist for smaller firms (for example AIM in the UK).

Efficient market hypothesisPrices already reflectImplication
Weak formAll past price dataChart patterns do not give excess returns
Semi-strong formAll public informationAnalysing published accounts gives no advantage; prices react quickly to news
Strong formAll information, including private (insider)Even insiders cannot profit (unlikely in practice)

Factors affecting share prices: company results and forecasts, dividends, sector news, interest rates, inflation and economic outlook, exchange rates, political events, investor sentiment, supply and demand. Implications for managers: in an efficient market the share price is a fair measure of value, so focus on value creation and good communication; creative accounting does not fool the market for long.

Bond valuation, yields and credit risk

Bond price = PV of coupons + PV of redemption value, discounted at the market yield (gross redemption yield). Flat yield = annual coupon ÷ market price. Gross redemption yield (yield to maturity) = the discount rate (IRR) that makes PV of cash flows equal to the price (spreadsheet: RATE or YIELD).
Price and interest ratesWhen market interest rates rise, bond prices fall, and the reverse. Long-dated and low-coupon bonds change more. Duration measures average time to receive cash flows and the sensitivity to yield: price change ≈ −modified duration × change in yield. Example: modified duration 4.2 and yields up 1% → price falls about 4.2%.
Yield curvePlot of yield against maturity. Normal: upward sloping (compensation for time). Flat or inverted: expectations of lower rates or recession. Yield = risk-free rate + credit spread + liquidity premium.

Credit (default) risk: the risk that the borrower fails to pay. Higher risk means a higher credit spread (extra yield over government bonds) and a higher cost of debt. Credit ratings (AAA to D) and financial ratios (interest cover, gearing, cash flow) assess it. The cost of debt is the yield to the investor, adjusted for tax relief.

Leasing

Attractions of leasingSpreads cost, preserves cash, 100% finance, flexibility and obsolescence cover (operating leases), tax effects, and off-balance sheet appearance is no longer allowed for lessees under IFRS 16 (liability recorded). Sale and leaseback raises cash from owned assets while keeping use.
Lease or buy decisionCompare the NPV of leasing (lease payments after tax relief) with the NPV of buying (outlay less tax allowances and residual value), discounted at the after-tax cost of debt. Choose the lower cost. Include maintenance, tax timing and residual value.

Derivatives

A derivative is a contract whose value depends on an underlying item (a currency, interest rate, commodity, share). Used to hedge risk, to speculate or to arbitrage.

DerivativeMeaningUse
Forward contractAgreement now to buy or sell at a fixed price on a future date (over the counter, tailored)Fix a future exchange rate or commodity price
Forward rate agreement (FRA)Fixes the interest rate on a future loan or deposit; settled in cash for the differenceHedge interest rate risk for a single period
FuturesStandardised contract on an exchange with daily margin paymentsHedge or speculate; high liquidity
Options (call or put)The right, not the duty, to buy (call) or sell (put) at an exercise priceProtect against adverse moves while keeping the upside; costs a premium
SwapsExchange of cash flows (for example floating interest for fixed, or currency swaps)Convert floating-rate debt to fixed; access cheaper markets
Credit derivatives and CDOsTransfer credit risk; pooled and tranched debtLinked to the 2008 crisis; complex and opaque
Option value depends onCurrent share price, exercise price, time to expiry, volatility, risk-free rate, dividends. Higher volatility and longer time raise value. Black-Scholes prices European options from these inputs. Monte Carlo simulation values complex options by simulating many price paths.
Limitations of derivativesCost, basis risk (hedge does not match exposure), counterparty risk, complexity and misuse, accounting volatility, need for controls and limits.

Markets: exchange-traded (standard terms, clearing house, margin, low credit risk) and over-the-counter (customised, higher credit risk). Participants: hedgers, speculators, arbitrageurs, banks. Derivatives hedging is detailed in Chapter 15.

Financial reporting for financial instruments

  • IFRS 9 recognition and classification: financial assets at amortised cost, FVOCI or FVTPL by business model and cash flow test (Corporate Reporting Chapter 33). Derivatives are at fair value through profit or loss unless in a hedge relationship.
  • Fair value: use quoted prices where possible; otherwise models. Assurance over fair values needs testing of inputs, models and controls and the use of experts.
  • Derecognition: remove an asset when rights expire or when substantially all risks and rewards are transferred; a repo does not derecognise.
  • Equity: issue proceeds in equity; buyback reduces equity; classification as liability if the issuer has an obligation to pay (IAS 32).
  • Reclassification of assets only when the business model changes. Impairment: expected credit loss model (12-month or lifetime).
  • Leases (IFRS 16): right-of-use asset and lease liability (Corporate Reporting Chapter 32).
Exam focus

Typical tasks: advise on a method of raising funds (rights issue calculation, bond, convertible); price a bond and explain yield changes; choose a lease or buy; explain a derivative and why it hedges; assess market efficiency and share price reaction.
