Ethics and professional behaviour

Typical SBM questionAnswer
Identify ethical issues for an FC, manager or member in a dilemmaFor each issue: facts → principle at stake (integrity, objectivity, competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour) → threat (self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity, intimidation) → action (refuse, escalate to audit committee, consult ICAB, document, resign if unresolved). See Chapter 19.
Independence threats and safeguards for an audit firmTable: threat → example from the case (fees, shares, family, long association, non-audit service) → safeguard (remove the person, rotate, second partner review, decline service, disclose to the audit committee).
Non-compliance with law (NOCLAR) found by the auditor (suspected sanctions breach, Jul–Aug 2025)Official steps: obtain a legal analysis (substance over form); discuss with management and those charged with governance (audit committee) immediately; evaluate whether further action is needed; if no timely corrective action, you may be required to disclose to regulators depending on the jurisdiction; assess the effect on the opinion (non-disclosure may be a material misstatement; modified opinion or emphasis of matter if unresolved); consider withdrawing if the client refuses remediation. A line manager who resists reporting a control deficiency must be overruled: document and escalate under ISQM 1. Conclusion: an offshore structure to avoid sanctions is not ethically sound.
Why is "anything legal is ethical" not enough?Law is a minimum standard, it lags behind new issues, differs by country and has gaps; ethics asks what is right and fair to stakeholders and protects reputation and trust. Examples: aggressive tax planning, misleading but lawful marketing, exploiting weak labour law abroad.
Accepting a new client (client acceptance)Integrity of owners and management; reason for change of auditor and contact with the predecessor; conflicts and independence; competence and resources; money-laundering and PEP checks; fees and time; legal status of the appointment. Decline if integrity is in doubt.
Key components of a code of conduct (any five)Purpose and values; scope and who it applies to; conflicts of interest; gifts, hospitality and bribery; confidentiality and data; fair dealing with customers and suppliers; anti-discrimination and safety; use of company assets; speaking up and whistleblowing; consequences of breach; training and review.
Ethical position of non-executive directorsIndependent judgement for all shareholders (not for the CEO); challenge management; guard against conflicts; oversee audit, risk and pay; speak up and, if ignored, record dissent or resign.
Building an ethical framework and culture (report)Tone at the top; code of conduct; training; clear reporting and whistleblowing; ethics committee or officer; link to performance and reward; monitoring and audit; response to breaches; supplier standards. Use headings and an action plan.
How culture caused a scandal and how to recover (NewGen, Volkswagen)Cause: pressure to hit targets, fear of speaking up, weak oversight, reward for results not ethics. Recovery: admit the problem, new leadership and board oversight, compensate those harmed, fix controls and incentives, independent investigation, transparent reporting, rebuild trust over time.
Supply chain risks (child labour) and compliance policyRisks: harm to children, legal action, loss of buyers, boycotts, reputation, forced sourcing changes. Mitigation: supplier code, due diligence and audits, traceability, certification, grievance channels, training, remediation and support for children, fair prices, exit from non-compliant suppliers.

Audit and assurance short parts

TopicAnswer outline
Engagement letter issues (ISA 210)Check it states: objective and scope; management's responsibilities (framework, internal control, access to information, written representations); auditor's responsibilities and limits (reasonable, not absolute, assurance); reporting framework and form of report; fees and timing; access and deadlines; use of experts; liability and restrictions; reference to other information; any unusual or inconsistent terms (such as fee contingent on the opinion or restriction of scope) that must be fixed before accepting.
Written representations (ISA 580)Confirm management's responsibilities and key assertions (completeness, related parties, litigation, subsequent events). Useful but not sufficient alone, since they are self-serving. If management refuses, reconsider integrity and the opinion.
Improper revenue recognition (fraud techniques)Fictitious sales, side agreements, bill-and-hold, channel stuffing, premature recognition, cut-off manipulation, round-tripping, improper estimates of returns and discounts, recording revenue as principal when an agent. Procedures: analytics, cut-off, confirmations, contract review, post year-end credit notes.
Big data, analytics and blockchain in auditBenefits: test whole populations, find anomalies, reduce sampling risk, real-time evidence, immutable records. Limits: data quality and completeness, skills, cost, privacy, over-reliance, validating the system itself. Auditor still needs judgement and professional scepticism.
Reporting suspected non-compliance (Bangladesh)Follow the ICAB Code and the FRC Act 2015: raise with management and the audit committee; consider the effect on the opinion; where the law requires or permits, report to the relevant authority (for example FRC, BSEC, Bangladesh Bank). Check the current Act for the exact reporting hierarchy.
Audit manager's actions when an issue is found (intangible assets case)Gather evidence and test against IAS 38 criteria; discuss with management; quantify the misstatement; raise with the engagement partner and those charged with governance; consider modifying the opinion if not corrected; document; consider ethical and legal duties.

For fuller audit templates use Corporate Reporting Chapter 35.

Governance, ESG and sustainability answers

TopicAnswer outline
Boards managing conflicts of interestWritten policy; register of interests; declaration at every meeting; abstain or leave the room; independent directors decide; related-party transactions approved by the audit committee and disclosed; regular training; sanction for breach.
Internal or external board evaluationInternal: cheap, knows the business, may be biased. External: independent, brings benchmarks, more costly. Best practice: annual internal review and an external facilitated review every few years; act on the results.
Role of technology in governanceSecure board portals, virtual meetings, e-voting, real-time dashboards, ESG and risk data, AI for monitoring and fraud detection, blockchain records. Risks: cyber security, over-reliance, privacy, digital divide among directors.
Lessons from a governance scandal (Volkswagen)Culture of results over ethics; dominant leadership and fear of speaking up; weak board and audit oversight; poor controls over engineering and emissions data; reputational, legal and financial cost; remedies: strong ethics, independent directors, whistleblowing, accountability and incentives for compliance.
IFRS S1 and S2: main pointsS1: disclose sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could affect the entity's prospects, with four pillars (governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets). S2: climate-specific: physical and transition risks, scenario analysis, scope 1, 2 and 3 greenhouse-gas emissions, targets.
ESG against SDGESG is a set of factors investors and companies use to measure and report environmental, social and governance performance and risk. SDGs are 17 global goals set by the UN for countries and organisations to achieve by 2030. Companies link ESG targets to SDGs.
Why is ESG reporting important?Investors and lenders use it to judge risk; regulation; customers and buyers demand it; reputation; attracts talent and finance; improves efficiency and risk management; avoids greenwashing when assured.
How integrated reporting developedFrom environmental and social reports (separate, voluntary) to sustainability reports (GRI), then integrated reporting (one report on how the six capitals create value) and now the ISSB standards. Aim: connect financial and non-financial information.
Green financing and greenwashingFunds for green projects; risk that claims are exaggerated. Third-party verification and independent audit check the use of proceeds, the project eligibility and impact reports against a recognised framework, giving investors confidence.
Recycling industry: prospects, challenges, government helpProspects: waste volume, circular economy, export demand for recycled textiles and plastics, jobs. Challenges: informal collection, weak standards, finance, technology, environmental harm. Government: incentives and tax breaks, standards and certification (such as GRS), infrastructure for collection, green finance, enforcement, skills and R&D.

Bangladesh economy and industry cases

TopicPoints to make
Syndicates and market manipulationTraders agree prices and hold stock to create shortages. Government can: strict laws and penalties against price-fixing and hoarding, market surveillance using technology to spot irregular trades in real time, support for farmer cooperatives to bypass middlemen, subsidies and incentives that lower farmers' cost and raise bargaining power. A sustainable solution (official answer): a digital marketplace where farmers list produce and consumers buy directly with transparent pricing. For a youth initiative such as "Youth Against Syndicate", sustainability depends on scalability, logistics and storage, and continued community support.
Port competitiveness (Chittagong)Capacity and congestion, long vessel turnaround, old equipment, road-only hinterland links. Actions: modernise equipment, expand terminals and deep-sea ports, digital port systems, rail and river links, performance targets, private participation, customs speed. Compare with regional ports.
LDC graduationOfficial answer (Jul–Aug 2025): why graduate despite drawbacks: national pride and prestige, Vision 2041 (developed country), avoiding the "LDC trap" of dependence on preferences. Benefits: better credibility and higher quality FDI beyond RMG (technology, pharmaceuticals, higher-end manufacturing), forced diversification of exports, stronger domestic institutions. Immediate RMG challenges: loss of price competitiveness as tariffs apply (the answer cites about 8 to 12% in the EU under GSP Plus rules), higher compliance and cost, pressure to move up the value chain. Check the current graduation date before quoting it.
Geopolitical tension and global financeSupply chain disruption, energy and commodity price rises, inflation, higher rates, currency swings and capital flight, sanctions, trade barriers, weaker confidence; risk of stagflation and financial crisis; effects on importing countries such as Bangladesh.
Porter's competitive advantage of nations (Bangladesh)Use the diamond: factor conditions (labour, location, skills, infrastructure), demand conditions (home market, quality demanded), related and supporting industries (textile chain, logistics), firm strategy and rivalry (competition among RMG firms), plus government and chance. Recommend point by point actions.
Safeguarding the economy against currency pressureDiversify exports and markets, raise productivity, encourage remittances, build reserves, manage import demand, develop capital markets, hedging tools for firms, stable policy, reduce dependence on a single sector.

Business, digital and strategy short answers

TopicAnswer
B2C e-commerce models and the best fitMarketplace (many sellers), inventory-led (own stock), direct brand store, subscription, social commerce, quick delivery. For Bangladesh consider: payment (cash on delivery, mobile wallets), logistics, trust, internet and delivery in cities, competition and regulation. Recommend a phased mix, e.g. marketplace plus own fast delivery in major cities.
Why entering another country is a strategic decisionLarge and long-term commitment, affects the whole organisation, uncertain, hard to reverse, needs new capabilities and risk exposure, shapes future direction.
First-mover advantages (five)Brand and reputation; scale and experience-curve cost advantage; control of scarce resources and best locations; customer switching costs and loyalty; technology leadership and patents; shaping standards. (Disadvantages: high development cost, market risk, followers copy cheaply.)
Alignment of purpose and cultureNot always perfect: new leaders, growth or acquisitions change culture. A mismatch causes mixed messages, low trust, poor performance, scandal and staff turnover. Fix by leadership role-modelling, hiring and rewards that match values, and regular review.
Market follower strategyCost-efficient imitation, differentiate on service or price, focus on a niche, use proven technology, avoid direct attack on the leader, improve quickly where the leader is weak.
Segmentation bases and benefits of focusDemographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioural. Focus: better fit, loyalty, premium price, efficient promotion, clear brand (Chapter 5).
Social enterprise objectives and valuesPrimary social objective with financial sustainability; values communicated: access, community benefit, environmental care, fair treatment. Check mission drift and funding.
Restructuring and redundancies (Cyba)Value created = PV of cost savings and gains less restructuring costs; discuss estimates and methods; consider redundancy law and cost, morale, loss of key skills, reputation, and tax savings (deductibility, losses carried forward).
Downsizing and crisis strategy (paper manufacturer)Downsizing can cut cost but harms morale and capability if done without a plan. Crisis response: corporate strategy (retrenchment, focus, restructure) and functional actions (cut costs, renegotiate, product mix, cash control, digital sales). Leadership: visible, honest, decisive.

Writing the format asked

ReportTo / from / subject / date → introduction (one line on purpose) → numbered headed sections → conclusion and recommendation → next steps. Use short paragraphs and tables.
MemorandumTo / from / date / subject → purpose → analysis in numbered points → recommendation. Keep it concise (a memo for the Board on financing).
ChecklistNumbered or bulleted action items in a logical order (for ERM: set appetite, identify risks, assess, respond, assign owners, monitor, report, review, train, audit). One line each.
Point-form answersWhen the question says "in point form" (Porter's advantage of nations), give short numbered points with one line of reason each; no long paragraphs.
Final habits

Quote the case, name the tool, give a recommendation, add one risk. Use the "because" test. For every 4 marks, give 4 different points. Leave time at the end to read each part against its verb.
