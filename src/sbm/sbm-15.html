Financial risk management policy: board-approved policy with objectives (hedge not speculate), limits, authority, instruments allowed, reporting and independent monitoring. Reporting: IFRS 7 requires disclosures of risk exposures, sensitivity analysis and how risks are managed.

2

Interest rate risk

A borrower fears rising rates; a depositor fears falling rates. Options: use fixed-rate debt, or use derivatives.

InstrumentHow it works
Interest rate futuresA borrower sells futures (profit if rates rise and prices fall); an investor buys. Standardised and margined
Interest rate options (caps, floors, collars)A cap sets a maximum rate for a borrower (pay a premium, keep the benefit of falling rates). A floor sets a minimum rate for a lender or investor. A collar combines a cap with a floor to reduce the premium
Forward rate agreement (FRA)Fixes the rate for a future period. A "3 v 9" FRA fixes the rate for 6 months starting in 3 months. Cash is paid for the difference between the FRA rate and the market rate
Interest rate swapTwo parties exchange interest payments on the same principal, for example fixed against floating. A company with floating debt that pays fixed on a swap has effectively fixed its rate
FRA example

A company will borrow 10 million for 6 months starting in 3 months. It buys a 3 v 9 FRA at 5%. At settlement the market rate is 6.5%.

Interest paid on the loan at 6.5%: 10m × 6.5% × 6/12325,000
FRA receipt (6.5% − 5%) × 10m × 6/12(75,000)
Net interest = 5% × 10m × 6/12250,000

If the market rate falls to 4%, the company pays the bank under the FRA, so the net cost is still 5%. The FRA fixes the rate; it removes the benefit of a fall. Options keep the benefit but cost a premium.

Devising a strategy: forecast the rate view and exposure period, decide how much to hedge, compare cost (premium, margin, fees), flexibility, basis risk, accounting treatment, and counterparty credit risk.

Foreign exchange risk

Causes of exchange rate movementInflation differences (purchasing power parity), interest rate differences (interest rate parity), trade balance, capital flows, central bank action, political events, speculation.
The two parity formulasPPP: Future spot = Spot × (1 + inflation home) ÷ (1 + inflation foreign). IRP: Forward = Spot × (1 + interest home) ÷ (1 + interest foreign). Currencies with higher inflation or interest weaken against forward.
Receipt in US dollars: forward against money market hedge

A Bangladeshi exporter will receive USD 1,000,000 in 3 months. Spot 110 BDT per USD; 3-month forward 111.5. USD borrowing 4% a year (1% for 3 months); BDT deposit 8% a year (2% for 3 months).

Forward contract
USD 1,000,000 × 111.5BDT 111,500,000
Money market hedge
Borrow USD 1,000,000 ÷ 1.01 nowUSD 990,099
Convert at spot 110BDT 108,910,891
Deposit at 2% for 3 months → BDTBDT 111,089,109
Better choice (higher receipt)Forward: BDT 111.5m

The USD receipt repays the USD loan. For a payment in foreign currency, do the reverse: buy forward, or deposit foreign currency now (money market). Compare by BDT cost and choose the cheaper.

Hedging methodPoints
Forward contractFixes the rate; tailored; no premium; binding even if the rate moves in your favour; counterparty risk
Money market hedgeSame result as forward in theory (parity); useful when no forward market exists; needs borrowing and deposit
Currency futuresStandard sizes and dates, margins; hedge may not match exactly (basis risk)
Currency optionsRight not duty: protects against a bad move and allows gain from a good move; premium paid; good when the amount or the deal is uncertain (for example a tender)
Currency swap and FX swapExchange principal and interest in different currencies; used for long-term hedges and funding in a cheaper market
Natural hedgesMatch foreign income and costs, borrow in the foreign currency, invoice in the home currency, leading and lagging payments, netting

Hedge accounting (IFRS 9)

Without hedge accounting, a derivative is measured at fair value through profit or loss while the hedged item (a future sale, a loan) is not remeasured, so profit swings. Hedge accounting matches the timing of gains and losses of the hedge and the hedged item.

HedgeHedged exposureAccounting
Fair value hedgeChanges in fair value of a recognised asset or liability, or firm commitmentGain or loss on the hedging instrument and on the hedged item (for the hedged risk) both go to profit or loss; the carrying amount of the hedged item is adjusted
Cash flow hedgeVariability of future cash flows (forecast sale, floating-rate interest)Effective portion of the gain or loss on the instrument goes to OCI (cash flow hedge reserve) and is recycled to profit or loss when the hedged item affects profit; ineffective part to profit or loss immediately
Hedge of a net investmentForeign operation's net assetsEffective part to OCI (translation reserve), recycled on disposal
  • Conditions: formal designation and documentation at the start (risk management objective and strategy), an economic relationship between item and instrument, credit risk not dominating, and a sensible hedge ratio. IFRS 9 removed the strict 80–125% effectiveness test of IAS 39.
  • Hedged items: recognised assets and liabilities, firm commitments, highly probable forecast transactions, net investments; groups of items can be designated.
  • Hedging instruments: derivatives (and some non-derivatives for currency risk).
Exam focus

Typical tasks: identify exposures and recommend a hedging strategy with calculation (forward against money market, FRA outcome, option result); explain why not to hedge or the limits of hedging; explain hedge accounting and its effect on profit. Always compare methods on cost, certainty, flexibility, and risk.
