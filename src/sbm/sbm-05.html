1

Marketing and marketing strategy

Marketing identifies, anticipates and satisfies customer needs profitably. Strategic marketing decides where to compete, for which customers and with what value proposition, and links to the business strategy.

Marketing auditA systematic review of the marketing environment, objectives, strategy, organisation, systems and activities: finds strengths, weaknesses and gaps. External: market size and growth, customers, competitors, trends. Internal: products, price, distribution, promotion, people.
Competitor analysisWho are the competitors (current and potential), what are their goals, strategies, strengths and weaknesses, and how will they respond to our moves? Build competitor response profiles. See Chapter 1.
2

Value proposition, segmentation and targeting

The value proposition states the benefits the customer gets for the price: why choose us? It must match a customer need, be different from rivals and be deliverable. Sources of competitive advantage: cost, differentiation, brand, service, innovation, relationships.

Bases for segmentingExamples
DemographicAge, income, family, occupation
GeographicRegion, urban or rural, climate
Psychographic and behaviouralLifestyle, values, usage rate, loyalty, benefits sought
Business marketsIndustry, size, buying process, location

Evaluating segments: size and growth, profitability, competition, fit with strengths, accessibility, measurability. Targeting strategies: undifferentiated (one offer), differentiated (several offers), concentrated (one segment) and micromarketing (individuals).

3

Positioning, pricing and revenue

Positioning is the place a product holds in the customer's mind compared with rivals. Steps: choose the segment, identify what customers value, compare with competitors (a perceptual map with two axes such as price and quality), decide the position, communicate it consistently. Repositioning changes the position when tastes, competitors or the product change.

Pricing toolMeaning
BundlingSell several items for one price (can raise sales; allocate price by relative stand-alone selling prices)
UnbundlingPrice components separately (low headline price, extras charged)
Discounts and markdownsTrade, volume or promotional price cuts. Short-term sales gains but profit and brand damage risk
Skimming and penetrationHigh initial price for innovation; low price to win share fast

Revenue recognition and profit (IFRS 15): revenue is recognised when a performance obligation is satisfied, at a point in time or over time (for example by an output method such as units delivered or milestones). Marketing deals (bundles, discounts, loyalty points) affect when revenue and profit are recognised. See Corporate Reporting Chapter 32.

4

The marketing mix

Product

Features, quality, design, range, brand, packaging, service.

Price

List price, discounts, payment terms, value perception.

Place

Channels, distribution, location, availability, online access.

Promotion

Advertising, sales promotion, PR, direct selling, digital content.

People

Staff and customers in the service encounter.

Process

The flow of service delivery, systems and waiting times.

Physical evidence

Premises, website, uniforms, documents that show quality.

Services are intangible (cannot be touched), inseparable (produced and consumed together), variable (quality differs), perishable (cannot be stored) and give no ownership. So services need strong people, process and physical evidence, and attention to the "moments of truth" when the customer meets the firm.

International marketingStandardise the mix for cost savings and a global brand, or adapt to local needs, culture and regulation. "Glocalisation" combines a global brand with local adaptation.
The internet and the mixProduct (customisation), price (transparency, dynamic pricing), place (direct online sales), promotion (digital and social), people (online service), process (self-service), physical evidence (website, reviews). The customer is at the centre.
5

Databases, CRM and digital marketing

Database marketing uses customer data to target offers, build loyalty and find new customers. Big data adds browsing, purchase and social data. Customer relationship management (CRM) manages all contact with customers to raise retention and lifetime value: capture data, analyse, personalise, reward (loyalty programmes), review.

Push and pullMeaning
Push marketingThe seller sends messages to the customer (email, ads)
Pull marketingThe customer searches and comes to the seller (search, content, social)
  • Benefits of digital marketing: lower cost, targeting, measurement (click and conversion rates, checkout abandonment), speed, two-way contact.
  • Social media: user participation, sharing knowledge and reviews. Limits: loss of control of the message, bad publicity, weak return measurement, privacy and law.
  • Analytics measures cost per sale, customer acquisition cost, lifetime value and campaign return.
6

Brand management and branding strategy

Why brands matterBrands build trust and recognition, support a price premium and loyalty, reduce risk for buyers, ease new product launch and protect against rivals. Brand strength is a competitive advantage.
Brand strategyMust align with business and marketing strategy and with customers' values. Options: corporate brand, product brands, family (umbrella) brands, brand extension, own-label. Risk: damage spreads across a family brand.

Online and offline branding: keep the brand consistent across channels, protect domain names and trademarks, manage online reputation. Developing a position: define identity, promise, personality and values, then test with customers.

7

Valuing brands and intangible assets

Brand equity is the added value a brand gives to a product. Why value brands? Acquisitions, licensing, financing, tax, disputes, management information, and accounting.

Accounting ruleEffect
IAS 38 Intangible assetsInternally generated brands are not recognised. Acquired brands are recognised at cost
IFRS 3 Business combinationsAn acquired brand is identified and recognised at fair value separately from goodwill
IFRS 13 Fair valueUses market participant assumptions; inputs hierarchy
IAS 36 ImpairmentBrands with indefinite life are tested yearly
Valuation methodIdea
Relief from royaltiesValue = present value of the royalties saved by owning the brand rather than licensing it
Premium profitsValue = present value of extra profit the branded product earns over an unbranded one
Capitalisation of earningsBrand earnings × multiple (or ÷ capitalisation rate)
Market transactionsCompare with prices paid for similar brands
Cost approachCost to create or replace (rarely used for brands)
Relief from royalties: example

Brand sales 100 million a year; fair royalty rate 5%; tax 25%; discount rate 10%; useful life 5 years.

Royalty saved: 100m × 5%5.00m
Tax 25%(1.25m)
After-tax saving each year3.75m
Value = 3.75m × 5-year annuity factor at 10% (3.791)14.2m

Measures of intangible value for a whole firm: the market-to-book ratio (market value ÷ book value of equity), Tobin's q (market value ÷ replacement cost of assets), calculated intangible value. A high ratio suggests unrecorded intangible assets or intellectual capital. Problems with valuing brands: subjective inputs, separating brand from other assets, changing markets, double-counting.

Exam focus

Typical tasks: advise on segmentation and positioning for a scenario, evaluate a marketing strategy (digital, CRM), discuss brand strategy in a merger, or calculate and explain a brand or intangible valuation and the related accounting.
