International trading: risks and financing

Trade risksCredit and non-payment, delays, transport and insurance, currency, political (embargo, war, tariffs), legal differences, documentation, long distance and time.
Three currency risksTransaction: gain or loss on settling a foreign-currency deal. Translation: accounting effect when foreign statements are converted. Economic: long-term change in competitiveness from exchange rates (a strong home currency makes exports uncompetitive).
Method of reducing credit risk or financing tradeHow it works
Letter of credit (L/C)Importer's bank promises to pay the exporter when documents match the terms; confirmed L/C adds the exporter's bank guarantee
Documentary collection and bills of exchangeDocuments are released to the buyer against payment or acceptance of a bill
Export credit insuranceInsures against buyer default and political risk
Factoring and invoice discountingSell or borrow against export invoices for immediate cash
ForfaitingSell medium-term export receivables (usually backed by bank guarantee) without recourse
CountertradePay with goods instead of cash when currency is scarce

Manage currency risk using the tools of Chapter 15 and natural hedges (invoice in your own currency, match income and costs, local sourcing).

Appraising a foreign project: two consistent methods

Both give the same NPV if exchange rates follow parity.

  • Method 1: forecast cash flows in foreign currency, discount at the foreign required return, convert the NPV at the spot rate.
  • Method 2: convert each year's cash flow into home currency at the forecast exchange rate (from PPP or forward rates), discount at the home required return.
  • Adjust for local tax, withholding tax on remitted dividends, exchange controls and royalties; consider incremental cash flows to the parent only (those that can be remitted).
3

Financing foreign investments and treasury

  • Capital structure of a multinational: use local borrowing to match local assets and cut exchange risk, take advantage of tax rates, interest rates and subsidies; consider political risk (local debt reduces exposure) and the parent's overall gearing.
  • Borrowing internationally: Eurocurrency loans, Eurobonds, syndicated loans, local bank finance. Compare the cost after exchange rate movements.
  • Equity finance: listing in another market, local shareholders in subsidiaries, cross-listing.
  • Developing markets: weak financial markets, high rates and volatility, development finance institutions, guarantees, local partners.
  • Hedging a net investment: borrow in the foreign currency so that exchange gains on the net assets are offset by losses on the loan (hedge accounting to OCI).
  • Global treasury management: centralise cash, funding and risk management: netting of intra-group payments, pooling of balances, in-house bank, central hedging, centralised policy and reporting. Gains: lower cost, better control. Risks: local rules and tax.
  • Assurance: work on overseas finance and investment risks (reviews of controls, forecasts, compliance).

Exchange controls

Exchange controls limit the amount of local currency that can be converted or sent out of a country, to protect reserves. Effects on investment: profits may be blocked or delayed, which lowers the value of the project to the parent. In appraisal include only cash flows that can be remitted, or the value of blocked funds reinvested locally.

Ways to deal with exchange controlsIdea
Royalties, management fees and interest on intra-group loansTake profit out as deductible payments rather than dividends

Local reinvestment or local borrowingUse trapped cash locally and lower the exposure
CountertradeTake goods instead of cash

Dividend policy and transfer pricing in multinationals

Dividend policy of a groupSubsidiaries pay dividends to the parent to meet its investment, financing and shareholder payments. Consider: local reinvestment needs, withholding tax and tax credits, exchange controls, exchange rates, timing, the need for managerial control, minority shareholders' rights, and the parent's cash needs.
Transfer pricingThe price at which goods, services or intellectual property pass between group companies. Bases: market price (best if a market exists), cost (marginal, full) or cost-plus, negotiated. Aims: goal congruence, fair performance measures, divisional autonomy, tax efficiency.

Factors: tax rate differences, tariffs and customs, exchange controls, restrictions on profit remittance, minority interests, motivation of managers. Manipulation (setting prices to shift profit to a low-tax country) is regulated: tax authorities require arm's length prices following OECD guidelines, documentation, and may adjust profits and impose penalties. Reputation risk is serious. Bangladesh also has transfer pricing rules for related-party transactions.

Exam focus

Typical tasks: advise on financing or hedging an international trade; appraise an overseas investment including political risk, exchange controls and remittance; recommend the form of entry (branch, subsidiary, JV); discuss transfer pricing and its ethics and tax risk; design a treasury function.
