Information technology and strategy

Information systems (IS) are the people, processes and technology that collect, process and distribute information. IT is the hardware, software and networks. IT can support a strategy (efficiency), shape it (new channels) or create it (new business models).

How IT changes strategyExamples
Lower costAutomation, self-service, e-procurement
DifferentiationPersonalised products, apps, faster delivery
New markets and channelsE-commerce, platforms, global reach
Changes in industry structureLower entry barriers, more buyer power through price comparison, new substitutes
Integration of the value chainReal-time links with suppliers and customers
Networks and cloudIntranet (internal), extranet (partners), internet (public). Cloud computing offers software, platforms and infrastructure as a service: low upfront cost, scalability, access anywhere. Risks: provider dependence, security and legal location of data, outages.
E-business strategyChoose how far to move processes online: e-commerce (selling), e-procurement (buying), e-collaboration (partners). Digital transformation changes processes, culture and the business model, not just systems.

IT and competitive advantage: an IT advantage is temporary if rivals can buy the same technology. Advantage comes from how it is used with unique data, processes and people. Beware of spending on IT with no clear strategic benefit.

Information for planning and control: management accounting tools

Break-even analysis

Selling price 50 per pair, variable cost 25, fixed costs 300,000, expected sales 20,000 pairs.

Contribution per unit 50 − 2525
Break-even units 300,000 ÷ 2512,000
Break-even revenue 12,000 × 50 (or fixed ÷ C/S ratio 50%)600,000
Margin of safety (20,000 − 12,000) ÷ 20,00040%
Target profit 100,000: units (300,000 + 100,000) ÷ 2516,000

Multi-product break-even: use the weighted average contribution per unit (or C/S ratio) at the expected sales mix; the answer depends on the mix assumed. Limits: assumes constant prices and costs, linear behaviour and a fixed mix.

ToolKey points
Costs (absorption and marginal)Marginal costing for decisions (relevant costs: future, incremental, cash); absorption costing for inventory valuation
PricingCost-plus, market-based, value-based, demand-based (price elasticity), skimming, penetration
Budgets and flexed budgetsCompare actual with a budget flexed to actual activity to isolate efficiency from volume effects
Variance analysisPrice and usage (material), rate and efficiency (labour), expenditure and efficiency (overhead), sales price and volume. Investigate significant ones: cause, controllability, linked variances
Expected valuesWeigh outcomes by probability (Chapter 7)
Balanced scorecard and sustainability informationAdd non-financial and ESG measures (Chapter 4)
Transfer pricingPrice for goods moved between divisions. General rule: minimum price = marginal cost + opportunity cost; maximum = external market price (or net marginal revenue). Aim: goal congruence, divisional autonomy, fair performance measures. Methods: market price, marginal or full cost, cost-plus, negotiated. Cross-border: tax rules (arm's length).
Variance interpretationFavourable is not always good (cheap materials can cause waste). Check for interdependence: a favourable price variance with an adverse usage variance. Report to the manager who can control it.

Management information systems

Systems include transaction processing, management information systems, decision support, executive information and enterprise applications (ERP: one integrated database for finance, production, sales, HR; CRM; supply chain systems).

  • Sources of information: internal (accounts, operations, HR) and external (market research, competitors, government statistics, industry reports, social media).
  • Strategic management accounting: uses external and forward-looking information such as competitor costs, market share and customer profitability.
  • Link strategic and operational information: use CSFs to decide what information is needed at each level and make sure KPIs roll up.
  • Competitive advantage: a good MIS speeds decisions, finds cost savings, spots customer trends and supports innovation.
4

The value of information

Qualities of good information (ACCURATE)Accurate · Complete · Cost-beneficial · User-targeted · Relevant · Authoritative · Timely · Easy to use.
Value and costValue = the improvement in decisions it allows (higher expected profit, lower risk). Cost-benefit analysis: buy more information only if its expected value exceeds the cost of getting it. Costs: collection, processing, storage, security, user time.

Information risks: inaccurate or incomplete data, loss, theft, unauthorised access, misuse, weak credibility (unknown source), old data, information overload. Assurance over information systems (internal audit, IT audit, external reports) increases reliability.

Cybersecurity and data protection

LayerControls
PeopleTraining and awareness (phishing), acceptable-use policies, background checks, culture of reporting
ProcessAccess rights and least privilege, change control, incident response and recovery plans, backup, supplier checks, regular testing
TechnologyFirewalls, encryption, patching, multi-factor login, monitoring, network segmentation, secure development

Cybersecurity and assurance: boards want assurance that cyber risk is managed. A cyber security risk management report (description by management, controls and an independent opinion) or a penetration test can give it. Data protection laws (such as the EU GDPR; check the current position in Bangladesh) require lawful and fair use of personal data, consent, minimisation, security and prompt breach reporting, with heavy penalties.

6

Knowledge management and digital skills

Knowledge managementExplicit knowledge is written and stored; tacit knowledge sits in people's heads. Convert and share through databases, communities of practice, mentoring, lessons-learned reviews, wikis. KM systems capture, store and share knowledge; organisational learning turns experience into better practice.
Competitive advantage from informationKnowledge-based organisations (consulting, tech, finance) rely on people and data. Use big data and digital tools to share knowledge in the supply chain and with customers. Protect intellectual property.

Digital skills are needed at every level: data literacy, analytics, use of cloud tools and automation, cyber awareness, plus judgement and communication. The accountant's role shifts to interpreting data, advising and assuring.

Exam focus

Typical tasks: advise on an IT or e-business strategy; make a break-even, limiting-factor or pricing decision and comment; analyse variances; evaluate information quality and risk; recommend cybersecurity controls; discuss cost-benefit of information.
