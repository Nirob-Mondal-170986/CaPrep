Business ethics and ethical stances

Ethics are the moral principles about what is right and wrong. Business ethics apply them to commercial decisions: how the organisation treats customers, staff, suppliers, competitors, the community and the environment. Different ethical frameworks give different answers:

ApproachTest
Deontological (duty and rules)Is the action right in itself, whatever the consequence? Would I accept it as a universal rule?
Utilitarian (consequences)Does it give the greatest good for the greatest number?
Virtue ethicsWhat would a person of good character do?
Rights and justiceDoes it respect people's rights and treat them fairly?
Ethical stance of an organisationView
Short-term shareholder interestOnly profit and the law matter; ethics only if profitable
Long-term shareholder interestGood ethics protects reputation and long-term value (enlightened self-interest)
Multiple stakeholder obligationsThe organisation has duties to all stakeholders, not only owners
Shaper of societyThe organisation actively promotes social good, even at a cost to profit

The ICAB Code of Ethics: principles and dilemmas

The ICAB Code of Ethics (based on the IESBA Code) applies to members in practice and in business. Ethics is also regulated by law, professional bodies, company codes and the organisation's culture.

Integrity

Be straightforward and honest; do not be associated with false or misleading information.

Objectivity

No bias, conflict of interest or undue influence.

Professional competence and due care

Keep knowledge up to date; act diligently and in line with standards.

Confidentiality

Do not disclose or use confidential information unless there is a legal or professional right or duty.

Professional behaviour

Comply with law; avoid actions that discredit the profession.

Accountants in business face pressure from employers: to prepare misleading reports, manipulate results, ignore control weaknesses, or support unethical deals. The Code requires them to resist, escalate within the organisation, seek advice, and as a last step consider resigning.

Five threatsSelf-interest (financial or other gain), self-review (judging your own work), advocacy (promoting a position too far), familiarity (close relationships) and intimidation (pressure).
Inducements and briberyGifts, hospitality or payments offered to influence decisions. Accept only if trivial and clearly not meant to influence. Bribery is a crime. In Bangladesh the Prevention of Corruption Act and the Anti-Corruption Commission apply, and multinationals also follow home-country laws such as the UK Bribery Act. Facilitation payments are risky.

Safeguards and action in unethical situations

SafeguardsCreated by the profession or law: education, standards, disciplinary action, external review. In the organisation: code of conduct, ethics training, independent directors and audit committee, whistleblowing, clear authority limits, rotation, second review, ethics officer. Personal: consultation, documenting concerns.
Ethics assuranceAn assurance report on ethics programmes or codes gives stakeholders confidence that the organisation lives its stated values.

Consider confidentiality against disclosure: you may disclose where law requires or allows, in the public interest, or to protect professional interests, but get legal advice first.

4

Ethics and strategy

  • Impact on strategy: ethics affects strategic choice (which markets, suppliers, products), reputation, brand, trust, regulation, recruitment, access to finance and long-term value. Unethical behaviour can destroy a company quickly.
  • Ethics and sustainability: fair treatment of workers and suppliers, safe factories (important for ready-made garments in Bangladesh), environmental responsibility, honest ESG reporting (no greenwashing).
  • Ethics, data and AI: privacy and consent; bias and fairness in algorithms; transparency and explainability; accountability for automated decisions; data security; safe and lawful use of AI; human oversight.
  • Conflicts between ethics and business: pressure to meet targets, cut cost, win contracts, hide bad news, tax avoidance. The aim is a culture where ethical behaviour is rewarded and speaking up is safe.
Ethical issues in the exam: a template

(1) Identify the ethical issues in the scenario and the people affected. (2) Name the principle breached or threat created (integrity, objectivity, self-interest, intimidation...). (3) Consider the law and the Code. (4) Recommend specific actions: stop, escalate, consult, safeguards, disclosure, resignation as last resort. (5) Link to business consequences (reputation, legal, stakeholders). Use facts from the scenario and give a clear action, not only a list of principles.
