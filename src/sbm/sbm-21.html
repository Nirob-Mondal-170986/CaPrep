Spreadsheet Formulae

The spreadsheet functions used in the SBM exam: totals and conditional totals, sorting, investment appraisal (NPV, IRR, XIRR, MIRR), bond functions (RATE, PV), statistics (AVERAGE, standard deviation, correlation, confidence, rank), logical functions (IF, IFS, nested IF), absolute references, TREND and good formatting.

1

Totals, conditions and sorting

FunctionFormatUse
SUM=SUM(B2:B6)Add a range: totals of cash flows, costs
SUMIF=SUMIF(range, criteria, sum_range)Add values that meet one condition (profit for product "Bed")
SUMIFS=SUMIFS(sum_range, range1, crit1, range2, crit2)Add with several conditions (product and region)
COUNTIF and COUNTIFS=COUNTIF(range, criteria)Count items that meet one or more conditions
SORT=SORT(range, column, order)Order data ascending or descending
AVERAGE=AVERAGE(range)Mean of values
2

Investment appraisal and bond functions

FunctionFormatNotes
NPV=NPV(rate, year1:yearN) + year0NPV assumes the first value is at the end of period 1. Do not include the outlay at time 0 in the range; add it afterwards (as a negative)
IRR=IRR(values, [guess])Values must include the time-0 outflow; needs at least one negative and one positive; may give a wrong answer with multiple sign changes
XIRR=XIRR(values, dates)IRR for cash flows on irregular dates
MIRR=MIRR(values, finance_rate, reinvest_rate)Uses the cost of finance for outflows and reinvestment rate for inflows
RATE=RATE(nper, pmt, pv, fv)Yield to maturity of a bond: nper = years, pmt = coupon, pv = negative current price, fv = redemption value
PV=PV(rate, nper, pmt, fv)Price of a bond at a given yield (the result is negative, which means a cost; put a minus sign in front if you want a positive price)
POWER=POWER(number, power)Compound growth: =POWER(1+g, n) or use ^
Example: bond price and yield

An 8% bond with 5 years to maturity, redemption at 100.

  • Price at 10% yield: =-PV(10%, 5, 8, 100) gives 92.42.
  • Yield at a price of 92.42: =RATE(5, 8, -92.42, 100) gives 10%.
  • NPV of the project in Chapter 17: =NPV(10%, B3:B6) + B2 where B2 = −100,000.

Statistics, logic and forecasting

FunctionFormatUse
Standard deviation=STDEV.S(range) (sample) or =STDEV.P(range) (population)Spread of data; choose sample or population correctly
CORREL=CORREL(array1, array2)Correlation coefficient between two sets (−1 to +1)
CONFIDENCE=CONFIDENCE.NORM(alpha, standard_dev, size)Margin for a confidence interval for a mean (alpha 0.05 = 95%)
RANK=RANK(value, range, order)Position of a value in a list (for example projects by NPV)
IF=IF(test, value_if_true, value_if_false)Decision rule: =IF(NPV>0,"Accept","Reject")
IFS and nested IF=IFS(test1, result1, test2, result2, ...)Several conditions without nesting (for example tax bands)
TREND=TREND(known_y, known_x, new_x)Linear regression forecast (for example sales for months 10 to 15)
Absolute referencesUse $ to lock a cell when copying a formula: $B$2 fixes the column and row (a discount rate used in many cells); B$2 fixes the row only. Without it the reference moves and gives wrong answers.
Formatting and good practicePut assumptions (rates, growth) in separate labelled cells and refer to them; do not hard-code numbers inside formulas; label rows and columns; show units; format negatives in brackets; check totals; keep formulas simple so the marker can follow.
Exam note

The manual includes this chapter because spreadsheet skills are part of the syllabus (check how your own exam sitting is delivered). Be ready to build a small model (cash flows, NPV, sensitivity table, ratio comparison), use formulas not typed numbers, and add a clear summary and recommendation. Marks go for correct formulas, clear layout and interpretation. Practise the functions above with Chapter 7, 12, 13 and 17 examples.
