1

How the paper is built

Unlike Corporate Reporting, the SBM paper has no fixed type of question. Each of the five questions is a bundle of several short parts (a), (b), (c) on different topics, usually built on one or two short business cases from Bangladesh or abroad. Typical features:

  • Mixed content in one question: for example a strategy case, then an accounting issue, then an ethics part.
  • Many 2 to 6 mark parts. A "3 mark" part wants three specific points, not an essay.
  • Bangladeshi context: ports, RMG, recycling, airlines, pharmaceuticals, mobile operators, LDC graduation, syndicates, Islamic banking.
  • Calculation parts are modest: a hedge, a netting table, a lease or buy comparison, an acquisition offer price, a segment test. Long consolidation-style workings do not appear.
  • Ethics is almost always present (a full question or a part of one).
What this means for study

Because parts are short and mixed, you need broad coverage of all 21 chapters and a quick, structured way to answer each small part. Learn the frameworks as lists with one application sentence, and practise the standard calculations (Chapter 23). Time: about 2 minutes per 1 mark.

Scoring habits for short, mixed questions

  • Answer the exact verb: "identify" = list with one line each; "explain" = reason; "analyse" = split and link to cause; "evaluate" or "critically assess" = for and against and a conclusion; "recommend" = a clear choice with reasons.
  • Point count equals marks: 3 marks need 3 distinct points, each tied to the case (use names and numbers).
  • Apply the "because" test (Chapter 8): every point says why it matters in this case.
  • Use the model, then the case: name the framework in one line, then spend the rest applying it. Choose the tool that fits (industry problem: five forces; market and political change: PESTEL; capability: resource analysis).
  • Calculations in a table with the formula written, and one line of interpretation.
  • Ethics: name the principle, the threat and the action (Chapter 19). Never end on a list of principles alone.
  • Format asked: memo, report, note, checklist, canvas: use the headings.
  • Never leave a part blank; 2 marks are often earned by one sensible, relevant point.
Suggested time plan for 210 minutes

10 minutes reading · 40 per question (about 2 minutes per mark) · 5 minutes to check. Start with the question where you are strongest, but attempt all five parts of every question: marks are spread over many small parts.

If a calculation stalls, write the formula and the steps, leave a gap, and return. Method marks are given even when the final figure is wrong.
