Principles and perspectives of governance

Corporate governance is the system by which companies are directed and controlled. It aims to protect investors, give accountability, and support long-term, ethical and effective business. OECD principles stress shareholder rights, equitable treatment, the role of stakeholders, disclosure and transparency, and board responsibilities.

TheoryIdeaImplication
Agency theoryManagers (agents) may pursue their own interests, not the owners' (principals')Monitoring (audit, boards), incentives (shares, bonuses), disclosure, independent directors
Stewardship theoryManagers want to do a good job and act as stewards of the companyTrust, empowerment, less control; fewer costs of monitoring
Shareholder theoryThe company exists to maximise shareholder wealthFocus on profit and share price
Enlightened shareholder and stakeholder theoryLong-term shareholder value needs fair treatment of employees, customers, suppliers, communityBroader reporting (strategic report, ESG), stakeholder engagement
Governance mechanismsInternal: board and its committees, internal audit and control, remuneration policy, risk management, code of conduct, whistleblowing. External: external audit, regulators, stock exchange rules, institutional investors, market for corporate control, media.
Typical weaknessesDomination by one person (CEO-chairman), board not involved or not informed, lack of supervision, excessive pay unrelated to performance, weak control function and audit independence, misleading accounts and information, culture of secrecy.

Governance and sustainability: the board owns sustainability strategy, sets ESG targets, oversees reporting and links pay to ESG goals.

Institutional investors: they can vote on resolutions, meet directors, push for changes in strategy or pay, and in the worst case sell shares. Issues: short-term pressure, conflicts of interest, free-rider problems and herd behaviour. Stewardship codes encourage active, long-term engagement. The main institutional investors in Bangladesh include the Investment Corporation of Bangladesh (ICB), scheduled banks, merchant banks, BDBL, NBFIs and insurance companies.

Role and effectiveness of the board

RoleSet direction and values; approve strategy and key decisions (the "matters reserved" to the board); monitor management and performance; oversee risk and control; ensure accurate reporting; appoint and pay senior executives; engage shareholders.
Board structureUnitary board (UK, US, Bangladesh): executive and non-executive directors sit together. Two-tier board (Germany): a supervisory board (including employees) oversees a management board.
Effectiveness factorPoints
Size and compositionRight skills, experience and knowledge; balance of executives and non-executives
DiversityGender, age, background and thinking; reduces groupthink
IndependenceNEDs not tied to the company by employment, large shareholdings, business links or long tenure
Performance appraisalAnnual review of the board, committees, chair and directors; periodic external evaluation
Chair and CEOSeparate roles: the chair runs the board, the CEO runs the business. Combining them concentrates power

Non-executive directors: bring independent judgement, challenge strategy, scrutinise performance, review controls and risk, set executive pay, and act as a safeguard in conflicts. They need time, information and independence.

Board committeeMain tasks
Audit committeeReview financial reporting, internal control, risk systems, internal audit and the external auditor's independence and fees; recommend auditor appointment
Remuneration committeeSet executive pay policy and packages, linked to long-term performance

The board also sets strategy, takes responsibility for risk and internal control reviews (strategic and operating risks, assurance procedures), and monitors performance by choosing metrics, setting targets and revising forecasts.

Board agenda in the Bangladesh Code

The manual notes that the Bangladesh Code lists many matters a board must consider: annual operating plans and budgets, capital budgets, quarterly results, internal audit reports (including theft and misconduct), notices from revenue authorities, serious accidents or pollution problems, default on interest or principal to creditors, material product liability, joint ventures, appointment and pay of senior officers below the board (including the company secretary and top finance officers), and labour issues.

4

Governance, organisational structure and strategy

Governance is also strategic: good governance supports culture, leadership, alignment, systems and structure.

  • Culture and leadership: the "tone at the top" shapes ethics and control; poor leadership creates fraud and risk-taking.
  • Alignment: objectives, incentives, structure and information must point the same way.
  • Systems and structure: clear authority, delegation limits and reporting lines; control of subsidiaries and overseas units.
  • Strategy implementation: the board approves plans, watches for unexpected change, and monitors progress against milestones.

Corporate governance in Bangladesh

Companies listed on the Dhaka Stock Exchange (DSE) and Chittagong Stock Exchange (CSE) must follow the Corporate Governance Code issued by the Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC). Insurance companies follow the Corporate Governance Guidelines of the Insurance Development and Regulatory Authority (IDRA). The Companies Act 1994 sets the directors' statutory duties.

Area in the BSEC Code (2018)Main requirements (check the latest text)
BoardSize roughly 5 to 20 directors; at least one-fifth independent directors; chair and CEO are different people, and the chair is not an executive
Key officersAppoint a Managing Director or CEO, Company Secretary, Chief Financial Officer and Head of Internal Audit and Compliance, with defined roles
Audit committeeAt least three members, led by an independent director with finance skills; reviews reporting, controls, internal and external audit

Exam note

The manual says you need awareness of the Bangladesh Code, not detailed clause numbers. Use the Code to recommend improvements in a scenario (independent directors, audit committee, split of chair and CEO), and compare with international codes.

Exam focus

Typical requirements: identify governance weaknesses in a scenario (dominant CEO, weak board, no committees) and recommend changes; explain the role of NEDs and committees; evaluate board effectiveness; link governance failures to risk and strategy; discuss compliance culture.
