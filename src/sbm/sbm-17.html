NPV vs IRR

If NPV and IRR disagree when ranking mutually exclusive projects, use NPV: it measures the actual increase in wealth. Use the IRR only as a margin-of-safety indicator (here the cost of capital could rise from 10% to 26% before the project fails).

Adjusted present value (APV) splits the appraisal into a base-case NPV (all-equity financed) and the PV of financing side effects (tax shield, issue costs, subsidised loans): see Chapter 12. Use it when the project changes the financing structure.

2

Inflation and taxation

InflationBe consistent: discount money (nominal) cash flows at the money rate, or real cash flows at the real rate. Real rate = (1 + money rate) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. Example: money rate 12%, inflation 5% → real rate = 1.12 ÷ 1.05 − 1 = 6.7%. Different items can have different inflation rates (wages, materials, prices).
TaxationInclude tax on operating cash flows, tax allowances (capital allowances) as cash savings in the year they reduce tax (watch the timing: often one year after), and balancing charges or allowances on disposal. Use after-tax cost of capital if cash flows are after tax. Ignore depreciation itself (non-cash) except for its tax effect.

Relevant cash flows: future, incremental, cash. Include working capital needs (and recovery at the end), opportunity costs and lost contribution; exclude sunk costs, allocated overheads that do not change, interest and financing flows (they are in the discount rate), and depreciation.

International investment appraisal

  • Two methods (Chapter 16): foreign cash flows at the foreign rate then convert the NPV; or convert cash flows at forecast exchange rates (PPP) and discount at the home rate. Results should agree.
  • Cash flows to include: only those that benefit the parent: after local tax, after withholding tax, and subject to exchange controls. Include royalties, management fees and transfer pricing effects if real.
  • Exchange rate assumptions change project value: a falling foreign currency cuts the home-currency value of inflows.
  • Double taxation relief (DTR): profit taxed abroad and again at home is relieved by a tax credit up to the home rate. The extra home tax is the difference if the home rate is higher.
  • Reporting: translation of the foreign subsidiary, impairment tests using the discount rate and local conditions, disclosures of risks.
5

Real options

Traditional DCF treats a project as a one-off, now-or-never decision. In reality managers can change the plan as information arrives. This flexibility has value that NPV ignores: a project with a negative NPV may be worth doing if it creates valuable options.

Option to delay

Wait until market conditions are clear (like a call option).

Option to expand

A small first step creates the right to a larger next stage if it succeeds.

Option to abandon

Stop and sell the assets if it fails (like a put option); limits the downside.

Option to redeploy

Switch the assets or capacity to another use or product.

Valuing options: use an option pricing model (Black-Scholes, binomial) with inputs: PV of project cash flows (underlying asset), cost of investing (exercise price), time, volatility, risk-free rate. Problem: inputs such as volatility are hard to estimate. Practical approach: calculate the static NPV, then add the value of the main options with a judgement of their size.

6

Externalities and social responsibility

Externalities are costs or benefits of a project borne by others, not the investor: pollution, noise, congestion, jobs created, community benefits. Responsible decision-making considers impacts (what the project does to the environment and society) and dependencies (what it relies on, such as water, skills, community goodwill).

  • Manage costs: classify environmental costs (prevention, detection, internal and external failure) and include them in the appraisal; use life-cycle costing and carbon prices.
  • Social costing: estimate monetary values for social impacts (cost-benefit analysis), or use scoring where money values are unreliable.
  • Other costs: hidden costs (regulation, reputation, licence to operate), future clean-up and decommissioning (IAS 37).
  • Reporting: provisions for obligations, impairment from climate risks, sustainability disclosures.
Exam focus

Typical tasks: calculate NPV, IRR, payback with inflation and tax and recommend; do sensitivity analysis and comment; appraise an overseas project with exchange rates and remittance limits; identify real options and non-financial factors (strategic fit, ESG, stakeholders). Show workings in a table and finish with a decision and conditions.
