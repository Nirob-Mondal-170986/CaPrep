Leadership versus managementManagement copes with complexity: planning, budgeting, organising, controlling, solving problems. Leadership copes with change: setting direction and vision, aligning and motivating people. Organisations need both.
Strategic leadershipLeading the whole organisation: set vision and strategy, shape culture, allocate resources, build capabilities, balance short and long term, take responsibility for major decisions.

The role of leadership in strategy

  • Communication: leaders explain the vision and strategy clearly and repeatedly, listen, and use the right channel and message for each audience. Poor communication is a main reason change fails.
  • Decision making: leaders decide under uncertainty. Use evidence and analysis, test assumptions, involve experts, consider stakeholders and risks, and take responsibility. Avoid groupthink and overconfidence.
  • Business ethics: leaders set the tone; unethical leadership spreads (Chapter 19).
The role of the chartered accountant (ACA) in strategyProvide and analyse financial and non-financial information; model options and risks; appraise investments and valuations; measure performance; advise on funding; assure information; act as ethical conscience and as business partner on the leadership team.
Strategic alignmentLeaders and managers must align structure, people, systems, culture and incentives with the strategy; they translate strategy into plans and targets and keep the organisation focused.

Entrepreneurial orientation and strategic innovation

Autonomy

Freedom to act and decide.

Innovativeness

Support for new ideas, experiments and creativity.

Risk taking

Willingness to commit resources to uncertain projects.

Proactiveness

Acting ahead of the market, shaping it.

Competitive aggressiveness

Intense stance towards rivals.

Strategic innovationInnovation that changes the strategy: new products, processes, business models, markets. Incremental (improve what exists) against radical or disruptive (new to the market, may undermine leaders). Needs leadership support, funding, tolerance of failure and links to strategy.
IntrapreneurshipEntrepreneurial behaviour inside an established firm: staff and teams develop new ideas with company resources. Needs freedom, time, funds, rewards and protection from bureaucracy; risks: weak control, conflict with core business.
5

Open innovation and emerging business models

Open innovation uses both internal and external ideas and routes to market: partnerships with universities and start-ups, licensing in and out, crowdsourcing, joint ventures, innovation hubs and incubators, corporate venture capital. Benefits: speed, lower cost, wider ideas, shared risk. Risks: loss of intellectual property, dependence, culture clash, coordination.

Leading business model innovationPoints
Technology-enabled modelsPlatforms, subscriptions, sharing, digital services: need new skills, data, partnerships and risk management
Start-upsFast, flexible, founder-led; risk of weak control and cash shortage; leaders must build structure while keeping speed
Established businessesResources and customers but inertia and fear of cannibalising existing business; use separate units, intrapreneurs, partnerships
Stakeholder managementInvestors, regulators, customers and staff must understand and support the new model
Leadership developmentBuild digital, strategic and ethical skills; coaching, mentoring, rotation
Exam focus

Typical tasks: recommend a leadership style for a situation (change, turnaround, team stage); explain leader and manager roles in implementing strategy; evaluate how to encourage innovation or intrapreneurship; advise on open innovation or a new business model; discuss the accountant's role in strategy.
