1

Capital structure

The capital structure is the mix of debt and equity used to finance the business. Test it with suitability, acceptability and feasibility.

Suitability

Fits the strategy, assets and business risk; matches the life of assets (long-term assets with long-term funds); gearing suits the stability of cash flows.

Acceptability

Do shareholders and lenders accept the risk and return? Effect on EPS, gearing, dividends, control, covenants.

Feasibility

Can the funds actually be raised? Market conditions, security available, covenants, issue costs, time.

TheoriesTraditional: there is an optimal gearing where WACC is lowest. Modigliani and Miller (no tax): capital structure does not change value. With tax: debt interest is tax deductible, so more debt adds value (tax shield), until financial distress costs and agency costs rise.
Pecking order theoryFirms prefer retained earnings, then debt, then new equity as a last resort (information and issue costs). Explains why profitable firms often have low gearing.
WACC and asset beta

Equity 60 (market value), debt 40; Ke 12.2% (β 1.2); pre-tax cost of debt 6%; tax 25%.

Equity: 60% × 12.2%7.32%
Debt: 40% × 6% × (1 − 0.25)1.80%
WACC9.12%
Asset (ungeared) beta = 1.2 × 60 ÷ (60 + 40 × 0.75)0.80

To value a project in a different business, take a proxy company's equity beta, ungear it (βa = βe × E ÷ (E + D(1−t))), then regear to the project's financing: βe = βa × (E + D(1−t)) ÷ E.

Developing technologies and financing: crowdfunding, peer-to-peer lending, fintech platforms, digital assets and blockchain-based fundraising widen options but bring regulatory and security risk.

2

Dividend policy

TheoriesIrrelevance (Modigliani and Miller): value depends on investment and earning power, not on how profit is split. Relevance: dividends signal management's confidence; some shareholders want income (clientele effect); cash in hand is valued more than a promise ("bird in the hand"); tax differences matter.
Influences on policyProfit and cash flow; investment needs; legal limits on distributable profit; debt covenants; shareholder expectations; tax; liquidity; stability of earnings; cost of new finance.

Impact of change: an increase signals good news (price may rise); a cut signals trouble unless explained by investment opportunities. Firms often smooth dividends. Alternatives to dividends: share buyback, special dividend, scrip (share) dividend.

3

Financial reconstruction and distress

A financial reconstruction reorganises the capital of a company in difficulty so that it can survive: creditors and shareholders accept changes because they would receive less if the company were liquidated.

ToolWhat happens
Debt for equity swapLenders exchange debt for shares
Extending maturity or lowering interestRepayment holiday, rescheduling
Write-down of debt (haircut)Lenders accept less than full repayment
New equity or rights issueExisting holders put in fresh money (often with a share capital reduction and re-denomination)
Sale of assets or unitsRaise cash and cut debt
RefinancingReplace existing debt with new funding on new terms
SecuritisationPackage receivables or loans and sell them to investors to raise cash
  • Test each party: is it better off than in liquidation? Will it accept? Does the plan make the company viable (cash flow, new strategy, management)?
  • Legal consequences of distress: directors' duties to creditors, wrongful trading, schemes of arrangement and court-supervised rescue, administration, liquidation, personal liability for fraudulent trading. In Bangladesh these are governed by the Companies Act 1994 and the Bankruptcy Act 1997.
  • Managing a company in distress: stabilise cash (13-week cash forecast), prioritise suppliers, talk to lenders early, cut costs, replace weak management, sell non-core assets.
  • Reporting: going concern assessment and disclosures, impairment, classification of debt (covenant breach makes it current), debt modification and extinguishment accounting under IFRS 9, and IFRIC 19 for debt for equity swaps (equity measured at fair value, difference to profit or loss). Assurance work includes going concern reviews and reports on forecasts for lenders.

Demergers, disposals, buyouts and buybacks

TransactionMeaningWhy
Divestment (sell-off)Sell a business unit to another companyRaise cash, exit non-core or loss-making units, refocus, value unlocking
Demerger (spin-off)Split a company into separate companies, with the subsidiary's shares given to existing shareholdersShareholders get two focused companies; removes conglomerate discount

Going privateDelist a listed companySave listing costs, escape short-term pressure and disclosure
Purchase of own sharesCompany buys back sharesReturn cash, support EPS and price, change capital structure
Valuation of divestmentsValue to the seller = the higher of continuing value (PV of cash flows) and sale proceeds. Consider tax on disposal, stranded costs, pension obligations, break fees. The price should exceed the value kept (value-adding disposal).
ReportingIFRS 5: a unit held for sale is shown separately at the lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell; discontinued operations are shown on one line. IFRS 8: segment reporting shows divisional performance. Buybacks must come from distributable profits. See Corporate Reporting Chapter 30 for consolidation of disposals.
5

Financing small and medium-sized companies

Characteristics and problemsOwner-managed, limited track record and security, high failure rate, little negotiating power, cash flow volatility. Banks and investors see them as high risk: the finance gap (lack of funds at fair cost).
Sources of financeOwner's funds and retained profit; bank loans and overdrafts; leasing and hire purchase; invoice finance; trade credit; business angels and venture capital; crowdfunding; government schemes; listing on a smaller market.

Government aid: subsidised loans, guarantees, grants, tax incentives. In Bangladesh, Bangladesh Bank refinancing schemes and SME financing targets for banks support the sector. Assurance on loan finance: banks may require reports on forecasts, covenant compliance or use of funds. Sustainability in SMEs: start with energy and waste savings and supply-chain requirements from larger customers.

Exam focus

Typical tasks: recommend a capital structure or financing method (S-A-F with numbers: gearing, EPS, interest cover); calculate WACC; discuss dividend policy; design and evaluate a financial reconstruction (who gets what, is each party better off?); advise on a disposal, spin-off or buyout; or review SME funding.
