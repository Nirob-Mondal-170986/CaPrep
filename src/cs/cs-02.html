Financial Statement Analysis

How to analyse the draft financial statements in the case: the first rapid review, the detailed analysis of profit or loss, the balance sheet and cash flow, the ratio families, adjusting the draft first, using the non-financial information, and writing the commentary that earns marks.

5 familiesProfitability, liquidity, ROCE, financial structure, asset efficiency
3 statementsProfit or loss, balance sheet, cash flow: read them together
4 quadrantsCurrent assets and liabilities; non-current assets; equity plus long-term debt
Adjust firstCorrect the draft for the Exhibit 5 issues, then analyse
1

The rapid initial review

Before any ratios, spend a few minutes getting the headlines. Ask:

AreaQuestions
Nature of businessService, retail, manufacturing? Is the sector expanding or contracting? Effect of technology and sustainability?
GrowthGrowing, shrinking or static (life cycle stage)? Organic or by acquisition? Profit, marginal or loss? Stable or erratic?
SizeSmall, medium or large? Single company or group? Local or international? Listed or unlisted?
StabilityGood mix of debt and equity? Able to meet current liabilities? Realistic budgets and forecasts?
Wider contextEconomy (recession or recovery), inflation, interest rates, availability of finance, government policy (duties, taxes)
Skills tip

Do not blame the economy for poor results too early. A business that shrinks less than its sector is not necessarily failing, while an internal cause (weak management, loss of a key customer) may be more serious. Look for the real cause.

2

Profit or loss: how is the business performing?

  • Revenue: trend and how it was achieved (price, volume, new outlets, new products)? Changes in the sales mix and their effect on margin (do a mix table: revenue by product or channel, % share, change).
  • Gross profit: trend in amount and %; margins by product or segment; the effect of input costs and discounts.
  • Costs: pattern against revenue; which are fixed or variable (operating gearing); any unusual item; one-offs to strip out.
  • Operating profit: level, stability, direction; finance cost and tax; dividends.
  • Bridge items between profit and the balance sheet: closing inventory, depreciation and disposals, deferred income, receivable allowances, accruals and prepayments. They are judgemental, so check consistency between years and against the asset in the balance sheet.
3

Balance sheet and cash flow: is it stable?

Balance sheet quadrants(1) current assets, (2) current liabilities, (3) non-current assets, (4) equity and non-current liabilities. Is investment in (3) financed by long-term funds (4)? A shortfall means short-term funds are financing long-term assets (a liquidity and refinancing risk).
Quality of assets and liabilitiesIs inventory slow-moving or overvalued? Receivables overdue or disputed? PPE revalued or impaired? Intangibles real? Are liabilities missing (provisions, penalties, lawsuits, gratuity, tax)? What is the maturity of debt and the covenant position?
Cash flowDoes profit turn into cash? Check operating cash flow against profit (cash conversion), investment, financing and the movement in cash. Profit explains part of the change in the balance sheet; cash flow explains the rest.
Link the three statementsA fall in cash with rising profit points to receivables or inventory build-up, or large capital spending. A cash-rich year may hide an overdue payables build-up. Explain, do not list.
4

Ratio families

Ratios are only a starting point, like a PESTLE or SWOT. They are useful if you can calculate them quickly and then explain what they mean and why they changed.

FamilyRatios
ProfitabilityGross margin; operating margin; net margin; return on assets; return on equity
Liquidity and working capitalCurrent ratio; quick ratio; inventory days; receivable days; payable days; cash cycle; cash conversion
Return on capital employedProfit before interest and tax ÷ (equity + long-term debt); split into margin × asset turnover
Financial structureDebt to equity; gearing; interest cover (times interest earned); debt service ability; dividend cover
Asset efficiencyAsset turnover; non-current asset turnover; revenue per outlet or per employee

Comparisons: with last year, with budget, with industry standard or competitors, between segments or revenue streams, and with a target company in an acquisition.

Using the analysis in the Case Study

1 Adjust
Read the accounting and compliance issues email and correct the draft: schedule with journals 2 Rebuild
Adjusted profit or loss and balance sheet (annexures) 3 Analyse
Mix, trend, ratios on adjusted figures; compare draft against adjusted 4 Add non-financial facts
Market, customers, operations, people, news 5 Judge and conclude
Is performance and position good, adequate or weak? What should the board do?
  • Compare draft and adjusted results: show how much profit, net assets and ratios change, and which adjustments matter most.
  • Link ratios to non-financial information: falling margin and a news clip about import duties; high receivables and customer credit terms; rising wages and labour unrest.
  • Spot management bias: the draft may overstate profit (to support an IPO or a loan). This is an ethical point as well.
  • Conclude on three things: performance, financial position, and the implication for the decisions in requirement (b) (borrowing capacity, IPO pricing).
Exam focus

Most past cases ask requirement (a) as: (i) a schedule of adjustments for the issues in Exhibit 5 and an evaluation of adjusted performance and position using non-financial information; (ii) a SWOT. See Chapters 7 and 8 for the layouts.

6

Spreadsheets and writing the commentary

Pre-populated spreadsheetsThe case may supply spreadsheets with revenue by channel, cost of sales by category, working capital and ratio tables already set up. Complete them, check the formulas, then use them: percentage change, mix and averages are quick ways to find the story.
Four-part commentFor every key movement write: what changed (number), why (cause from the case), so what (implication for the business), and action (what the board should do). Combine ratios that tell the same story.
Weak commentStrong comment
Revenue increased by 12%.Revenue rose 12%, but 9 points came from price rises after the duty change and volume grew only 3%, so growth may not be sustainable if customers resist higher prices.
Current ratio is 0.84.Current ratio of 0.84 means current liabilities exceed current assets; because the new plant was funded from short-term loans, refinancing risk is high and the bank will see little headroom.
