Past Paper Map: What Was Tested and What Comes Next

A map of all 13 ICAB Corporate Reporting sessions, from March–April 2022 to May 2026: how the paper is built, what each question tested, how often each topic came up, what has never been tested, and a study order based on that. It comes from the official question papers and suggested answers.

How to read this chapter

The session map and the counts are facts taken from the 13 papers (my own count from reading every question; a topic counts only if it carried marks). The "what may come next" parts are my judgement from the patterns, not an official forecast. ICAB can set anything in the syllabus, so use this to decide your order of study, not to skip topics.

1

How the paper is built

Every paper is 3 hours 30 minutes, 100 marks, five compulsory questions. Each question is a long scenario with several parts, so you must read carefully and keep strict time. The pattern has been very stable for four years:

QuestionWhat it usually isMarksTarget time
Q1Group consolidation (statement of financial position and profit or loss, with workings). In some sessions the first 5 marks are adjustments to the parent's own books, or the question carries a small audit part18–2045 min
Q2Accounting issues: several separate IFRS problems with calculations and journal entries, or a financial-analysis question after adjustments15–2040 min
Q3Financial analysis and report (ratios, comment, recommend), often joined to adjustments, ethics, a briefing note or a due-diligence view. Sometimes a second accounting-issues question16–2040 min
Q4Audit scenario: risks, procedures, opinion, ethics, plus a small technical piece (cyber, AML, quality control, expert, interim review)15–2040 min
Q5Audit and ethics scenario, very often a first-year audit, or a group of mini-cases with threats and safeguards15–2040 min
What this means

About 40–45 marks are numbers (consolidation, adjusted statements, ratios) and about 55–60 marks are written (audit, ethics, analysis comment, IFRS explanations). The numbers need proformas (Chapters 30–34). The written marks need answer banks (Chapter 35). Both reward a fixed layout, because markers give marks line by line.

Not yet tested, and what may come next

My judgement, not a forecast

Examiners tend to rotate twists and bring back topics after a few sessions. The lists below show where the gaps are. A gap is a reason to be ready, not a promise.

Consolidation: seen once or neverNever seen as an explicit requirement: deferred tax on fair value adjustments; part-disposal that keeps control (equity transaction, no profit or loss); contingent consideration remeasured to fair value through profit or loss; acquisition costs expensed; joint venture (IFRS 11) as a separate topic; downstream and upstream unrealised profit with an associate; consolidated cash flow; goodwill of a foreign subsidiary retranslated; sub-subsidiary in a pyramid. Seen only once or twice: bargain purchase (Ares, Nov 2023, gain already recorded), step acquisition, foreign subsidiary, goodwill impairment, equity-settled schemes in a subsidiary. Disposal has appeared in 5 sessions, so a discontinued-operation presentation (separate line in profit or loss, IFRS 5 comparatives) is a likely next step.
Accounting issues: due or rareStrong repeaters, expect again: IFRS 15 (principal vs agent, contract modification, licences, loyalty, variable consideration), IFRS 16 (modification, sale and leaseback, lessor), IAS 37, IFRS 2, IFRS 9 (expected credit loss, amortised cost, convertible). Not yet seen: IFRS 5 as its own question (held for sale, measurement and presentation), IAS 7 cash flow statement preparation, IAS 8 errors and policy change with restatement, IAS 40 and cost vs fair value, IFRS 7 and IFRS 8 disclosures, IAS 32 compound instruments in depth, hedge accounting, IAS 36 cash-generating unit allocation, IAS 10 as a question, IAS 38 website and AI costs. Rising: IFRS S1 and S2 (first seen May 2026) and sustainability assurance.
Audit: due or rareStrong repeaters: first-year audit, ethics, fraud, IT and cyber, KAM and modified opinions. Not yet seen as a stand-alone question: going concern (ISA 570; it appears only inside risk lists), accounting estimates (ISA 540), related parties (ISA 550, only inside risk lists), subsequent events (ISA 560), written representations (ISA 580), sampling and audit evidence, communicating deficiencies (ISA 265), NOCLAR (ISA 250), ISQM 1, data analytics and AI (touched in May 2026, likely to grow).

Scoring habits seen in the suggested answers

  • Answer in the order of the requirement and label each part (a), (b), (i). ICAB says different parts of one question must be answered in one place in order.
  • Show working notes and refer to them in the columns of the consolidation (a "Ref" column). Marks are given for each working even if the final figure is wrong.
  • State the rule in one line, then calculate, then give the journal. The official answers do exactly this for every accounting issue.
  • Journals matter: many questions ask for them directly (May 2026 gave 8 of 20 marks for journals only).
  • Ratios need comment. A table of ratios alone earns little. Every ratio needs: what changed, why, what it means, what to do.
  • Ethics answers need a named threat and a matching safeguard, not a general statement. Link to the fundamental principle.
  • Audit answers need a risk, the assertion affected, and a specific procedure, tied to facts in the scenario (numbers and names).
  • Use the format asked: memo, report, email, briefing note. The heading, to, from and subject lines are part of the marks in several sessions.
Suggested time plan for the 210 minutes

10 min read all questions and choose the order · Q1 45 · Q2 40 · Q3 40 · Q4 35 · Q5 35 · 5 min final check. Start with the question where you are strongest, but never leave a question blank: ICAB marks every attempted line.

Marks per minute is about 0.5. A 4-mark part deserves about 8 minutes. If you are stuck for 3 minutes on a number, write the method and move on.
