1

Current tax

Recognise unpaid tax for current and past periods as a liability; overpayments as an asset if recoverable. Measure at the amount expected to be paid, using rates and laws enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date. Recognise in P/L, except tax on items in OCI or equity (revaluations, prior period adjustments). Offset current tax assets and liabilities only with a legal right of set-off and intention to settle net. Uncertain tax treatments (IFRIC 23): assume the authority will examine; use the most likely amount or expected value.

Bangladesh point

Current tax in Bangladesh is now governed by the Income Tax Act 2023 (which replaced the Income Tax Ordinance 1984) and yearly Finance Acts. Tax depreciation follows the Act's schedule, which commonly differs from accounting depreciation, creating deferred tax. Minimum tax and final-settlement tax rules can make some tax unrelated to profit, which affects deferred tax assets. See the Taxation paper notes for current rates.

2

Deferred tax: the idea and the tax base

Deferred tax is not a tax the government charges. It recognises the future tax effect of recovering assets and settling liabilities at their carrying amounts, so the tax charge matches the accounting profit.
Permanent vs temporaryPermanent differences (fines, non-deductible entertainment, tax-free income) never reverse: no deferred tax. Temporary differences reverse in later periods (e.g. depreciation vs tax allowances).
Tax baseAsset: the amount deductible for tax against future taxable benefits (if the benefits are not taxable, tax base = carrying amount). Liability: carrying amount − amounts deductible in future. Revenue received in advance: carrying amount − amount not taxable in future.
SituationResult
Asset carrying amount > tax base (accelerated tax allowances, revaluation, development costs deducted when paid, accrued income taxed on receipt)Taxable difference → deferred tax liability
Asset carrying amount < tax base; liability carrying amount > tax base (warranty or other provisions deducted when paid, unused tax losses, intra-group unrealised profit)Deductible difference → deferred tax asset, if probable
3

Exceptions, measurement and recognition

Exceptions (no deferred tax)Initial recognition of goodwill (DTL); initial recognition of an asset or liability in a transaction that is not a business combination and affects neither accounting nor taxable profit (e.g. Petros buys an asset whose depreciation is never deductible: no DTL in any year). Since 2023, this exception does not apply where equal taxable and deductible differences arise (e.g. leases, decommissioning).
MeasurementRates expected when the difference reverses, based on laws enacted or substantively enacted at the year end. Reflect the manner of recovery (use vs sale rates; investment property at fair value is presumed recovered by sale). Not discounted.

Where it goes: P/L, unless the underlying item is in OCI (revaluations, pension remeasurements, FVOCI, cash flow hedges, FX translation) or equity (prior period adjustments, equity components of compound instruments), or arises in a business combination (goodwill).

Deferred tax assets and common scenarios

Recognise a DTA only to the extent that future taxable profit is probable: from reversing taxable differences (same entity, same tax authority, matching periods), forecast profits, or tax planning opportunities. A history of recent losses is strong evidence against. Reassess unrecognised DTAs every year. Pargatha: £10,000 warranty provision deductible when paid at 25% → DTA £2,500 if profits are probable.

Global minimum tax (Pillar Two)

The May 2023 IAS 12 amendment gives a mandatory temporary exception: no deferred tax is recognised or disclosed for the OECD Pillar Two global minimum top-up taxes, but groups must disclose that they use the exception and their current tax exposure. The workbook does not cover this; mention it as a current issue for large multinational groups.

6

Presentation, disclosure and audit

Presentation: deferred tax is always non-current. Offset DTAs and DTLs only if there is a legal right of set-off and they relate to the same tax authority and entity (or entities settling net). Disclosure: components of the tax expense (current, prior year adjustments, deferred from origination and reversal, rate changes, recognition of previously unrecognised losses); tax on OCI items; a tax rate reconciliation (accounting profit × applicable rate to actual charge, or effective rate to applicable rate); changes in rates; deferred tax by type of difference; unrecognised deductible differences and losses; and the evidence supporting a DTA when the entity has recent losses.

Audit risksLittle tax accounting knowledge at board level; foreign tax rules; complex or unusual transactions overlooked; spreadsheet-based tax workings; management bias (e.g. minimising a DTL to improve gearing; recognising DTAs on losses without credible forecasts); uncertain tax positions and transfer pricing disputes (Petrofac: a KAM on US$59m of uncertain tax liabilities).
ProceduresCurrent: prior and current computations; agree to the accounts; reasonable estimates; correct rates and allowances; open enquiries and correspondence with the tax authority; payments to bank. Deferred: check the deferred tax working's arithmetic; agree differences to the tax computation and accounts; challenge forecasts supporting DTAs; enacted rates; opening balance to last year; completeness (look for items that should create differences). Use tax specialists (ethical limits on tax services for PIE clients).
Exam focus

Deferred tax usually appears as an adjustment inside a bigger question: for each issue, give the carrying amount, tax base, difference, rate and deferred tax, and say whether it goes to P/L, OCI, equity or goodwill. Always check whether a DTA is supported by future profits, and flag management bias (understated DTL or optimistic DTA) and ethical pressure on the accountant.
