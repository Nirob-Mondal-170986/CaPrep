1

The audit process and planning standards

1Client acceptance and terms (ISA 210)
2Materiality and risk assessment (ISA 315, 320)
3Plan and design the approach (ISA 300)
4Evaluate and test internal control
5Tests for evidence (ISA 330, 500)
6Complete and evaluate
7Issue the auditor's report

This chapter covers stages 1–4; Chapter 6 covers evidence and Chapter 8 completion and reporting. The stages overlap in practice: almost any procedure gives evidence about risk, and the auditor keeps standing back to raise or lower audit effort as new evidence appears.

To plan, the auditor must: understand the business, its control environment and accounting system; assess the risk of material misstatement; set materiality; write an audit strategy (general approach); and write an audit plan (detailed procedures).

2

Professional scepticism and management challenge

Professional scepticism: an attitude that includes a questioning mind, being alert to conditions that may indicate misstatement due to error or fraud, and a critical assessment of audit evidence.

Be alert to: evidence that contradicts other evidence; information that questions the reliability of documents or answers; conditions that may indicate fraud; and circumstances that call for extra procedures. ISA 200: the auditor may accept documents as genuine unless there is reason to doubt them, and a belief that management is honest does not remove the need for scepticism (repeated in ISA 240).

Regulator case (UK FRC)What went wrong
Carillion / KPMG (fined £21m, 2023)Failed to challenge management's judgements and estimates, even when they looked unreasonable or biased
Eddie Stobart / PwC (£2m, 2023)Did not treat revenue on property deals as a significant risk; no consultation; no challenge of accounting policy
Stagecoach / EY (£2.2m, 2021)Did not evaluate and challenge management's experts and key assumptions
Mitie / Deloitte (£1.45m, 2022)Goodwill impairment flagged as a significant risk, but cash flows and discount rate not properly tested

FRC Professional Challenge Framework (2022): mindset; a professional judgement trigger and process; consultation; environmental factors. The FRC's "good environment" paper lists four enablers: learning, culture, audit firm operating model, and the wider ecosystem (including audit committees).

Worked example: Confront Ltd (tennis retailer)

Revenue £72.2m (£63.1m), cost of sales £41.1m (£44.8m), operating expenses £18.1m (£7.2m), operating profit £13.0m (£11.1m). PPE up £100m for a new IT system with a 5-year life; goodwill up a round £10m "for the move online"; cash fell from +£12.1m to −£1.5m while the loan rose only £22.7m. Management says "no change in the way we operate" and "focus on cost control".

Red flagChallenge
Gross margin 29% → 43% but operating margin flat at 18%; total costs still 82% of revenueCosts probably moved from cost of sales to operating expenses; "cost control" and "sales mix" claims look wrong
PPE up exactly £100m; no £20m depreciation (£100m ÷ 5) visibleIT system not depreciated; profit overstated
Goodwill up a round £10mArbitrary; goodwill cannot be revalued upward (IFRS 3 / IAS 38)
"No change in operations" vs a major move onlineInconsistent explanations
Financing does not add up; cash and gearing worseHow was £100m paid for? Possible going concern issue
3

Understanding the entity (ISA 315 Revised)

Objective: identify and assess risks of material misstatement, due to fraud or error, at the financial statement and assertion levels, as a basis for responses.

Risk assessment proceduresEnquiries of management and others (including internal audit) · analytical procedures · observation and inspection · team discussion of the framework and how the statements could be misstated (required on every audit) · prior-period knowledge (check it is still valid).
What to understandIndustry, regulatory and external factors · nature of the entity · the reporting framework and accounting policies (and why they changed) · how performance is measured · inherent risk factors · the system of internal control, including IT.

Steps: identify risks while gaining the understanding → decide whether they are pervasive (financial statement level) → link them to assertions → consider likelihood (including multiple misstatements) → consider whether they could be material.

Significant risk indicatorsBusiness processes and audit focus
Fraud · recent significant developments · subjectivity · unusual transactions · significant related party transactions · complexity. For a significant risk, evaluate the design and implementation of related controls.Financing (share issues, loans, bank support) · purchasing (purchases cycle, payables) · human resources (payroll, bonuses, commissions) · inventory (WIP, valuation, NRV) · revenue (sales cycle, receivables)
Context example: Ockey Ltd

Makes tableware to order for high-street stores and regularly produces 10% extra, which it cannot sell to anyone else. Some stores reject goods at quality checks.

Inventory valuation: surplus stock may be obsolete; up to 10% of a material balance. Look at review, scrapping and reworking of surplus. Losing customers: longer-term going concern risk; disputed sales could overstate revenue and receivables, but probably not material.

Scalability: ISA 315 applies to every entity. A small entity with weak controls can still be complex and risky, and a large entity with strong controls may be lower risk. Have no preconceptions.

4

Business risk and the business risk model

Business risk: the risk that an entity will not achieve its objectives.

Financial

Going concern, market, credit, interest rate, currency, overtrading, treasury risks.

Operating

Loss of orders or key staff, asset damage, brand, technology change, stock-outs.

Compliance

Company law, accounting standards, listing rules, tax, health and safety, environment, data protection.

The business risk model (top-down) starts from the client's objectives, strategy, risk management, industry and economy, and works down to the financial statements. Supporters say the big audit risks come from failed strategy more than from mis-posted transactions.

Effect on proceduresTests of controls focus on high-level controls (control environment, governance). Analytical procedures are used more heavily. Detailed testing is reduced but never removed.
Business risk → financial statementsFalling sales and margins → inventory NRV, going concern · customers wanting longer credit → receivables · product quality → inventory, returns, provisions · old IT → anywhere · contract labour → employment taxes, fines.
Interactive question: KidsStuff Ltd (toy importer)

Family business importing toys from one Far East supplier; poor recent results; relies on a large overdraft; son is moving into computer games and says results have improved.

Business riskAudit effect
Overdraft reliance, could be withdrawnGoing concern
Foreign currency purchasesPayables, purchases, inventory and FX gains/losses misstated
One distant supplier; long shippingLarge payable hard to confirm; cut-off
Fashion and seasonal marketObsolete inventory; inventory or receivables material depending on year end

ESG as business risk

Separate physical risks (weather, finite oil) from transition risks (cost of moving to clean energy, e.g. the motor industry switching to electric). Both can affect asset lives, impairment, provisions and going concern.

5

Audit risk: inherent, control and detection

Audit risk = risk of material misstatement (inherent risk × control risk) × detection risk
ComponentMeaning and key points
Financial statement levelPervasive risks: going concern and liquidity problems; control deficiencies that question management integrity; adverse economy needing new finance (pressure to manipulate); changes to IT systems
Inherent riskSusceptibility of an assertion to material misstatement before controls. ISA 315 factors: complexity, subjectivity, change, uncertainty, susceptibility to management bias or fraud. Assessed on a spectrum (likelihood × magnitude); the high end = significant risks
Control riskRisk that the entity's internal control will not prevent, or detect and correct, a material misstatement. Components: control environment; risk assessment process; monitoring; information system and communication; control activities
Detection riskRisk that the auditor's procedures miss a material misstatement. The only component the auditor controls, through the nature, timing and extent of procedures
Reliance strategy more likely whenOrders are electronic (EDI) with no paper trail · services are delivered and billed electronically (telecoms, ISPs) · testing for understatement.
Substantive strategy more likely whenNo controls exist · controls are ineffective · testing controls is inefficient · testing for overstatement. Some substantive work is always needed for each material area.

Control environment factors: integrity and ethical values; commitment to competence; participation by TCWG; management's philosophy; structure; reporting lines; HR policies. Events that raise control risk: new technology or systems, new key staff, regulatory change, rapid growth, restructuring, foreign expansion. ISA 315 also separates direct controls (precise enough for an assertion) from indirect controls (supporting).

Limits of internal control: human error and poor judgement; poor design or operation (e.g. ignoring exception reports); collusion; management override.

Interactive question: Forsythia (garden landscaping, new client)

Part of a complex group (Poppy Ltd, White Holdings) and trades with a sister garden centre. Mr Rose runs everything, does the payroll and is paid on commission plus large dividends. The ledgers are on his own PC; malware destroyed only the sales ledger just after the year end. The sales clerk is his daughter, who is on a low salary but has just bought a sports car. Extended credit is offered and old debts remain.

TypeAudit risks
InherentUndisclosed related party transactions and controlling party (IAS 24); group reporting; receivables recoverability with little evidence; fraud indicators (no segregation, extended credit, malware hitting only the sales ledger, lifestyle beyond salary); profit-driven management (commission and dividends)
ControlNo segregation over sales, purchases and payroll; weak PC security; a dominant key man who can override controls
DetectionFirst-year audit: opening balances and comparatives (ISA 510); limited knowledge of the client
Check the detail

The workbook still lists "limitations in internal control" as a sixth item in the ISA 315 (Revised 2019) appendix. The revised standard itself lists five components of internal control (environment, risk assessment, monitoring, information and communication, control activities); limitations are discussed separately. Answer with the five components.

ISA 315 AppendixWhat the auditor considers

IT systems audit looks at three areas: management policy (written, current, followed), segregation of duties (enforced by passwords and access rights) and security (physical, access and data, including firewalls).

Deepfake fraud (Arup, 2024)An employee transferred about £20m after a video call with AI-faked senior managers. Controls such as call-back verification and dual authorisation of payments must evolve.
Cryptoassets and blockchainA distributed ledger gives shared, permanent records, so auditors can read the chain directly but need the skills and tools. Risks: no specific IFRS on crypto, volatile valuation, unclear ownership, fraud and money laundering (FTX collapse, 2022).
7

Creative accounting

Creative accounting: manipulating accounting results to create an altered impression of financial position or performance, using the rules in a spirit other than intended.

Spectrum (most to least acceptable): normal policy choice → reasonable estimates → judgement on classifying costs → systematically choosing policies in one direction → choices at the edge of the rules → artificial transactions → fraud. Companies can also change the real transactions: deferring maintenance or R&D, timing asset sales, delaying investment.

OpportunitySubjectivity · complexity · weak governance · auditors who are not independent enough · imprecise rules · poor sources of evidence · weak penalties.
IncentivesIncome smoothing · hitting forecasts · boosting profit or share price · loan covenants and profit-related pay · directors' bonuses and options · tax · regulated industries · moving profit between divisions · losses · takeovers or raising finance.
TypeExamples
Overt (disclosed)Not depreciating; capitalising development costs; large provisions; changing depreciation policy
Covert (not disclosed): bigger share price impactTiming of revenue on long contracts; overhead allocation; capitalise-or-expense decisions
SustainableIncome smoothing at a normal level; off-balance-sheet finance
Unsustainable (reverses later)Capitalising expenses; bringing revenue forward

Red flags: operating cash flow out of line with profit; accounting profit far from taxable profit; many acquisitions; unusual year-end transactions; switching between cautious and aggressive policies; falling gross margin but rising net margin; inventory or receivables growing faster than sales; frequent policy or estimate changes; past estimates proved wrong; always meeting forecasts; pay tied to earnings; material related party transactions. Several flags together should put the auditor "on inquiry".

Techniques: under-providing in bad years and over-providing in good years; aggressive capitalisation; optimistic asset lives; premature or out-of-period revenue; recognising sales before delivery; misapplied cut-off; structuring deals to keep them off balance sheet. Example: Patisserie Valerie (2018), where cash was allegedly inflated and the company went into administration.

Materiality and performance materiality (ISA 320)

Material: omissions or misstatements that could, individually or together, influence users' economic decisions. Judged by size, nature and circumstances.
BenchmarkTypical %
Profit before tax / adjusted PBT~5%
Revenue~1%
Total assets1–2%
Equity1–2%
Lower materiality whenFirst-year audit · control deficiencies · past misstatements · fraud risk · management turnover · market pressure · tight loan covenants · results near a trend or break-even point. Items can be material by nature (directors' pay, related parties) or impact (turning a profit into a loss).

Three steps: (1) set overall materiality at planning; (2) set performance materiality below it, to cover the sum of small misstatements and undetected errors, overall or for specific balances; (3) at completion, add up known and likely misstatements and compare. Revise materiality if new information appears and document why. Also set a lower "clearly trivial" threshold for reporting to the audit committee. In a group, component materiality is always below group materiality.

Context example: performance materiality

Overall materiality £100,000. Misstatements found: (a) abortive R&D capitalised £41,000 (the only intangible); (b) PPE cut-off £50,000; (c) receivable from a liquidated customer £29,000.

Each is below £100,000, but together they total £120,000, which is material. A general performance materiality of £50,000 catches (b); a specific level of £40,000 for intangibles catches (a), which also matters by nature. After correcting (a) and (b), only £29,000 is left uncorrected.

Interactive questions: Albreda Ltd and Oscar Ltd

Albreda: revenue £42.2m, PBT £1.8m, total assets £30.7m; home-delivery sales of £0.6m are stopping, with a £0.2m redundancy provision. Oscar: PBT £10k (last year £300k), revenue £2.0m, assets £1.8m; a colleague set materiality at 5% × £10k = £500.

Albreda home delivery: 0.6 ÷ 42.21.4% of revenue: material
Albreda provision: 0.2 ÷ 30.70.65% of assets: not material
Albreda provision: 0.2 ÷ 1.811% of PBT: material
Oscar: £500 is far too lowUse revenue/assets or average profit

Adapt the benchmark to the users: post-tax profit where dividends drive value; profit before owners' pay in owner-managed businesses; assets in asset-driven entities; costs for charities. UK listed auditors disclose materiality in the auditor's report. Real examples: Barclays (KPMG) £275m at 3.9% of PBT; Tesco group (Deloitte) £100m at 4.64% of adjusted PBT; Sainsbury (EY) performance materiality at 75% of planning materiality.

Data analytics, RPA and AI in the audit

Data analytics collects and analyses large data sets to find patterns; data mining sorts data to find relationships. Common routines: last purchase vs last sale (cost vs NRV); inventory, receivables and payables ageing; revenue and margin by product or region (negative margins); order-to-cash and purchase-to-pay matching; "can do / did do" user access tests for segregation; depreciation recalculation; capex vs repairs; three-way matching.

Journal tests to target creative accounting

Which journals should data analytics pull out?

Credits to revenue where the debit is not receivables or cash. Debits to PPE where the credit is an expense account, which can mean costs are being capitalised. Investigate each and get management's explanation.

BenefitsVisual results that are easy to drill into · wide query options · usable by non-specialists · speed and scale, up to 100% of a population · supports scepticism (e.g. predictive tests of management's figures) · better targeted substantive work.
ChallengesExtracting and transforming client data (mapping bespoke systems) · data protection and confidentiality · quality assurance of the tools (pilot and parallel runs) · data retention · fit with ISAs: why test controls or sample if 100% can be checked? · exceptions may be set-up errors, so performance materiality still matters.

IAASB support material (2020–23) covers documenting automated tools and techniques (ATTs), using them in risk assessment and substantive analytics, the risk of over-reliance and hidden bias in algorithms, and investigating exceptions. The UK FRC (2020) found wide use of ATTs, mainly for journals, risk assessment and revenue, but still "no match for an experienced auditor's judgement".

RPA (e.g. RFID-tagged inventory counted remotely) streamlines processes but brings over-reliance and skills risks. AI and generative AI allow testing of whole populations and better fraud detection and forecasting, but need investment, struggle with unusual cases, and raise ethical and legal questions. Auditors must also audit AI-related assets (valuation, impairment).

Updates since the workbook

The workbook describes ISSA 5000 as an exposure draft. The IAASB issued the final ISSA 5000 in late 2024. The US SEC climate rules adopted in 2024 were challenged in court and the SEC stopped defending them in 2025. The EU has also delayed and narrowed parts of the CSRD. In the exam, explain the principles and note that requirements are changing.

Exam focus

Audit risk appears in every CR paper. Use the scenario: link each issue to a financial statement line and assertion, explain why it is a risk (inherent factor, control weakness, fraud incentive), and give a specific procedure. Calculate materiality where numbers are given and say whether each issue is material. Show scepticism: test management's explanations against the numbers (Confront Ltd). Practice: Forsythia, KidsStuff, Gates Ltd (revenue plus data analytics), Suttoner plc (business risks).
