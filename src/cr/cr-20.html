Control = power over the investee (current ability to direct relevant activities, even if not used) + exposure to variable returns + ability to use power to affect those returns.

Power can come from voting rights, substantive potential voting rights (in the money, exercisable), rights to appoint or remove key management, management contracts, or a large holding when the rest is widely spread. Returns include dividends, fees, credit support exposure and synergies.

Worked example: Twist, Copperfield, Scrooge

(1) Twist owns 40%; 12 others own 5% each; Twist has a contractual right (never used) to appoint and remove management. (2) Copperfield owns 45%; two others own 26% each. (3) Scrooge owns 70%; Marley owns 30% with a deeply out-of-the-money option over half of Scrooge's shares.

(1) Contractual right over management = power, even unusedTwist controls
(2) Two others together can outvote CopperfieldNo control
(3) Marley's option is not substantiveScrooge controls
Exemption from group accountsParent is a wholly owned (or partly owned, with no objection from others) subsidiary; its securities are not publicly traded or being issued; and its ultimate or intermediate parent publishes IFRS consolidated accounts. Separate accounts (IAS 27): subsidiaries at cost, IFRS 9 or equity method.
No exclusion just becauseA subsidiary is loss-making or in a different business: consolidate and explain with segment information. Exclusion (keeping debt off the group balance sheet) is a classic manipulation. Subsidiaries held for sale follow IFRS 5. Use uniform policies; reporting dates no more than 3 months apart, with adjustments.
Bangladesh point

Consolidation in Bangladesh is driven by IFRS 10 (adopted through the FRC) and BSEC rules for listed companies. The Companies Act 1994 itself focuses on attaching the subsidiaries' accounts to the holding company's balance sheet. Check the current FRC and BSEC requirements when discussing legal duties to consolidate.

IFRS 3: goodwill, consideration and NCI

Goodwill = consideration transferred (fair value) + NCI + fair value of any previously held interest − fair value of identifiable net assets. Negative → bargain purchase gain in P/L (after re-checking all values).
Consideration itemTreatment
Cash, shares issued, assets givenFair value at the acquisition date
Deferred cashPresent value
Contingent considerationFair value at acquisition, even if not probable. Cash or shares of a fixed value → liability, later changes from post-acquisition events → P/L. A fixed number of shares → equity, not remeasured. New information about conditions at the acquisition date (within 12 months) → adjust goodwill
Acquisition costs (legal, advisers)Expense; share issue costs deducted from equity; debt issue costs in the effective rate
NCI: share of net assetsGoodwill is the parent's share only ("partial goodwill"). For impairment testing, gross up goodwill for the NCI's notional share (Chapter 12).
NCI: fair value"Full goodwill" including NCI goodwill. Chosen deal by deal. The parent's goodwill share is usually more than its % holding because of the control premium. Impairment is shared in the profit-sharing ratio.
Worked examples: National, ABC

(1) National buys 80% of Locale for £25m; net assets £21m; fair value of the 20% NCI £5m. (2) ABC buys 90% of DEF (net assets £18m) for 3m shares at £7 plus 1m more shares in 2 years if revenue rises 10% (likely); fees £50,000; NCI at share of net assets.

(1) NCI at share: 25 + 4.2 − 21£8.2m
(1) NCI at fair value: 25 + 5 − 21 (NCI goodwill £0.8m)£9.0m
(2) Consideration 21m + contingent 1m × £7 = 28m (equity, fixed number of shares)£28m
(2) Goodwill: 28 + 1.8 − 18; fees expensed£11.8m

Net assets acquired: recognition and fair value

ItemRule
Intangibles not in the subsidiary's own books (brands, customer lists, licences)Recognise if identifiable (separable or contractual/legal)
Restructuring plans and future lossesNot liabilities unless the acquiree was already committed before acquisition (stops "big bath" provisions released later)
Contingent liabilities of the acquireeRecognised if a present obligation and fair value is reliable, even if outflow is not probable (an exception to IAS 37); later at the higher of IAS 37 amount and initial amount
Exceptions to fair valueDeferred tax (IAS 12), employee benefits (IAS 19), share-based payment (IFRS 2), held for sale (IFRS 5), indemnification assets (match the item), reacquired rights (remaining contract term)
Provisional valuesAdjust within the 12-month measurement period, retrospectively against goodwill; afterwards only error corrections (IAS 8)

IFRS 13 fair value: exit price in the principal (or most advantageous) market; highest and best use for non-financial assets (physically possible, legally allowed, financially feasible), even if the acquirer plans another use; market participant assumptions; Levels 1–3. PPE with no market: depreciated replacement cost.

Worked example: P Co and S Co (75%, NCI at fair value £17,000)

Cost £51,000; S's share capital £20,000, retained earnings £21,000 at acquisition and £41,000 now; non-current assets £23,000 above book value at acquisition, needing extra depreciation of £3,000 this year.

Net assets: acquisition 20 + 21 + 23 = 64,000; now 20 + 41 + 23 − 3 = 81,000Post-acq 17,000
Goodwill: 51,000 + 17,000 − 64,000£4,000
NCI: 25% × 81,000 + (17,000 − 25% × 64,000)£21,250
Group retained earnings: 96,000 + 75% × 17,000£108,750

Reverse acquisition: when a listed company issues so many shares that the target's owners control the combined group, the legal subsidiary is the accounting acquirer. Consideration = the shares the legal subsidiary would have had to issue to give the same split (e.g. 8,000 shares × £62 = £496,000).

W1 StructureHoldings, dates, mid-year acquisitions
W2 Net assetsYear end / acquisition / post-acquisition, with fair value adjustments, extra depreciation, sub's PURP
W3 GoodwillConsideration + NCI − net assets at acquisition − impairment
W4 NCINCI at acquisition + NCI% × post-acquisition (− NCI share of impairment if fair value method)
W5 ReservesParent + group % × post-acquisition − parent's PURP − group share of impairment
Intra-group itemsCancel intra-group balances (adjust for cash and goods in transit first), sales and purchases, dividends, and interest. Unrealised profit in inventory or PPE: remove in full; if the subsidiary sold, adjust its net assets (W2) so the NCI shares it; if the parent sold, adjust group reserves only.

Associates and joint arrangements

Equity method: cost + group share of post-acquisition profit and OCI − dividends received − impairment, including other long-term interests (loans, preference shares). Group share of profit after tax in P/L; share of OCI in OCI. Fair value adjustments and the group share of unrealised profit are removed. Stop at nil unless there are obligations to fund losses.

Worked example: P Co buys 25% of A Co for £60,000

A Co's profit £24,000; dividend paid £6,000.

P's own accounts: dividend income 25% × 6,000£1,500
Group P/L: 25% × 24,000£6,000
Group investment: 60,000 + 25% × (24,000 − 6,000)£64,500
PURP with an associateEntry (group share only)
Associate sells to group (goods in group inventory)Dr group retained earnings / Cr inventory
Group sells to associateDr group retained earnings / Cr investment in associate
Joint arrangements (IFRS 11)Need a contractual arrangement giving joint control (unanimous consent on relevant activities). No separate vehicle → always a joint operation. With a vehicle: look at legal form, contract terms and other facts. Rights to assets and obligations for liabilities (e.g. output taken by the parties) → joint operation; rights only to net assets → joint venture (equity method). Guarantees alone do not decide it.
Joint operationsRecognise own assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, plus share of joint ones, in both separate and group accounts (e.g. shared pipelines, jointly operated oil fields, aircraft shared between airlines). Joint venture sales: recognise profit only to the extent of the other parties' interest; recognise losses showing impairment in full.

IFRS 12 disclosures: significant judgements about control, joint control and influence; interests in subsidiaries (NCI, restrictions, risks from structured entities), joint arrangements and associates (summarised financial information, commitments); and unconsolidated structured entities (special purpose vehicles where voting rights are not the deciding factor).

Step acquisitions: crossing the control line

Cross the 50% (control) line → treat the old stake as sold and bought back at fair value: gain or loss in P/L (or OCI for an FVOCI equity investment) and goodwill calculated once, at the date of control. Do not cross the line → transaction between owners in equity; no gain, goodwill unchanged.
Worked examples: Bristol / Bath, Good / Will, Santander / Madrid

(1) Bristol holds 5% of Bath at FVOCI (carrying £230,000, fair value now £250,000) and buys 65% more for £3.9m; net assets £4m; NCI at share. (2) Good buys 20% of Will for $120m, then 60% a year later for $480m when net assets are $750m. (3) Santander owns 70% of Madrid (goodwill £415,000) and buys 10% more for £105,000 when net assets are £970,000.

(1) Revalue old stake: +£20,000 to OCI (FVOCI election)£20,000
(1) Goodwill: 3,900 + 1,200 + 250 − 4,000£1,350,000
(2) Old stake at fair value 480 × 20/60 = 160; gain 160 − 120 in P/L$40m gain
(2) Goodwill: 480 + 150 + 160 − 750$40m
(3) NCI falls by 10% × 970,000 = 97,000; Dr NCI 97,000, Dr equity 8,000, Cr cash 105,000No gain; goodwill £415,000

Disposals

ChangeGroup treatment
Subsidiary → subsidiary (e.g. 90% → 70%)Equity transaction: proceeds − increase in NCI → equity. No gain, goodwill unchanged. Consolidate all year; NCI time-apportioned
Subsidiary → associate, investment or nilLoss of control: gain = proceeds + fair value of retained stake − (net assets + goodwill − NCI). Retained stake starts at fair value. Consolidate to the disposal date, then equity method or IFRS 9. Recycle OCI as if the assets were sold directly (FX reserve to P/L; revaluation surplus to retained earnings)
Associate → investmentGain = proceeds + fair value retained − carrying amount of the associate
Parent's own accountsProceeds − cost of the shares sold
Worked examples: Kingdom, Allister, Express

(1) Kingdom sells all of 80%-owned Westville mid-year for £350,000; net assets at disposal £317,000 (after a £10,000 dividend not yet recorded); full goodwill £59,000; NCI at acquisition £67,000. (2) Allister sells two-thirds of 75%-owned Brown for £490,000; net assets £800,000; goodwill fully impaired; 25% retained worth £220,000. (3) Express sells 10% of 90%-owned Billings for £70,000 when NCI is £42,800 for 10%.

(1) 350,000 − (317,000 + 59,000 − 63,400 − 9,400)Gain £46,800
(2) 490,000 + 220,000 − (800,000 − 200,000)Gain £110,000
(2) Split: realised 490 − 400 = 90,000; holding gain 220 − 200 = 20,000£110,000
(3) Dr cash 70,000; Cr NCI 42,800; Cr equity 27,200 (no gain)Equity £27,200

If the subsidiary sold is a discontinued operation, show its profit to disposal plus the group gain as one line (IFRS 5).

Group audits (ISA 600) and global audits

The group engagement partner takes sole responsibility for the group opinion; the report does not refer to component auditors (unless law requires). At acceptance, judge whether enough evidence can be obtained on the consolidation and components (access to component management, information and auditors).

Component auditorsEvaluate: ethics and independence; competence (standards, resources, skills, IFRS knowledge); whether the group team can be involved enough; whether they work under active regulatory oversight. Use visits, questionnaires and confirmations. Send instructions: cooperation, ethics, component materiality and clearly trivial threshold, significant risks, related parties list, reporting deadlines.
They report backCompliance with ethics and instructions; the information reported on; non-compliance with laws; uncorrected misstatements; signs of management bias; significant control deficiencies; fraud; other significant matters and their overall findings. The group team reviews their work (key meetings, working papers) and decides on more procedures.
Consolidation audit steps
(1) Agree component figures to audited accounts · (2) review consolidation adjustments (acquisition dates and costs, goodwill, pre-acquisition reserves, policy alignment) · (3) acquisitions: correct classification, date, fair values (IFRS 13), goodwill and impairment · (4) disposals: date, results to disposal, gain · (5) existing investments still correctly classified (control, influence) · (6) recalculate, including checking the spreadsheet logic · (7) compliance: reporting dates, excluded subsidiaries, policies of foreign subsidiaries, intra-group eliminations, associates, FX, tax, restrictions on distributions · (8) overall review and subsequent events for all components up to the group report date

Intra-group balances: get a schedule of balances, agree to each component's records, investigate differences (cash or goods in transit, disputes). Mismatches signal errors and possible manipulation. Overseas subsidiaries: IAS 21 translation (closing rate for assets and liabilities including goodwill, average rate for income if stable), hyperinflation (IAS 29 first), political risk and control, currency restrictions on remitting profits, going concern, local tax; involve tax specialists.

Revised standard

The workbook's paragraph references (significant components, ISA 600.26–29) are from the old ISA 600. ISA 600 (Revised), effective for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2023, replaces "significant components" with a risk-based approach: the group team decides what work is needed where, based on assessed risks, and must be more involved in component auditors' work. Check the version adopted by the FRC in Bangladesh and describe the risk-based approach.

Global organisations: extra inherent risk (inflation, interest and exchange rates, currency controls, overseas financing, political instability), control risk (decentralised divisions, culture, different systems), compliance with many legal regimes. Transnational audits (relied on outside the home country, e.g. all listed entities) are covered by the Forum of Firms, whose members commit to ISAs, ISQM and global quality reviews.

Exam focus

Question 1 is consolidation every time (about 20 marks), usually with a twist: a mid-year or step acquisition, a disposal, contingent consideration, a fair value issue or an error in the client's draft. Show W1–W5 neatly, explain each adjustment in words, correct the draft figures, and then give the audit risks and procedures (goodwill, fair values, intra-group, component auditors).
