1

Scope, recognition and the effective interest method

Recognise when the entity becomes party to the contract (not on settlement). Outside IFRS 9: subsidiaries, associates and JVs; leases (mostly); employee benefits; own equity; insurance contracts; share-based payment; contracts to buy or sell non-financial items for the entity's own use (e.g. a cocoa order for production), unless they are settled net in cash.

Amortised cost = initial amount (fair value + transaction costs) + interest at the effective rate − cash received − loss allowance
Worked example: zero-coupon bond at amortised cost

Bought for £18,900 + broker fees £500; redeemable in 2 years at £22,000 (10% premium); effective rate 6.49%.

Initial: 18,900 + 500£19,400
Year 1 interest: 19,400 × 6.49%£1,259
Year 2 interest: 20,659 × 6.49%£1,341
Cash on redemption£22,000

Impact on ratios: classification decides whether gains and losses hit profit, OCI or nothing, and whether something is debt (gearing, interest cover). Watch for management choosing classifications to protect covenants or EPS.

Worked examples: partial sale, extinguishment, modification

(1) Westerly sells 25% of FVTPL bonds (carrying £106,250) for £37,500. (2) A £10m loan settled by handing over a property worth £7m. (3) Delta's £10m 10% loan (5 years left) is changed to no more interest and £17m at maturity; fees £500,000.

Check the rule

For changes under the 10% test, the workbook says the carrying amount is just "adjusted by the fees and amortised". Under IFRS 9 (as clarified in 2017), a modification that does not extinguish the liability requires the amortised cost to be recalculated as the new cash flows discounted at the original effective rate, with the difference recognised as a modification gain or loss in profit or loss. Fees then adjust the carrying amount and are spread.

Regular way purchases and sales may use trade-date or settlement-date accounting (applied consistently).

Liabilities at FVTPL: the change in fair value due to own credit risk goes to OCI (never recycled), unless that would create an accounting mismatch. This stops a struggling company reporting profit because its debt is worth less.

Expected credit losses (impairment)

Applies to debt at amortised cost and FVOCI, lease and trade receivables, contract assets, loan commitments and financial guarantees. Forward-looking: probability-weighted, time value, using reasonable and supportable information (including forecasts).

StageWhenAllowanceInterest on
1At recognition; no significant increase in credit risk12-month ECLGross amount
2Significant increase in credit risk (presumed if over 30 days past due)Lifetime ECLGross amount
3Credit-impaired: objective evidence (default presumed over 90 days)Lifetime ECLNet amount
Worked example: Dexter Lee loan of £500,000 at 4%

Lifetime losses if default £125,000; 12-month default probability 1% at start, 1.5% after year 1; significant deterioration in year 2 (lifetime ECL £50,000); default in year 3 (loss £125,000).

Start, stage 1: 1% × 125,000£1,250
Year 1, stage 1: 1.5% × 125,000 = 1,875 (charge 625); interest 20,000£1,875
Year 2, stage 2: lifetime £50,000 (charge 48,125); interest on gross 20,000£50,000
Year 3, stage 3: £125,000 (charge 75,000); interest 20,000£125,000
Year 4 interest on net: 4% × 375,000£15,000
Simplified approachTrade receivables and contract assets without a significant financing component: always lifetime ECL, often by a provision matrix (e.g. not due 1%, 1–30 days 5%…). Optional for those with financing components and for lease receivables. Kredco: £200,000 sale → 1% = £2,000; at the end of credit with news of the customer's cash problems → 5% = £10,000 (charge £8,000).
Other points12-month ECL = probability of default in 12 months × lifetime loss (Samipa: 25% × £14.4m = £3.6m). Assess groups of loans together using forward-looking data (Credito Bank: borrowers in a struggling clothing industry move to stage 2; collateral reduces the loss). Purchased or originated credit-impaired assets: no day-one allowance; credit-adjusted effective rate. Write off when no reasonable expectation of recovery.
Bangladesh point

Banks in Bangladesh classify and provide for loans under Bangladesh Bank rules (BRPD circulars), which differ from the IFRS 9 expected credit loss model. Bank financial statements therefore disclose departures from IFRS. Bangladesh Bank has been working towards ECL-based provisioning; check the latest circular before quoting the rules.

6

Derivatives, embedded derivatives and fair value

Derivative: value changes with an underlying (rate, price, index); little or no initial net investment; settled at a future date.
ExamplesForwards, futures, swaps, options. A currency swap exchanging equal fair values is a derivative (no net initial investment). Two offsetting fixed and floating loans with netting are, in substance, an interest rate swap. A steel purchase contract for own use is not a derivative, even if it could be net-settled, unless the entity has a practice of settling net.
AccountingAt FVTPL unless a hedging instrument (Chapter 17). Theta buys puts for £11,100; fair value rises to £13,500 then £15,000: gains of £2,400 and £1,500 in P/L; sold for £15,000.

Embedded derivatives: if the host is a financial asset within IFRS 9, classify the whole contract (it usually fails SPPI → FVTPL). Otherwise (financial liability or non-financial host) separate the derivative when its economic risks are not closely related to the host, a separate instrument with the same terms would be a derivative, and the whole contract is not at FVTPL. Not closely related: equity- or commodity-linked returns in a debt host, leverage, currency of neither party. Closely related: inflation-linked lease payments in the local currency, interest caps and floors out of the money at issue.

IFRS 13 for financial instruments: exit price from a market participant's view; Level 1 quoted prices, Level 2 observable inputs, Level 3 unobservable. Liabilities and own equity: assume transfer, not settlement; include non-performance (own credit) risk. Portfolio exception allows net measurement of groups managed on a net basis.

Exam focus

Recent CR papers give IFRS 9 7–20 marks: classify the asset (state both tests), measure it with an amortised cost table or fair value movement, compute ECL by stage, and say where each number goes (P/L or OCI). Then audit: valuation (Level 3 inputs, experts), ECL assumptions and staging (management bias), and disclosures (Chapter 17, topics 9–11).
