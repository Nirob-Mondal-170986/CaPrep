1

Scope and short-term benefits

Short-term

Due within 12 months: wages, paid leave, bonuses, benefits in kind.

Post-employment

Pensions, gratuity, retirement medical cover.

Other long-term

Long-service leave, sabbaticals, long-term disability.

Termination

Redundancy, voluntary severance.

Short-term benefits: expense when the service is given, undiscounted. Paid absences: accumulating (can be carried forward) → accrue the expected cost of unused entitlement at the year end, even if not vested; non-accumulating (sick leave that lapses) → expense only when taken. Profit-sharing and bonuses: accrue when there is a legal or constructive obligation (past practice counts) and a reliable estimate; allow for staff expected to leave.

Bangladesh point

Common local schemes: gratuity (usually an unfunded defined benefit plan, measured by actuarial valuation), recognised provident fund (a defined contribution plan), and the Workers' Profit Participation Fund under the Labour Act 2006 (5% of net profit, a short-term profit-sharing liability accrued in the year the profit is earned). Check the current Labour Act and Income Tax Act rules for amounts and recognition conditions.

2

Defined contribution vs defined benefit

Defined contributionThe entity pays fixed contributions and has no further obligation; the employee bears the investment and actuarial risk. Expense = contributions due for the period; accrue or prepay differences. Discount only if due beyond 12 months.
Defined benefitThe entity promises a benefit (e.g. a share of final salary), so it bears the risk. Recognise the net defined benefit liability (asset) = PV of obligation − fair value of plan assets, limited by the asset ceiling. Multi-employer and state plans follow their substance.

Measuring the obligation: the projected unit credit method treats each year of service as earning one unit of benefit, using actuarial assumptions (mortality, staff turnover, salary growth, benefit increases). Discount at the yield on high-quality corporate bonds (government bonds where there is no deep market) of matching currency and term. Plan assets at fair value, excluding unpaid contributions and non-transferable own instruments.

Other long-term and termination benefits, IAS 26 and disclosure

Other long-term benefitsMeasured like defined benefit, but all movements (including remeasurements) go to profit or loss; there is no OCI.
Termination benefitsRecognise at the earlier of when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer and when it recognises related IAS 37 restructuring costs. Voluntary schemes: when employees accept. Measure by the nature of the benefit; discount if over 12 months.

Defined benefit disclosures: characteristics and risks of the plan; reconciliations of obligation, assets and asset ceiling; split of assets by type; key actuarial assumptions and a sensitivity analysis; how the plan affects the amount, timing and uncertainty of future cash flows (funding policy, expected contributions, maturity profile). IAS 26 covers the reports of the retirement benefit plans themselves (not the employer's accounts): net assets available for benefits, plus the actuarial PV of promised benefits for defined benefit plans.

Real case: BHS (2016)

British Home Stores collapsed with a pension deficit of about £571m. Questions were raised about the earlier sale of the business for £1, the pension funding and the audit, showing how a large defined benefit deficit can drive going concern and governance issues.
