How ethics is tested here

Ethics appears in every paper, usually inside a reporting or audit scenario (your analysis: 4–10 marks per paper). At this level the examiner tests judgement, not recall of the Code: spot the issue, explain who is affected and which principle is threatened, weigh the options, and recommend action. There may be more than one acceptable answer. You may be asked to judge the ethics of management or of an accountant in business, not only of the auditor.

1

Why ethics matters

Business ethics covers how an organisation treats staff, customers, suppliers, shareholders, regulators and society. Accountants hold a position of public trust: their reports are relied on by people who cannot check them. Scandals such as Enron and WorldCom (where a junior accountant made false entries under pressure from senior managers) show how individuals get drawn into fraud through pressure, fear for their job and small steps.

Example: the individual under pressure (WorldCom)

A mid-level accountant is told by senior managers to move expenses into capital accounts to hit targets. She objects, but is told it is a one-off and that the company depends on it.

Factors: pressure from senior people, fear of losing her job, loyalty to colleagues, "everyone is doing it", being told it is temporary, no one to ask. Other actions she could take: refuse and put her concerns in writing; raise it with the audit committee or internal audit; use the whistleblowing line; seek advice from ICAB's ethics helpline or a lawyer; and as a last resort resign and report to the authorities.

Greenwashing and "sportswashing": using sport, sponsorship or vague claims to look ethical or green while practices do not change. Accountants must not be associated with misleading claims (integrity).

Ethical codes in Bangladesh

FRC Code = IESBA Code (2025 edition)The FRC adopted the International Code of Ethics including the International Independence Standards. It is principles-based: identify threats, evaluate them, and address them (remove the cause, apply safeguards, or decline). Parts: 1 (all accountants), 2 (accountants in business), 3 (accountants in practice), 4A (independence for audit and review), 4B (other assurance).
ICAB Code (bye-laws, Schedule C)Compliance-based list of professional misconduct for members in practice and in service. Following the FRC and ICAB Codes means following the IESBA Code. Applies to members, students and firms, paid or voluntary work.
Professional misconduct for a CA in practice (ICAB Schedule C, Part I)
Associating with unqualified persons in practice, or letting unauthorised people practise or sign · sharing fees with non-members · commissions or profit-sharing that harm independence
Accepting an audit improperly: without communicating with the previous auditor, without checking legal requirements are met, or by undercutting fees
Solicitation, advertising and self-promotion, including publishing client praise in a way that amounts to advertising
Certifying work not verified under the member's supervision; misleading reports; profit forecasts presented as certain; contingent fees unless allowed by law
Not disclosing a material interest in an entity reported on; not reporting a material misstatement known to them; gross negligence; not getting sufficient evidence; not following applicable standards
Breaching confidentiality; not keeping client money in a separate bank account; not cooperating with the ICAB Council; bringing the profession into disrepute
Correction to the Audit and Assurance notes

The IESBA Code allows honest advertising, but the ICAB bye-laws treat solicitation and advertising as professional misconduct for members in practice. For a Bangladesh exam answer on advertising, state the ICAB rule first, then the IESBA principle that marketing must not make exaggerated claims or disparage others.

General duty to report: under ICAB's bye-laws, a member must report to ICAB any facts suggesting a member or firm may be liable to disciplinary action, where it is in the public interest.

Practical significance

Finance staff often face pressure from non-finance colleagues to dress up short-term results. The examiner expects the accountant to resist, record concerns, use internal channels, and get help, not simply to comply.

Role and mindset, bias and technology

The IESBA decided that "professional scepticism" belongs to audit, but all accountants need a similar mindset: act in the public interest; strengthened objectivity and professional behaviour; integrity even when easier options exist; competence in technology; an inquiring mind (challenge others' views, reserve judgement until the facts are in); awareness of culture and bias.

AnchoringSticking to the first piece of information even when it proves wrong

ConfirmationFavouring information that supports what you already think
GroupthinkGoing along with the group instead of thinking independently
OverconfidenceOverestimating your own expertise
RepresentationDrawing conclusions from a sample that is not representative
Selective perceptionSeeing what you expect to see
IESBA Technology Initiative (final report November 2022; Code 2023)
Competence: all accountants, not only younger ones, must keep up with technology · Objectivity: bias built into algorithms or training data, over-reliance on systems, and "black box" AI that cannot be challenged · Independence: sharing insights from audit analytics with TCWG can blur audit and non-audit work (a management threat); keeping large amounts of client data; clients relying on the auditor's tools to produce their own information (self-review)

Ethics for auditors: NOCLAR, long association, appointments

NOCLAR (IESBA s.260 and s.360)

Objectives: comply with integrity and professional behaviour; alert management or TCWG so they can fix, remedy or reduce the effects, or prevent non-compliance that has not yet happened; take further action in the public interest.

Understand the matter (the law, the act, its effects) Address it: discuss with management and TCWG; check they act Decide whether further action is needed (report to an authority, withdraw) Act quickly on imminent breaches causing serious harm Document

Long association with PIE audits (IESBA s.540)

Maximum 7 cumulative years as engagement partner, EQR or other key audit partner. Cooling-off: engagement partner 5 consecutive years; EQR 3; other key audit partner 2.

A safeguard is an action that effectively reduces a threat to an acceptable level, judged by a reasonable and informed third party. Detailed independence rules (fees, gifts, financial interests, employment, non-audit services) are in Audit and Assurance Chapter 4.

Professional appointment and successor auditors

  • Consider threats before accepting: client integrity (check governance) and competence (understand the work).
  • Research the client and, with the client's consent, contact the existing auditor; keep an open mind if they do not reply, and get information other ways. Avoid tipping off if money laundering is suspected.
  • Bangladesh: the Companies Act 1994 does not require the predecessor to give the successor access to working papers (unlike the UK Companies Act 2006). Good practice: request after appointment, be specific (for ISA 510, 710 and 300); the predecessor explains orally or in writing; the successor may take notes; copying of extracts is reasonable; this is not a breach of confidentiality. Copy the letter to the client.
Example: Stewart Brice (Ethics Partner)

(a) Audit manager Teresa's husband inherits 1,000 shares in Recreate, a listed audit client, a week into the audit, and wants to keep them. (b) The firm's pension scheme, run by an unconnected company, holds shares in Tadpole Group; the firm is tendering for the audit of a Tadpole subsidiary. (c) Kripps Bros' constitution requires its auditor to own a token £1 share.

(a) A direct financial interest held by an immediate family member of a team member is not allowed. The shares were inherited (not chosen), so dispose of them as soon as practicable, or remove Teresa from the audit; have her work reviewed; record it. (b) An indirect interest through a pension scheme the firm does not control is generally acceptable if immaterial; check materiality and that the firm has no say in investment decisions. (c) Any direct financial interest, however small, is not allowed. Ask Kripps to change its constitution; otherwise the firm cannot continue as auditor.

Making ethical judgements: the resolution framework

Where the client's interests and the public interest conflict, the public interest comes first. Use this framework (ICAEW help sheet) to structure answers:

StepQuestions
1. Relevant factsDo I have all the facts, or am I assuming? Is it my problem? Check policies, codes and history; talk to trusted colleagues.
2. Relevant partiesWho is affected (shareholders, staff, lenders, the public) and how? Any conflicts between them? Who are my allies?
3. Ethical issuesProfessional, organisational and personal issues; effect on the profession and the public interest
4. Principles and threatsWhich principles are at risk? Which threats? Are there safeguards? Is any threat clearly insignificant?
5. Internal proceduresEscalate: immediate superior → next level → audit committee or board → legal, internal audit, HR; whistleblowing line. External help (ICAB) if needed, but internal resolution is preferred.
6. Courses of actionEvaluate the consequences of each against policies, law and society's values. Would it stand the test of time, and scrutiny by peers, family and friends? Record your reasoning.
7. Last resortIf still unresolved: legal advice, withdraw from the engagement, or resign
Example: Bellevue Ltd (revenue and a covenant)

A venture capital loan requires interest cover of at least 2. 70% of revenue is 3-year subscriptions; 30% is short consultancy billed on completion. Last year management reclassified 40% of subscription revenue as "consultancy", recognising it up front. The audit manager objected, but the partner, James Cowell, agreed and later left the firm abruptly. Interest cover: 2.02 (20X7) and 2.01 (20X8). The same reclassification is made again this year.

IssueResponse
Revenue: subscription services are performance obligations satisfied over time (IFRS 15); relabelling them to recognise up front looks like earnings managementTest the contracts: is there a distinct consultancy service delivered at the start? If not, recognise over 3 years
Covenant only just met (2.02, 2.01): strong incentive to inflate profit; without the change the covenant is probably breached, with going concern and disclosure effectsRecalculate cover without the reclassification; review loan terms and consequences of breach; going concern
Last year's accounts may contain a material errorIAS 8 restatement; effect on comparatives and last year's opinion (ISA 710)
Previous partner overruled the manager and left abruptly: possible integrity or independence issue, poor quality managementConsult the ethics and quality partner; review last year's file; consider an EQR; consider reporting to ICAB if misconduct is suspected
Pressure from a dominant MD (intimidation); possible fraudulent reportingScepticism; senior team; tell TCWG; if not corrected, modify the opinion; consider whether the lender is owed a duty and legal advice
