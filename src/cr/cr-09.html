IAS 1 presentation and materiality

Owner vs non-owner changesOwner changes (share issues, dividends) go in the statement of changes in equity. Non-owner changes = total comprehensive income = profit or loss + OCI, in the statement of profit or loss and OCI (one statement or two).
ComparativesAt least one prior period. A third balance sheet (start of the earliest comparative period) is needed when a policy is applied retrospectively, or items are restated or reclassified.
Other comprehensive incomeRecycled to profit or loss later?
Revaluation surplus (IAS 16/38); defined benefit remeasurements (IAS 19); equity investments at FVOCI (IFRS 9); share of associates' revaluationsNever reclassified
Foreign operation translation differences (IAS 21); effective part of cash flow hedges (IFRS 9)May be reclassified

Present OCI in these two groups, with the tax on each shown (either net of tax or with one tax line per group). The split is not realised vs unrealised. Show profit and total comprehensive income split between owners of the parent and NCI.

Material: information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions of primary users.

Practice Statement 2 (non-mandatory) four steps: identify potentially material information → assess (quantitative and qualitative) → organise clearly in the draft → review as a whole. Too much immaterial detail can hide what matters.

IFRS 18 (from 1 January 2027)

IFRS 18 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements replaces IAS 1. Most of IAS 1 carries over, but profit or loss gets three categories, operating, investing and financing, with two new subtotals: operating profit and profit before financing and income tax. Management-defined performance measures (e.g. "adjusted EBITDA" used in public communications) must be reconciled in the notes. Expenses by function need a note of depreciation, amortisation, staff costs, impairment and inventory write-downs. The workbook treats it as a current issue only. Check when the FRC adopts it in Bangladesh.

Operating segments (IFRS 8)

Applies to entities whose debt or equity is publicly traded (or in the process of issue). It uses the management approach: segments are what the chief operating decision maker (a function, not a job title) reviews.

Operating segment: a component that earns revenue and incurs expenses, whose results are regularly reviewed by the CODM to allocate resources and assess performance, and for which discrete financial information is available.
Reportable if any one is 10% or moreRevenue (external + internal) of total segment revenue · absolute profit or loss of the greater of total profits of profitable segments and total losses of loss-making segments · assets of total segment assets. Then check reportable segments cover at least 75% of external revenue; if not, add more.
AggregationSimilar economic characteristics (e.g. similar long-term margins) and similar products, production process, customers, distribution and regulation. Small segments may combine if they share a majority of these. The rest go in "all other segments". Disclose profit or loss, assets and liabilities if reported to the CODM, reconciliations to totals, and entity-wide data (products, geography, customers over 10% of revenue).

Held for sale and discontinued operations (IFRS 5)

Held for sale criteriaCarrying amount recovered mainly through sale · available for immediate sale in its present condition · sale highly probable: management committed, active search for a buyer, marketed at a reasonable price, expected within 1 year, plan unlikely to change. Delays beyond a year are allowed only if outside the entity's control. A subsidiary bought only to resell: sale within a year and other criteria met within about 3 months. Assets to be abandoned are never held for sale.
MeasurementLower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell. Remeasure under the asset's own standard just before reclassifying (e.g. revalue). Write-down → impairment loss in profit or loss. Stop depreciating. If no longer held for sale: lower of the carrying amount as if never classified (with depreciation caught up) and recoverable amount.
Discontinued operation: a component disposed of or held for sale that is a separate major line of business or geographical area (or part of one plan to dispose of one), or a subsidiary bought only to resell.

Presentation: one line in profit or loss for post-tax profit or loss of the discontinued operation plus the post-tax gain or loss on remeasurement or disposal; an analysis (revenue, expenses, tax, gain or loss) on the face or in the notes; net operating, investing and financing cash flows. Comparatives are re-presented. Held-for-sale assets and liabilities are shown separately on the balance sheet (not offset).

Interactive questions: facility, steel works and Robinson Ltd

(1) A factory to be sold, but only after a backlog of orders is finished in spring. (2) A steel works (10% of revenue) being closed gradually, finishing in July next year. (3) Robinson: machinery used until end of January for remaining orders; land (revaluation model) carried at £180,000, with fair value £210,000 and selling costs £6,000 at classification.

Audit procedures: management's intentions (enquiry and written representations); board minutes for a firm plan; estate agent contract and sale particulars; asking price vs fair value; ask the agent if completion within a year is likely; recompute fair value less costs to sell.

Related parties: IAS 24 and the audit (ISA 550)

Related to the reporting entity: a person (or close family member) who controls, jointly controls or has significant influence over it, or is key management personnel (KMP) of it or its parent; and entities in the same group, associates and joint ventures (of the entity or group members), post-employment benefit plans, entities controlled by a related person, and entities providing KMP services. Substance over form.

Not related just because ofA common director · sharing joint control of a joint venture · normal dealings with lenders, unions, utilities or government · economic dependence on a big customer, supplier, franchisor or distributor.
DiscloseParent and ultimate controlling party (always, even with no transactions); KMP pay in total and by type (short-term, post-employment, other long-term, termination, share-based); nature of each relationship, amounts, balances, terms, guarantees and bad debt provisions. No materiality relief by size in practice: nature makes RPTs sensitive.
Interactive question: KMP pay in subsidiary S

S pays its KMP £500,000 for services to S; P pays S's KMP £60,000 for services to S; S pays its KMP £20,000 for services to P; group pension £50,000 and options £28,000 for services to S.

S discloses all pay for services to S, whoever pays: 500,000 + 60,000 + 50,000 + 28,000 = £638,000. Exclude the £20,000 for services to P.

Audit of related partiesKey points
Why riskyNon-arm's-length prices distort profit and assets; "third-party" evidence may really come from a related party; unusual motives; fraud (common in scandals). Harder when there is no charge, disclosure is sensitive, no system exists to track RPTs, old balances remain, or the group is complex
Risk assessmentTeam discussion of fraud risk; ask management about related parties, changes, transactions and controls to identify, approve and disclose them; stay alert when reading bank and legal confirmations and minutes; ask about significant transactions outside the normal course of business
Finding undisclosed partiesPrior-year files; minutes and register of directors' interests; large or unusual year-end transactions; loan and bank confirmations (guarantors); investment deals; pension trusts; directors' other roles; shareholder register; other or predecessor auditors; tax and regulatory returns; legal invoices
ResponsesConfirm terms with the related party or intermediaries (banks, lawyers); inspect contracts and approval; check "arm's length" claims; written representations that all related parties and transactions are disclosed

First-time adoption (IFRS 1)

Goal: first IFRS statements that are transparent, comparable across all periods shown, a good starting point, and not too costly. Prepare an opening IFRS balance sheet at the date of transition (start of the earliest comparative period) using the IFRSs in force at the first reporting date, applied retrospectively, except for optional exemptions and mandatory exceptions.

Opening balance sheetRecognise all assets and liabilities IFRS requires; remove those it does not allow; reclassify; measure under IFRS. Adjustments go to retained earnings (or another equity category).
EstimatesMust be consistent with previous GAAP estimates (after policy differences) unless they were in error. New information found later is not used with hindsight; it is treated as a later event.
Interactive question: Europa (first IFRS year ends 31 Dec 20X8)

When is the transition date, and what about a court case settled after a previous-GAAP report?

With one year of comparatives, the transition date is 1 January 20X7. Reconcile equity and comprehensive income from previous GAAP to IFRS. For the court case, use information available when the earlier statements were approved: if a provision was probable then, recognise it under IFRS for that period.

Practical steps: project team and timetable; staff training; identify GAAP differences; change systems to capture new data (e.g. fair values, leases); dual reporting during transition; talk to auditors, lenders (covenants) and investors early. Audit: if comparative financial statements are presented, the opinion covers both periods (ISA 710) and representations are needed for both; check restated comparatives and consistent policies.

Interim reporting (IAS 34) and IFRS 14

Minimum content: condensed balance sheet, profit or loss and OCI, changes in equity and cash flows, plus selected notes (same policies, seasonality, unusual items, changes in estimates, debt and equity issues, dividends, segments, subsequent events, changes in the group, fair value disclosures). Periods: interim balance sheet vs last year end; profit or loss for the interim and year to date vs the same periods last year.

Same recognition and measurement as at year end, on a "year-to-date" basis (each interim stands alone). Do not smooth or anticipate.
ItemInterim treatment
Seasonal or occasional revenue; uneven costsNot anticipated or deferred; recognise when they occur (unless they would be deferred at year end too)
Year-end bonusAccrue if there is a legal or constructive obligation and it can be measured
Development costsExpensed costs cannot later be capitalised; do not defer hoping for an asset
DepreciationOnly on assets already held
InventoryLower of cost and NRV at the interim date (not expected year-end NRV)
Income taxInterim profit × expected average annual rate
Worked examples

(1) Bonus of 10% of salary if annual output exceeds 1m units; budget 1.4m and on track; half-year salaries £45m. (2) Half-year profit £6m, second half £10m; tax 25% if annual profit ≤ £10m, 30% on all profit if above. (3) Alshain: H1 £4m, H2 £8m; 25% below £11m, 30% above. (4) 100,000 units, cost £1.40, NRV £1.20 now and £1.55 expected at year end.

(1) Bonus probable: 10% × £45m£4.5m
(2) Annual £16m → 30%: 30% × £6m£1.8m
(3) Annual £12m → 30%: 30% × £4m£1.2m
(4) Use NRV at interim date: 100,000 × £1.20£120,000

IFRS 14 Regulatory Deferral Accounts (optional, first-time adopters only): lets a rate-regulated entity (e.g. utilities with regulated prices) keep its previous-GAAP regulatory assets and liabilities, presented as separate line items, with disclosure of the regulation and its effects. The IASB is working on a full replacement standard.

Exam focus

Discontinued operations, held for sale and related parties are frequent mini-issues inside the long case: state the rule → apply to the facts → give the numbers → give the audit procedures. IFRS 5 traps: closure is not a sale; "available for immediate sale" fails while the asset is still in use; remeasure before classifying. Related parties: always check for directors' family companies and non-arm's-length pricing.
