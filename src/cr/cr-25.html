Assurance and Related Services

Assurance engagements under ISAE 3000 (Revised) and the subject-specific ISAEs (3402 service organisations, 3410 greenhouse gas, 3420 prospectus pro forma); reviews of financial statements (ISRE 2400) and interim information (ISRE 2410); due diligence; prospective financial information (ISAE 3400); agreed-upon procedures (ISRS 4400); compilations (ISRS 4410); and forensic audit.

1

Assurance engagements (ISAE 3000 Revised)

Assurance engagement: a practitioner obtains evidence to express a conclusion designed to increase the confidence of intended users about the outcome of measuring or evaluating a subject matter against criteria.

Three parties

Practitioner, responsible party, intended users.

Subject matter

e.g. KPIs, controls, sustainability data, compliance.

Suitable criteria

Relevant, complete, reliable, neutral, understandable.

Evidence

Sufficient and appropriate.

Written report

Conclusion in the right form.

Reasonable assuranceLimited assurance
Risk reduced toAcceptably low (like an audit)Acceptable but higher; fewer procedures, mainly enquiry and analytics
ConclusionPositive: "in our opinion … presented fairly in accordance with [criteria]"Negative: "nothing has come to our attention that causes us to believe … not … "

Types: attestation (the responsible party measures and presents the information; practitioner concludes on it) vs direct (practitioner measures or evaluates it). Acceptance: ethics and independence, competence, preconditions (appropriate subject matter, suitable criteria, access to evidence, rational purpose, a meaningful level of assurance). Report: title, addressee, subject matter and criteria, responsibilities of each party, standard used, ethics and quality management statements, summary of work (especially for limited assurance), conclusion, signature, date and place.

Interactive question pattern: assurance on risk management for investors

Investors want assurance on a company's risk management process before investing. What do you consider before accepting?

Why: independent comfort reduces information risk. Elements: practitioner, management (responsible party), investors (users), the risk management process (subject matter), a framework such as COSO (criteria). Acceptance: independence (no conflicting services), competence, suitable criteria, access, what level of assurance is meaningful, engagement letter with restricted use and liability limits.

3

Reviews of financial statements (ISRE 2400 and 2410)

ISRE 2400 (Revised): review by a non-auditorLimited assurance. Quality: partner competence, team direction and review. Acceptance: preconditions, rational purpose, no scope limit making it pointless. Materiality as for an audit. Procedures: mainly enquiry and analytical procedures, plus specific work on related parties, fraud and non-compliance, going concern and subsequent events. More work if something looks misstated. Conclusion: unmodified ("nothing has come to our attention…"), qualified, adverse, or disclaimer.
ISRE 2410: interim review by the entity's own auditorThe auditor already knows the entity from the audit, so the review builds on that. Enquiries, analytics, reading minutes, comparing with prior interim and annual figures; considers going concern. Limited assurance conclusion; published with the interim report where required (e.g. listed companies).

Review vs audit: no tests of controls or detailed substantive testing unless needed; lower assurance; cheaper and faster. A review does not have to uncover fraud (CD Sales case: the auditor's review report on interim figures gave only limited assurance, but a confidentiality agreement that limited scope was a red flag).

4

Due diligence

A review, usually for a buyer or lender, to confirm the information a bid is based on, assess the target independently, identify and quantify risks, support financing and help value the target. Caveat emptor: the seller usually has no general duty to disclose, so the buyer relies on its own investigation, warranties and indemnities. Poor due diligence can lead to claims against the advisers and the directors.

TypeCovers
FinancialQuality of earnings (normalised profit), net assets, working capital needs, debt and debt-like items, cash flow, forecasts, accounting policies; not a statutory audit (set by contract, specific purpose, focused on risk and value)
CommercialMarket, competitors, customers, strategy, synergies
Operational and ITSystems, processes, capacity, cyber security
LegalContracts, title, litigation, change of control clauses, regulation
TaxExposures, structuring, losses
Human resourcesKey people, pensions, employment terms
Environmental and ESGContamination, compliance, climate exposure

Ethics: independence if the target or buyer is an audit client (self-review, advocacy); confidentiality; conflicts of interest (acting for more than one bidder). Report: findings, key risks, price-affecting items, matters for warranties, limitations of scope.

5

Prospective financial information (ISAE 3400)

PFI: forecasts (best-estimate assumptions) and projections (hypothetical assumptions). Agree terms and consider the purpose, users, period covered and the nature of assumptions. Do not accept if assumptions are clearly unrealistic or the PFI is unsuitable for its use.

ProceduresUnderstand the business and the process for preparing PFI; past accuracy of forecasts; check assumptions are consistent and supported (orders, market data, capacity, financing); recalculate; check consistency with historical figures and policies; sensitivity; written representations on assumptions.
ReportNegative assurance that the assumptions provide a reasonable basis; opinion that the PFI is properly prepared on those assumptions and presented under the framework; caveat that actual results may differ. Plus purpose or restrictions, management's responsibility, date when procedures were completed.

WhatPerform procedures agreed with the engaging party and report factual findings (e.g. checking grant expenditure is eligible; observing destruction of goods)Use accounting expertise to help management prepare and present financial information under a framework

Procedures in an AUP must be clear and objective (no "review for reasonableness" wording); use experts if needed and evaluate their work. If the compiled information is misleading and management will not amend it, withdraw.

7

Forensic audit

Forensic auditing: gathering, analysing and reporting on data in a predefined context to find facts or evidence in financial or legal disputes or irregularities, and advising on prevention.
UsesFraud (quantify losses, find bribes, misstatements, misrepresentation to auditors); negligence claims against auditors; insurance claims (fire, flood, theft losses); shareholder, partnership, contract, sale and matrimonial disputes; terrorist financing and money laundering; acting as an expert witness.
Procedures and evidenceClear terms and scope with lawyers; preserve evidence (chain of custody, forensic copies of data); interviews; document examination; data analytics and email review; trace assets and transaction trails; quantify loss; report clearly so it can be used in court. Evidence must meet legal standards.

Ethics: independence and objectivity (an expert witness owes a duty to the court); self-review if investigating an audit client's matters the firm audited; confidentiality; competence (legal procedure); conflicts of interest; fee basis (no contingent fees for expert evidence). In Bangladesh, money laundering suspicions must be reported to BFIU without tipping off (MLPA 2012).

Exam focus

Typical questions: "what type of engagement is this and what assurance can be given?", "matters to consider before accepting", "procedures", and "draft the conclusion paragraph". Always identify the subject matter, criteria and users, the level of assurance (reasonable, limited, none), the ethical threats (especially if the client is also an audit client), and the right report wording.
