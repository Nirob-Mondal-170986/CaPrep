The regulatory framework for IFRS

Monitoring Board: public authorities oversee the Trustees IFRS Foundation Trustees: governance, funding, appoint board members IASB sets IFRS Accounting Standards · ISSB sets sustainability standards IFRS Interpretations Committee: interpretations (IFRIC) · Advisory Council: advice

Aims: one set of high-quality, understandable, enforceable global standards giving transparent, comparable information for capital markets; promote their use, considering small entities and emerging economies; converge with national standards.

Due process for a new standard
Research and agenda decision → discussion paper (sometimes) → exposure draft with basis for conclusions, public comment → consider comments, re-deliberate → issue the standard (with effective date and transition) → post-implementation review

Scope and authority: IFRS apply to general purpose financial statements of profit-oriented entities; they do not apply to immaterial items and do not override local law, but entities must disclose whether they comply. In Bangladesh IFRS apply through the Companies Act 1994 and the Financial Reporting Act 2015. Standards limit choice, which improves comparability and reduces manipulation.

Exam basis

Questions are based on the Required Standards (standards mandatory on 1 January 2024). The Issued Standards also include standards issued but not yet effective; you need awareness of these as current issues (for example IFRS 18 on presentation and IFRS 19 for subsidiaries without public accountability).

The Conceptual Framework (2018)

The Framework is not a standard and does not override any IFRS. It helps the IASB develop standards, helps preparers choose policies where no standard applies, and helps everyone understand standards.

ChapterKey points
1 ObjectiveUseful information for existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in deciding about providing resources, including assessing management's stewardship. Shows resources and claims, changes in them, and how efficiently management used them. Accrual accounting, supported by cash flow information.
2 Qualitative characteristicsFundamental: relevance (predictive and confirmatory value; materiality) and faithful representation (complete, neutral, free from error; neutrality supported by prudence, meaning caution under uncertainty; substance over form). Enhancing: comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability. Constraint: cost.
3 Statements and reporting entityPrepared for a period, with comparatives, on a going concern basis. A reporting entity may be one entity, part of one, or several (consolidated, unconsolidated, combined).
4 ElementsAsset: a present economic resource controlled as a result of past events; a resource is a right that can produce economic benefits. Liability: a present obligation to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events; an obligation is a duty the entity has no practical ability to avoid. Equity: residual interest. Income / expenses: increases / decreases in assets or liabilities, other than contributions from or distributions to equity holders.
5 Recognition and derecognitionRecognise if the item meets an element definition and gives relevant information and a faithful representation (consider existence uncertainty, low probability of flows, measurement uncertainty). Derecognise when control of the asset is lost or the obligation no longer exists; consider retained parts.
6 MeasurementHistorical cost or current value: fair value, value in use (assets) or fulfilment value (liabilities), and current cost. Choose considering relevance, faithful representation and cost.
7 Presentation and disclosureCommunicate effectively: classification, aggregation, offsetting rules. Profit or loss is the primary performance measure; OCI is used only when it makes P/L more relevant, and is normally recycled later.
8 Capital maintenanceFinancial capital (money or constant purchasing power): profit is the increase in net assets in money terms. Physical capital (operating capability): profit only after maintaining productive capacity, usually with current cost.
Trap: asset or not?

Ask three questions: is there a right? Can it produce economic benefits (even with low probability)? Does the entity control it (can direct its use and stop others)? Staff skills, for example, are not controlled, so not an asset. Then consider whether recognition gives useful information.

Smaller entities: reporting and auditing

Most entities are small, owner-managed businesses with no outside shareholders to protect. Large and listed companies have distant investors who need more protection. This is the big GAAP / little GAAP divide.

Differential reportingA separate, simpler standard (the IFRS for SMEs): fewer topics, simpler recognition and measurement, far fewer disclosures. Drawback: less comparability with larger companies, and problems when an entity grows out of the "small" category.
Exemptions from full IFRSSome standards do not matter to small companies: an unlisted company need not apply IAS 33 (EPS), and IFRS 8 (segments) applies only to listed entities.

Auditing smaller entities: "an audit is an audit", so there are no separate ISAs for small entities. Newer ISAs use scalability: apply requirements in proportion to the complexity of the risks, not size alone.

IFRS 13 Fair Value Measurement

Fair value = the price that would be received to sell an asset, or paid to transfer a liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date (an exit price).
ConceptRule
ScopeApplies when another IFRS requires or allows fair value. Excludes IFRS 2 share-based payment, IFRS 16 leases, and measures like NRV (IAS 2) and value in use (IAS 36).
MarketUse the principal market (most volume); if none, the most advantageous market (best net amount after transaction and transport costs). Transaction costs are not deducted from fair value; transport costs are.
Market participantsIndependent, knowledgeable, able and willing to trade. Use their assumptions, not the entity's own intentions.
Non-financial assetsHighest and best use by market participants: physically possible, legally allowed and financially feasible, even if the entity uses it differently
LiabilitiesAssume the liability is transferred, not settled; include non-performance risk (own credit risk)
TechniquesMarket approach (prices of identical or similar items), cost approach (current replacement cost), income approach (discounted future amounts). Maximise observable inputs.
LevelInputsExample
1Quoted prices in active markets for identical itemsListed share price on DSE
2Other observable inputs, directly or indirectlyPrice of similar properties, interest rate curves
3Unobservable inputs (entity's own data)Valuation of unlisted shares using forecast cash flows

Disclosure: level in the hierarchy, techniques and inputs, transfers between levels, and for Level 3 a reconciliation and sensitivity information. Business combinations: IFRS 3 measures identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value under IFRS 13.

Example: principal vs most advantageous market

An asset sells in Market A for 26 (transaction costs 3, transport 2) and in Market B for 25 (transaction costs 1, transport 2). Market A is the principal market.

Fair value uses the principal market: 26 − 2 transport = 24 (transaction costs are not deducted). If there were no principal market, compare net amounts: A 26 − 3 − 2 = 21; B 25 − 1 − 2 = 22, so B is most advantageous and fair value is 25 − 2 = 23.

IAS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes in Estimates and Errors

ItemTreatment
Selecting policiesApply the relevant IFRS. If none, use judgement to give relevant and reliable information, looking in order at: IFRS on similar issues, then the Conceptual Framework, then other standard-setters and industry practice that do not conflict.
Change in accounting policyOnly if required by an IFRS or if it gives reliable and more relevant information. Apply retrospectively: restate comparatives and opening retained earnings, unless impracticable or the new IFRS has its own transition rules. Not a policy change: a different policy for transactions that differ in substance, or for new transactions.
Change in accounting estimateEstimates are monetary amounts with measurement uncertainty (useful lives, allowances, provisions). Apply prospectively in the current and future periods. A change in measurement technique is a change in estimate.
Prior period errorsOmissions or misstatements from failing to use reliable information that was available (mistakes, misapplied policies, oversights, fraud). Correct retrospectively: restate comparatives, or opening balances of the earliest period presented; disclose the nature and amounts.
Material (IAS 1 / IAS 8, 2018 definition)Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence the decisions of primary users.

Audit focus

Check that a "change in estimate" is not really an error being hidden prospectively, and that a "policy change" really gives more relevant information (not just a better profit). Test restated comparatives and disclosures; consider the effect on last year's audit opinion (ISA 710) and on communication with TCWG.

Current issues: sustainability and integrated reporting

Investors and other stakeholders now want information on environmental, social and governance (ESG) matters: how sustainability risks and opportunities affect the entity's cash flows, access to finance and cost of capital (dependencies), and how the entity affects people and the planet (impacts).

FrameworkKey points
Integrated reporting (<IR>, IIRC 2010)A concise report on how strategy, governance, performance and prospects create value over time, using six capitals: financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social and relationship, natural. Now part of the IFRS Foundation.
TCFD (2017)Climate disclosures under four pillars: governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets. Its approach is built into IFRS S1 and S2.
SASB StandardsIndustry-based sustainability metrics; used with IFRS S1 to find relevant industry topics
GRI and EU ESRSImpact-focused ("double materiality") reporting for a wide range of stakeholders

IFRS S1 General Requirements (ISSB, June 2023; effective 1 January 2024)

  • Objective: disclose information about sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could reasonably be expected to affect the entity's cash flows, access to finance or cost of capital over the short, medium or long term.
  • Disclosed for the same reporting entity and period as the financial statements, published at the same time; connected to the financial statements.
  • An entity may claim compliance only if it meets all requirements (explicit and unreserved statement).
  • Four pillars: governance (who oversees and how), strategy (risks and opportunities, effects on business model, value chain, financial position and plans), risk management (processes to identify, assess and monitor), metrics and targets (performance and progress).
  • Use IFRS S2 for climate and SASB Standards for industry topics; materiality focuses on primary users' decisions.

IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures

  • Covers physical risks (acute: storms, floods; chronic: rising temperatures, sea levels) and transition risks (policy, legal, technology, market, reputation), plus climate opportunities.
  • Strategy: transition plans, targets, and climate resilience tested with scenario analysis.
  • Cross-industry metrics: Scope 1, 2 and 3 greenhouse gas emissions; amount or share of assets and activities exposed to transition and physical risks; those aligned with opportunities; capital deployed on climate; internal carbon prices; how climate is linked to executive pay.
  • Targets: describe each target, its base period, milestones, progress, and whether it was validated by a third party.
Link to the financial statements and audit

Climate matters affect impairment (IAS 36), useful lives and residual values (IAS 16), provisions and decommissioning (IAS 37), fair values (IFRS 13), expected credit losses (IFRS 9), and going concern. Auditors check consistency between sustainability disclosures and the accounts (ISA 720) and may give assurance on them (ISAE 3000, ISAE 3410, ISSA 5000), covered in Chapters 25–26.
