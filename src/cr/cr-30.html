Consolidation Proforma: Workings 1 to 7

One fixed set of workings that fits every consolidation question in the 13 past papers: group structure, intra-group, net assets, goodwill, NCI, group retained earnings, and disposal or discontinued operation. Copy the layout, fill the numbers, and refer to each working in the "Ref" column of your answer.

W1Group structure: who owns what, from when, effective % and NCI %
W2Intra-group: sales, unrealised profit, assets, loans, dividends, balances
W3 · W4 · W5Net assets · Goodwill · Non-controlling interest
W6 · W7Group retained earnings and reserves · Disposal, spin-off, discontinued operation
Why this order

You asked for the workings as: structure, net assets, goodwill, retained earnings, NCI, intra-group, disposal. The ICAB suggested answers do the intra-group working second, because unrealised profit and extra depreciation change the subsidiary's net assets and the retained earnings. So the order used here is: W1 Structure → W2 Intra-group → W3 Net assets → W4 Goodwill → W5 NCI → W6 Group retained earnings → W7 Disposal. The seven items are exactly yours, in the order you need to calculate them.

W2 · Intra-group transactions and balances

Remove everything the group did with itself. Do it in this order: balances, income and expenses, unrealised profit, assets, loans, dividends.

ItemWhat to doEntry in the Adjustment column
Receivables and payables between group companiesDraw a cross-table (who owes whom). Remove both sides. If the totals do not agree, find the timing item (cash in transit, goods in transit)Dr Intercompany payable, Cr Intercompany receivable
Sales and purchases inside the groupRemove the full amount from revenue and cost of sales, whatever is still unsoldDr Revenue, Cr Cost of sales
Unrealised profit in closing inventory (PURP)Remove the profit still sitting in unsold goodsDr Cost of sales (seller), Cr Inventory
Asset sold inside the group (land, building, machine)Remove the gain on sale; reduce the asset to its original carrying amount; reverse the extra depreciationDr Gain (other income), Cr PPE. Then Dr Accumulated depreciation, Cr Depreciation
Intra-group loan and interestRemove the loan on both sides and the interest income and cost. Check if the interest was capitalisedDr Loan payable, Cr Loan receivable. Dr Interest income, Cr Finance cost
Dividend inside the groupRemove the parent's dividend income from the subsidiary and the subsidiary's dividend paid. The NCI share of the dividend stays as a movement in NCIDr Dividend income, Cr Retained earnings (dividend paid)
Management fee, guarantee fee, rentRemove income in one company against expense in the otherDr Other income, Cr Admin expense
PURP = unsold goods × markup ÷ (100 + markup) (when markup is on cost) PURP = unsold goods × margin % (when margin is on selling price)
Who bears the PURPParent sells to subsidiary: the whole PURP reduces the parent's retained earnings. Subsidiary sells to parent: the PURP reduces the subsidiary's post-acquisition profit, so it is shared with the NCI (put it in W3 net assets). Example: Mars bought goods from Jupitar for 40,000,000 at 20% markup, half unsold: PURP = 40,000,000 × 20/120 × 50% = 3,333,333.
Machine or building sold inside the groupExample (Orbit, Nov 2022): Galaxy sold a machine to Orbit, then 1 year of 3 remained. Orbit's book value = 180,000 × 1/3 = 60,000. Galaxy's would be 120,000 × 1/5 = 24,000. Difference 36,000 is the PPE PURP: Dr Retained earnings, Cr PPE. Each year, release it through reduced depreciation.
Entity that has left the group

If the other party is no longer in the group at the year end (spun off or sold), its balances become ordinary trade balances; do not eliminate them. Revenue with it is eliminated only up to the disposal date, and no PURP is raised on goods it still holds (May 2026 Lily, Nov 2023 Ares, Nov 2022 Comet).

Choice of NCI measureFair value (full goodwill): use it when the question gives the fair value of NCI or a share price for the NCI (Jul 2023 Circle, Jul 2024 Signature: NCI 28,440,000 × 45% × 22 = 281,556,000). Proportionate share: NCI % × net assets (this is what most ICAB answers show as the "net asset method": goodwill = consideration − parent's share of net assets). Say in one line which you used and keep it the same in W5.
What the examiners didMost official answers (Nov 2025, May 2026, Mar 2025, Nov 2024, Mar 2023, Nov 2022) use the proportionate method and give goodwill = consideration + NCI % × net assets − net assets. In Nov 2025 the question even gave NCI fair values (Venus 200,000,000), but the answer still used the proportionate method. Markers follow the scheme, so state your method clearly.
  • Deferred or variable consideration: discount to present value at acquisition, then unwind each year through finance cost. Example (Orbit): 5,500,000 due in 2 years at 5% = 4,988,662; the other 511,338 is finance cost (Dr Finance cost in group retained earnings).
  • Shares issued as consideration: value at fair value on the acquisition date (Atlas bought Apollo by issuing new shares).
  • Step acquisition: goodwill = fair value of old interest + new consideration + NCI − net assets (Daisy: 274,770,000 + 213,710,000 = 488,480,000 less 80% × 445,230,493 = 132,295,606).
  • Customer contracts and other intangibles identified at acquisition are added to net assets and amortised (King: PV of contract profit 440,000 for 2 years at 10% = 763,636).
  • Bargain purchase: if net assets exceed the price, recognise the difference as a gain in profit or loss at acquisition (Ares, Nov 2023: gain 3,941,930, already recorded).
  • Impairment: show goodwill after impairment. Charge the impairment to group profit (parent's share to RE, the rest to NCI when NCI is at fair value).
  • Acquisition costs are expensed, never added to goodwill.

Shortcut checkNCI = NCI % × net assets at the year end (W3) + NCI's share of unimpaired goodwill. Here: 20% × 1,280 = 256, plus goodwill belonging to NCI (200 − 180 = 20, less 8 impaired = 12) = 268. This is a good way to test your answer.
NCI in the profit or lossProfit for the year of S (adjusted for PURP, fair value depreciation, impairment) × NCI %. Put it in "Profit attributable to non-controlling interest" and do the same for OCI. If control starts mid-year, only the profit after that date.
Care point

Under the proportionate method, NCI has no share of goodwill, so there is no impairment line in W5. In the Daisy official answer, Working 1 says "30% non-controlling interest" but the calculation correctly uses 20% (Daisy owns 80%). Always take NCI % = 100% − effective %.

Presenting a discontinued operation (IFRS 5)

If the part sold or held for sale is a major line of business or area of operations, the consolidated profit or loss shows one line: "Profit or loss from discontinued operations" = the post-tax result of the operation to the date of disposal + the gain or loss on disposal. The detail goes in a note. Comparatives are re-presented. Disposal and spin-off questions so far asked for the gain; this presentation line is a likely next step.

Mini worked example: W1 to W6 in one page

Question

P bought 80% of S on 1 January 20X1 for 900. At that date S had share capital 500, retained earnings 300 and land with a fair value 100 above book value. The fair value of the NCI was 200. At 31 December 20X3, S has retained earnings 700 and P has retained earnings 1,500. During 20X3 S sold goods to P for 200 at a markup of 25% on cost; half are unsold. Goodwill has been impaired by 40 in total. Prepare the workings.

W1 S is a subsidiary from 1 Jan 20X1: effective 80%, NCI 20%.

W2 Intra-group sales of 200 removed from revenue and cost of sales. PURP = unsold 100 × 25/125 = 20, charged to S (the seller). Dr Cost of sales 20, Cr Inventory 20.

W4 Goodwill (fair value method) = 900 + 200 − 900 = 200; after impairment 40 = 160. Proportionate method: 900 + (20% × 900 = 180) − 900 = 180; NCI at acquisition 180.

W5 NCI = 200 + 20% × 380 (76) − 20% × 40 (8) = 268. Check: 20% × 1,280 = 256 + (20 − 8 = 12) = 268.

W6 Retained earnings = 1,500 + 80% × (380 − 40) (= 272) = 1,772.

Common marks lost

(1) Using the year-end balance instead of the acquisition-date balance in goodwill. (2) Forgetting that PURP on a subsidiary's sale is shared with NCI. (3) Taking 100% of the PURP when the parent sold, and sharing it with NCI by mistake. (4) Not removing the investment account. (5) Keeping the NCI% at the old figure after a step acquisition. (6) Consolidating a full year of profit when control started mid-year.
