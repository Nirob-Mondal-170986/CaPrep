Sufficient, appropriate evidence and triangulation (ISA 500)

Sufficient = quantityMore risk needs more evidence, but extra work must actually reduce risk. A bigger sample of weak evidence is not enough.
Appropriate = qualityRelevant (logically linked to the assertion) and reliable: external beats internal; internal is better when controls work; direct beats indirect (observe beats ask); documents beat oral; originals beat copies.

Management's expert (e.g. actuary or valuer used by the client): evaluate the expert's competence, capabilities and objectivity; understand their work; and evaluate it as evidence for the assertion.

Triangulation: gather complementary evidence from different sources that tells a consistent story. This is stronger than more evidence from one source. With data analytics, re-assess reliability: email analytics is not as reliable as accounting system data.

Mattel (Arthur Andersen)"Bill and hold" sales with false invoices and bills of lading. Confirmations showed big differences and the documents were stamped "bill and hold", with one person signing for both Mattel and the carrier. Nobody followed up.
Parmalat (2003)€3.95bn of cash at Bank of America "confirmed" by one posted letter that was forged. The cash did not exist. Even external confirmations need scepticism when amounts are very material.

Substantive procedures are always required for each material class of transactions, balance and disclosure (ISA 330), whatever the risk assessment: substantive analytics (large, predictable populations) and tests of details (balances).

The seven procedures and auditing non-current assets

ProcedureNotes
InquiryAsking knowledgeable people inside or outside the entity; written representations. Never enough alone: Escott v BarChris (1968) found auditors negligent for accepting "glib answers"
ObservationWatching a process (inventory count, a control). Shows only that it happened at that moment; staff may act differently when watched
InspectionRecords, documents or assets. Asset → record gives completeness; record → asset or document gives existence or occurrence; seeing an asset does not prove ownership. Cut-off: inspect the reverse population after year end
RecalculationChecking arithmetic (interest, invoices, WIP); very effective with automated tools, e.g. recalculating depreciation for every asset
ReperformanceAuditor re-does a client control, e.g. bank or supplier reconciliations
External confirmationDirect written reply from a third party to the auditor: banks, receivables, inventory held by others
Analytical proceduresSee topic 4
Tangible and intangible assets: main risksExpenses capitalised (existence) · wrong cost or valuation · wrong or no depreciation or amortisation · no proper impairment review (all valuation). For intangibles: patent agent confirmation and renewal fees (rights); specialist valuations; check capitalised development costs to costing records; check amortisation rates; review income from patents and licences.
Auditing value in use (IAS 36)Get management's calculation and reperform it · agree cash flows to board-approved budgets · growth rate not above the long-term average · compare with competitors · include all maintenance costs and the disposal value · exclude financing and tax cash flows · compare the discount rate with market rates · check the write-down and disclosures. If no review was done where indicators exist, ask for one; if refused, modify the opinion.

Analytical procedures (ISA 315, ISA 520)

StagePurpose
Planning (required by ISA 315)Identify risks and focus the approach (top-down, efficient). Interpret → investigate → corroborate. Ask "why did the client make more or less money?": revenue = volume × price; margin = selling price, cost price, product mix; overheads fixed or variable
Substantive (optional)Best for large, predictable populations (revenue, margin, payroll). Consider the reliability of data, how detailed it is, whether budgets are realistic expectations or stretch targets, and past experience
Completion (required by ISA 520)Are the statements consistent with the auditor's understanding? Any new risks? Use figures after audit adjustments
1. Expect
Build an independent expectation (e.g. average staff × average salary) 2. Compare
Set an acceptable difference; it gets smaller as risk rises 3. Explain
Ask management, then corroborate the answer 4. Evaluate
Unexplained difference → more tests of detail

Techniques: ratios (margins, receivable and inventory days, current and quick ratios, gearing, ROCE); related accounts (loans and interest, PPE and depreciation, receivables and bad debts); reasonableness tests ((cost + additions − disposals) × rate); trend analysis (time series without seasonality, regression); non-financial data (staff numbers, floor space, revenue per passenger mile).

Accounting estimates (ISA 540 Revised)

Accounting estimate: a monetary amount whose measurement is subject to estimation uncertainty (an inherent lack of precision).

Examples: inventory obsolescence; depreciation; valuation and impairment of assets and financial instruments; lawsuit provisions; revenue on long-term contracts; fair values in a business combination. Risk comes from estimation uncertainty, complexity and subjectivity, which open the door to management bias.

Understand and assessThe entity and environment · management's experience in making the estimate · volatility · degree of judgement · controls over the estimate and its data (design, implementation and, where needed, operating effectiveness).
RespondTest management's method, assumptions and data; ask why that point was chosen and how uncertainty is disclosed · use events up to the report date · develop an auditor's point estimate or range.

Indicators of management bias are treated like possible fraud. Misstatements are factual, judgemental or projected (ISA 450). Estimates with high uncertainty are often key audit matters (ISA 701). AI can help with complex, data-heavy estimates, but beware bias built into algorithms.

Initial engagements: opening balances (ISA 510)

Requirements: check prior closing balances were correctly brought forward (or restated); check accounting policies are appropriate and consistently applied; and either review the predecessor's working papers, use current-year procedures, or perform specific procedures.

Specific proceduresReceivables and payables: receipts and payments after the period start. Inventory (current work gives little comfort): observe a count now and reconcile back, test valuation of opening items, test gross profit and cut-off. Non-current items: examine records; confirm loans and investments with third parties.
ReportingCannot get enough evidence → qualified or disclaimer. Opening balances misstated and not corrected, or policies inconsistent → qualified or adverse. A predecessor's modification that is still relevant and material → modify the current opinion too.
Bangladesh point

The UK Companies Act 2006 forces a predecessor auditor to give the successor access to working papers. The workbook notes that the Companies Act 1994 and the Financial Reporting Act 2015 have no similar rule in Bangladesh, so access depends on professional courtesy and the client's permission.

Audit team, e-commerce and scepticism in fieldwork

Team (ISA 220): partner takes responsibility for quality and gives direction (team meeting on risks) → supervision of juniors by seniors → review (the partner must review critical judgements and significant risks) → consultation on difficult or contentious matters, documented.

E-commerce clientsTeam needs IT skills. Understand the e-commerce strategy, extent of online trade and outsourcing. Key issues: security (firewalls, encryption, system development controls), transaction integrity (complete, accurate, timely, authorised) and process alignment (website not linked to accounts and inventory, which affects completeness and timing).
High-scepticism areasFraud (discuss at every team meeting, ISA 240) · going concern (smaller entities have little forecast data) · impairment (challenge cash flows and assumptions) · receivables and revenue.

Ask yourself: Does it reflect the substance? Does it make sense? What is missing that should be there? Are management's assumptions still valid? What evidence is there besides management's? Is any evidence contradictory?

Documenting scepticism: Ben the audit senior

Ben spotted a wrong discount rate in a receivables impairment calculation. After 2 hours of discussion the client corrected it. He filed only the revised calculation. Is that enough?

No. File a note of the issues discussed, what the client said, the evidence offered, his follow-up questions, further information, how he verified it and his conclusion. Regulators say scepticism is often shown but not documented.

Automation bias (FRC Professional Judgement Guidance, 2022): believing the tool over your own knowledge. Data analytics supports scepticism but is not infallible.

External confirmations (ISA 505)

The auditor keeps control: decides what to ask and whom, designs the request with a reply direct to the auditor, and sends first and second requests. Uses: banks, receivables, payables, inventory held by third parties, deeds held by lawyers, loans.

Management refusesAsk why and seek evidence for the reason; consider whether it hides fraud; reassess risk; do alternative procedures; consider the opinion.
Doubtful repliesReply came via the client or from a different party → doubt reliability. Non-reply to a positive request → alternative procedures (e.g. cash received after year end, invoices and despatch notes).
Receivables circularisationPractice points
MethodPositive (reply either way) is preferred. Negative (reply only if you disagree) only with good controls and many small balances
Sample from a complete list; focus onOld unpaid balances, accounts written off, credit balances, round-sum payments, nil balances, accounts already paid
TimingJust after year end ideally; up to 3 months before if controls are strong, then roll forward (reconcile control account, test sales, returns and cash in between, compare ratios)
Reasons for differencesDisputes (provide?) · cut-off (sales or returns) · cash in transit · mis-postings · netting with supplier balances · teeming and lading (test prompt posting of receipts)

Bank letter: client's written authority (all parties for joint accounts); reaches the bank at least one month before year end; ask about all accounts including nil and recently closed ones, loan terms, facilities, set-off rights, security, guarantees and items in safe custody. Without one, enough evidence on cash is unlikely.

Sampling (ISA 530) and directional testing

Audit sampling: applying procedures to less than 100% of a population so that every sampling unit has a chance of selection, giving a reasonable basis for a conclusion on the whole population.
MethodNotes
RandomEqual chance for each item
Systematic / intervalRandom start then every nth item; monetary unit sampling picks every nth £ (bias to big items)
Block / clusterA group (e.g. all June sales); quick but not representative
HaphazardNo structure; risk of bias; not for statistical sampling

Statistical vs judgement: statistical gives unbiased selection, defensible sizes and valid projection, but sizes can be large and judgement is still needed. Bigger sample when: more assurance is needed from sampling, tolerable misstatement is lower, more errors are expected, or the population is more variable (substantive tests). Population size matters little except for small populations. Stratify when the population is not uniform.

Evaluation: project the sample errors to the population; if over tolerable misstatement, get more evidence. Look at patterns, size, whether errors are factual or judgemental, fraud, and whether they affect the balance sheet or profit. For controls, a high deviation rate raises the assessed risk.

Primary testAlso gives comfort on

Common trap

To find omissions (completeness) you must start outside the ledger (despatch notes, GRNs, supplier statements) and trace in. Sampling from the ledger can never find an item that was never recorded.

Exam focus

Most CR audit requirements ask for procedures. Make each one specific: what to do, to what document or source, and which assertion it covers ("Agree a sample of additions to purchase invoices and board minutes → existence and valuation"). Analytical procedures on scenario numbers are common: calculate, then give the audit risk. Practice: Darwin, Libby (payables), TrueBlue (fraud tip-off on receipts), Strombolix (related parties).
