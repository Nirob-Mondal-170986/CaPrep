1

Role and objectives of internal audit

Internal audit function: performs assurance and consulting activities designed to evaluate and improve the effectiveness of the entity's governance, risk management and internal control processes (ISA 610).

Need for internal audit depends on: scale, diversity and complexity; number of employees; cost vs benefit; changes in structure, systems or key risks; control problems; unexplained events. Listed company audit committees should review its effectiveness every year.

Controls

Review design, monitor operation, recommend cost-effective improvements (financial and non-financial).

Information

Examine financial and operating information.

3 Es

Economy, efficiency and effectiveness of operations.

Compliance

Laws, regulations, internal policies, authorisation.

Assets

Safeguarding cash, portable assets, investments.

Risk and governance

Risk management, corporate objectives, governance; special investigations (fraud).

Real case: WorldCom (2002)

Internal audit head Cynthia Cooper found that billions of dollars of operating costs had been capitalised, turning a $662m loss into a $1.4bn profit, despite the external auditor and CFO saying all was well. She reported to the audit committee chair. It shows the value of an independent internal audit with a direct line to the audit committee, and of whistleblowing.

External auditors may use internal audit work under ISA 610 (Chapter 6). Internal audit can also act as a check on reports not audited externally, help implement new standards, and liaise with the external auditor.

3

Regulation, ethics and standards

Internal audit is not regulated by law in most countries; professional bodies such as the Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA) set standards. The workbook covers the IIA Code of Ethics: integrity, objectivity, confidentiality and competency, and the International Standards (attribute standards on purpose, independence, proficiency, quality; performance standards on managing the activity, planning, performing, communicating and monitoring).

Update

The IIA replaced its Code of Ethics and Standards with the Global Internal Audit Standards (issued January 2024, effective 9 January 2025). They are organised in five domains: purpose of internal auditing; ethics and professionalism; governing the internal audit function (board oversight); managing the function; and performing services. The ethical principles (integrity, objectivity, competency, due professional care, confidentiality) are now part of the standards.

Independence threatsReporting to the finance director whose area is audited; auditing systems they designed; management limiting scope; long tenure; bonuses linked to results; outsourcing to the external auditor (self-review for PIEs).
SafeguardsAn internal audit charter approved by the audit committee; direct access to the audit committee chair; the audit committee approves the plan, budget and head of internal audit's appointment and removal; rotation; no operational duties.

Bangladesh: the BSEC Corporate Governance Code requires listed companies to have an internal audit function, with a Head of Internal Audit and Compliance who reports to the audit committee, and Bangladesh Bank requires banks to have internal control and compliance departments.

4

Internal audit, risk management and fraud

Management owns risk management; internal audit gives assurance that the process works and may help design it, without taking management decisions (it is the third line in the three lines model). Activities: review the risk register and appetite, test key controls over major risks, report on how well risks are managed, and advise.

Fraud: management is responsible for prevention and detection. Internal audit helps by assessing fraud risks and controls, being alert to red flags, running investigations when asked, and reporting to the audit committee; it is not a guarantee against fraud.

Self-test pattern: setting up internal audit and a payroll audit

Recommend how a new internal audit function should operate, deal with the FD's concerns, and draft terms of reference for a payroll audit.

Charter: purpose, authority (access to records, people and premises), independence (reports to the audit committee), scope, responsibilities, standards followed. Payroll terms of reference: objective (pay only real employees, the right amounts, correctly recorded and taxed); scope (period, sites, processes such as starters, leavers, rate changes, overtime, deductions); approach (walkthroughs, data analytics such as duplicate bank accounts, control tests); timing, staff, reporting lines and deadlines.

6

Multi-site operations and reporting

Multi-site (branches, shops, factories): rotate visits based on risk and size; use standard work programmes and checklists; data analytics centrally to spot outlier sites; surprise visits for cash and inventory; local staff training; use of self-assessment questionnaires confirmed by visits.

Report contentExecutive summary and overall opinion or rating; objectives and scope; findings (condition, criteria, cause, effect); prioritised recommendations; management responses with owners and dates; follow-up of previous issues.
Good reportingTimely, clear, concise, balanced (note what works), constructive and actionable; tailored to audience (audit committee summary vs detailed report for managers); significant issues escalated promptly.

Outsourcing internal audit: access to specialist skills and flexibility, but less business knowledge and continuity; if provided by the external audit firm, there are self-review and management threats (not allowed for PIE audit clients).

Exam focus

Expect to compare internal and external audit, assess the independence and effectiveness of an internal audit function, design an assignment (terms of reference, procedures), deal with an ethical dilemma (pressure from management to change findings), and decide whether the external auditor can rely on internal audit work (ISA 610).
