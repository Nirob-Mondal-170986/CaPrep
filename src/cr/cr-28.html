1

Supreme audit institutions and the legal framework

Supreme Audit Institution (SAI): the national body responsible for independent audit of government revenue and expenditure, reporting objectively to the legislature and the public on the lawful and proper management of public money.

INTOSAI describes public finance as a trust held by elected officials; external audit reveals departures from legality, economy, efficiency and effectiveness. The SAI's role is set by the constitution or by law (the trend is a separate audit law). Weak legal frameworks, poor government reporting and limited capacity lead to weak SAIs and weak accountability, so donors (World Bank, IMF, development banks) support SAI development.

Internal audit in governmentPart of management's internal control: gives the head of a ministry or agency an objective opinion on internal operations and recommendations for improvement. Follows IIA standards. May be a unit in each ministry or a central unit (e.g. in the Ministry of Finance); often focused on compliance.
External audit (the SAI)Guardian of the public interest; reports on misuse of public funds; broad view of the whole public financial management (PFM) system; can push PFM reform. Should use internal audit reports and coordinate to avoid duplication.

Features of public sector external audit

FeaturePoint
Fraud and corruptionNot the prime objective (watchdog, not bloodhound), though some SAIs can refer for prosecution; separate inspectorates or anti-corruption bodies may lead. Expectation gap
Systems-basedReview controls; select transactions by risk; not every transaction is checked
Ancillary rolesSome SAIs comment on budgets and assumptions
ReportingMainly to the legislature; sometimes also to the executive (more impact but less independence)
Pre-audit vs post-auditApproving transactions before they happen is debated; INTOSAI says it can be done by other bodies
Contracting outPrivate firms may audit some bodies (SOEs, local government); the SAI remains responsible and must control quality

Compared with the private sector, a public body's auditor may: give an opinion on the lawfulness of spending; focus more on fraud and corruption (e.g. data matching); focus on value for money; audit grants for grant givers; run special investigations; answer questions and objections from the public. Independence from politicians and government is critical. Audit opinions follow the ISA 700/705 pattern: unmodified, qualified, adverse, disclaimer.

Fraud warning signs: missing records; weak controls and poor segregation; difficulty getting source documents; unsatisfactory explanations; defensive staff; unusual payments; lifestyles beyond salary; fragile systems; problems with bankers, lawyers or previous auditors; staff who never take leave.

Types of public sector audit

TypePurposeStandards
Financial (regularity)Opinion on whether financial statements are "regular": comply with standards, rules and conventions; linked to stewardship of public money. Scope can include budget preparation and process, accounting and control systems, revenue collection, contracts, commitments, inventories, risk managementISSAI 200 / 2000–2899
PerformanceActivities and results, not the accounts: economy (lowest cost for the right quality and time), efficiency (most output per input), effectiveness (objectives achieved). Promotes transparency and accountability; effectiveness is often hard to measureISSAI 300 / 3000–3899
ComplianceWhether an activity follows the relevant laws, regulations and authorities (often checklist-based; key for banks, insurers, accredited bodies)ISSAI 400 / 4000–4899

Internal auditors can also perform all three (e.g. a regularity audit of the payables system coordinated with the external auditor). Principles-based ethics (e.g. for internal audit) are flexible and concise and fit many contexts ("comply or explain"), but are more subjective to enforce than rules.

4

Practice in Bangladesh

The Office of the Comptroller and Auditor General (OCAG) is Bangladesh's SAI, set up under Article 128(1) of the Constitution and regarded as a pillar of the National Integrity System. It audits government receipts and expenditure and reports whether money is properly accounted for under the rules. Under section 5(1) of the C&AG (Additional Functions) Act 1974 it also audits statutory public authorities, public enterprises and local governments.

17 audit directoratesCivil; Revenue; Commercial; Works; Transport; Foreign Aided Projects; Posts, Telecommunication, Science, Information and Technology; Defence; Health; Education; Agriculture and Environment; LGRD; Social Security; Power and Energy; IT and Public Services; Constitutional Bodies; Mission.
Accountability chainParliament approves the budget → government spends → C&AG audits and submits reports to the President, who lays them before Parliament → the Public Accounts Committee (PAC) examines whether money was used for approved purposes and within authority.

Four types of public audit in Bangladesh: compliance audit, performance audit, special case audit, financial audit.

Correction to the workbook

Sections 5.5.1 and 7 of the workbook say the "Controller General of Accounts (CGA) is the Supreme Audit Institution". That is wrong: the CGA is the government's accounting office (part of the executive). The SAI is the Comptroller and Auditor General (C&AG), through the OCAG, as the same chapter states in section 4. Use C&AG in your answers.

The C&AG's legal and operational framework

Legal framework (three pillars)Constitutional mandate: Articles 127–132 (audit of the Consolidated Fund and public accounts; reports go to the President, then Parliament). Statutory mandate: the C&AG (Additional Functions) Act 1974 under Article 128(3): statutory authorities, public enterprises, local authorities. Audit Code (rules): directions, principles and procedures. The C&AG's officers can examine all records, documents, stamps, securities, cash and government property.
Operational frameworkStandards: ISSAIs, with Government Auditing Standards of Bangladesh (GASB) based on them. Guidelines for compliance, financial and performance audits. Audit instructions for specific matters. Practice notes. Manuals (office- and subject-specific). Handbooks for practical know-how.
Interactive question: financial audit of the Ministry of Education for the OCAG

Which standards would you follow?

The OCAG follows INTOSAI, so use the ISSAI financial audit standards (ISSAI 2000–2899), applied through Bangladesh's GASB and the OCAG financial audit guidelines.
