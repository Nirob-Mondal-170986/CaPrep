P20

Forward contract v money market hedge

Typical: 6–12 marks = 12–24 minutes. Tested in almost every paper.

When to use: A foreign currency payment or receipt at a future date, with spot, forward and interest rates given.

  1. Pick the rate: the bank always wins. With a quote in Tk per foreign unit, you pay at the higher rate and receive at the lower rate.
  2. Forward: foreign amount × forward rate. Cash moves on the future date.
  3. Money market, payment: deposit foreign currency now (PV of the payment) → buy it at spot → borrow Tk → add Tk interest.
  4. Money market, receipt: borrow foreign currency now (PV of the receipt) → sell at spot → deposit Tk → add Tk interest.
  5. Interest: annual rate × months ÷ 12.
  6. Compare on the same date. Lowest cost or highest receipt wins.
Example data

A Bangladeshi company must pay USD 2m in 3 months and will receive EUR 1m in 6 months. Tk per USD: spot 120.00 – 120.50, 3-month forward 121.20 – 121.80. Tk per EUR: spot 130.00 – 130.60, 6-month forward 131.50 – 132.30. Annual interest: USD deposit 4%, EUR borrowing 5%, Tk borrowing 12%, Tk deposit 8%.

(a) Payment of USD 2m in 3 months

Particulars Workings Amount (Tk)
Forward 2,000,000 × 121.80 (higher rate) 243,600,000
Money market hedge
1. Deposit USD now 2,000,000 ÷ (1 + 4% × 3/12) USD 1,980,198
2. Buy USD at spot 1,980,198 × 120.50 238,613,861
3. Borrow Tk for 3 months 238,613,861 × (1 + 12% × 3/12) 245,772,277
Cost in 3 months 245,772,277

Decision: the forward is cheaper by Tk 2,172,277.

(b) Receipt of EUR 1m in 6 months

Particulars Workings Amount (Tk)
Forward 1,000,000 × 131.50 (lower rate) 131,500,000
Money market hedge
1. Borrow EUR now 1,000,000 ÷ (1 + 5% × 6/12) EUR 975,610
2. Sell EUR at spot 975,610 × 130.00 126,829,268
3. Deposit Tk for 6 months 126,829,268 × (1 + 8% × 6/12) 131,902,439
Receipt in 6 months The EUR 1m receipt repays the EUR loan 131,902,439

Decision: the money market hedge gives Tk 402,439 more.

Traps
  • Quote the other way round (foreign units per Tk, as in the 2026 mock): divide instead of multiply, and the "bank wins" side flips. Test it: a payment must cost you more.
  • Forward quoted as premium or discount: adjust the spot rate first.
  • Interest rates are annual. Always scale for 3 or 6 months.
  • Compare like with like: both results must be at the future date (or both at today).
  • Leading: if asked about paying now, compare the spot cost plus Tk interest with the hedges.
P21

Currency options – exercise or lapse

Typical: 4–10 marks = 8–20 minutes. Tested in 2024, Jul-Aug 2025 and the 2026 mock.

When to use: The company wants protection but also wants to gain if the rate moves in its favour.

  1. Contract size: amount ÷ contract size = number of contracts (traded options only).
  2. Call or put? Buying the contract currency = call. Selling it = put.
  3. Premium: paid now, whatever happens. Convert at today's spot if it is in foreign currency.
  4. Exercise or not? Compare the strike with the spot rate on the day. Exercise only if the strike is better.
  5. Result: cost or receipt, plus or minus the premium. Also state the worst-case rate.
Example data

(a) Pay USD 2m in 3 months. Bank (OTC) call option on USD at Tk 121.50, premium Tk 0.80 per USD. Spot in 3 months: Tk 124.00 or Tk 119.50. (b) Receive EUR 1m in 6 months. Traded EUR put options, contract size EUR 125,000, strike Tk 131.00, premium Tk 1.20 per EUR. Spot in 6 months: Tk 128.00 or Tk 134.00.

(a) Payment – call option on USD (Tk)

Spot Tk 124.00 Spot Tk 119.50
Strike 121.50 v spot Strike is cheaper: exercise Spot is cheaper: let it lapse
Cost of USD 2m 2m × 121.50 = 243,000,000 2m × 119.50 = 239,000,000
Premium (2m × 0.80) 1,600,000 1,600,000
Total cost 244,600,000 240,600,000
Effective rate 122.30 120.30
  • Worst-case rate = strike 121.50 + premium 0.80 = Tk 122.30. Maximum cost Tk 244.6m.

(b) Receipt – traded put options on EUR (Tk)

  • Contracts: 1,000,000 ÷ 125,000 = 8 put contracts. Premium: 8 × 125,000 × 1.20 = Tk 1,200,000.
Spot Tk 128.00 Spot Tk 134.00
Strike 131.00 v spot Strike is higher: exercise Spot is higher: let it lapse
Receipt for EUR 1m 1m × 131.00 = 131,000,000 1m × 134.00 = 134,000,000
Premium (1,200,000) (1,200,000)
Net receipt 129,800,000 132,800,000
Effective rate 129.80 132.80
  • Worst-case rate = strike 131.00 − premium 1.20 = Tk 129.80.
Traps
  • Contract in the home currency (BC Ltd: options on Taka, LKR payment): convert the foreign amount at the strike to get the Taka amount, then divide by the contract size. Buying LKR = selling Taka = put.
  • Part contracts: round to whole contracts. Hedge the small balance on the forward market or leave it at spot.
  • Expiry month: choose the first expiry after the transaction date.
  • Premium is a sunk cost on the day. It never affects the exercise decision.
  • Check your subtraction. ICAB's 2026 mock answer had a slip in the net receipt.
P22

Currency futures hedge – effective rate and basis

Typical: 11–14 marks = 22–28 minutes. Tested in Nov-Dec 2025 Q4.

When to use: The question gives futures prices and a contract size, and asks for the outcome or effectiveness of the hedge.

  1. Buy or sell? Buying the contract currency later = buy futures now. Selling it later = sell futures now.
  2. Number of contracts = amount ÷ contract size. Round to whole contracts.
  3. Basis = spot − futures. It falls evenly to zero at expiry. Estimate the closing basis by time.
  4. Close out: gain or loss = movement in futures price × contract size × contracts.
  5. Net result = spot transaction ± futures gain or loss. Effective rate = net result ÷ amount.
  6. Hedge efficiency = futures gain ÷ spot loss (or the other way round).
Example data

It is 1 September. A US company must pay EUR 2.4m on 30 November. December EUR futures: contract size EUR 125,000, price USD 1.1060 per EUR, expiry end of December. Spot now 1.1000. On 30 November spot is 1.1300 and the futures price is 1.1315.

Particulars Workings Amount (USD)
Action Must buy EUR, so buy December futures Buy
Contracts 2,400,000 ÷ 125,000 = 19.2 19
Opening basis Spot 1.1000 − futures 1.1060 (4 months to expiry) (0.0060)
Expected closing basis (0.0060) × 1 month ÷ 4 months (0.0015)
Expected lock-in rate Opening futures 1.1060 + closing basis (0.0015) 1.1045
Outcome on 30 November
Buy EUR 2.4m at spot 2,400,000 × 1.1300 (2,712,000)
Futures gain (1.1315 − 1.1060) × 125,000 × 19 60,562.5
Net cost (USD) (2,651,437.5)
Effective rate 2,651,437.5 ÷ 2,400,000 1.1048
Loss on the spot market 2,400,000 × (1.1300 − 1.1000) (72,000)
Hedge efficiency 60,562.5 ÷ 72,000 84.1%

Actual closing basis = 1.1300 − 1.1315 = (0.0015), as expected. The hedge is not 100% efficient because of the basis and because EUR 25,000 (0.2 of a contract) is not hedged.

Traps
  • Basis risk: the basis may not fall evenly, so the final rate is not certain.
  • Rounding contracts leaves an over-hedge or under-hedge. Say what happens to the difference.
  • Margin: initial and variation margin tie up cash. Worth a mark in discussion.
  • Futures v forward: futures are standard, traded and can be closed early. Forwards are tailor-made and binding.
P23

Transaction exposure – gain or loss

Typical: 6–9 marks = 12–18 minutes. Tested in Jul-Aug 2025 Q4b.

When to use: "Calculate the exchange gain or loss" or "should we invoice in Taka instead?"

  1. Expected Taka amount at the rate on the transaction date.
  2. Actual Taka amount at the settlement rate.
  3. Difference = exchange gain or loss. Do it for receipts and payments separately, then net.
  4. Profit under each scenario in one table, then recommend.
Example data

A Bangladeshi exporter sells goods for USD 500,000 and buys parts for USD 200,000, both on 3 months' credit. Local costs are Tk 26m. The rate on the transaction date is Tk 118 per USD. Possible rates at settlement: 115, 118, 121. The customer offers instead a fixed price of Tk 59.6m.

Tk '000 Rate 115 Rate 118 Rate 121 Fixed Tk price
Sales receipt (USD 500,000) 57,500 59,000 60,500 59,600
Parts payment (USD 200,000) (23,000) (23,600) (24,200) (USD risk stays)
Local costs (26,000) (26,000) (26,000) (26,000)
Profit 8,500 9,400 10,300 See note
Gain / (loss) on receipt v rate 118 (1,500) – 1,500
Gain / (loss) on payment v rate 118 600 – (600)
Net exchange gain / (loss) (900) – 900

Fixed Tk price: profit = 59,600 − 26,000 − parts at the settlement rate = 10,600 at 115; 10,000 at 118; 9,400 at 121.

Advice: the company has a net USD receipt of 300,000, so it gains if the Taka weakens. The fixed Taka price removes the risk on sales but leaves the USD 200,000 payment exposed. Better: net the receipt and payment (natural hedge) and hedge only the USD 300,000 balance.

Traps
  • Net first. Receipts and payments in the same currency and on the same date offset each other.
  • Direction: a weaker Taka helps an exporter and hurts an importer.
  • Transaction v translation v economic exposure: know the one-line definitions.
P24

Forward rate agreement (FRA)

Typical: 5–8 marks = 10–16 minutes. Tested in Nov-Dec 2023 and Nov-Dec 2024.

When to use: A company will borrow (or deposit) at a future date and wants to fix the interest rate now.

  1. Borrower buys the FRA at the higher quoted rate. A depositor sells at the lower rate.
  2. "3–9" FRA: starts in 3 months, ends in 9 months, so it covers 6 months.
  3. On the start date: FRA payment = (market rate − FRA rate) × principal × months ÷ 12. Receive it if the rate is above the FRA rate; pay it if below.
  4. Loan interest is paid at the actual rate, including the company's own margin.
  5. Net cost = loan interest ± FRA payment. It is the same in every scenario.
Example data

A company will borrow Tk 50m in 3 months for 6 months. It can borrow at the benchmark rate + 1%. The bank quotes a 3–9 FRA at 9.60 – 9.20. The benchmark rate in 3 months is either 11% or 8%.

Tk Benchmark 11% Benchmark 8%
Loan rate (benchmark + 1%) 12.0% 9.0%
Loan interest for 6 months (50m × rate × 6/12) (3,000,000) (2,250,000)
FRA: (benchmark − 9.60%) × 50m × 6/12 350,000 received (400,000) paid
Net interest cost (2,650,000) (2,650,000)
Effective annual rate 10.6% 10.6%

The effective rate is always the FRA rate 9.60% + the company's margin 1% = 10.6%.

Traps
  • Compare the FRA rate with the benchmark, not with the company's own loan rate.
  • Pro-rate for the loan period. ICAB's Nov-Dec 2024 answer used the wrong interest for one quarter.
  • The FRA is separate from the loan. It fixes the rate but does not provide the money.
  • No upside: if rates fall, the company pays the bank. An option (cap) keeps the upside for a premium.
P25

Interest rate swap – net payment and swap design

Typical: 5–11 marks = 10–22 minutes. Tested in Nov-Dec 2023, Jul-Aug 2025 and the 2026 mock.

When to use: (a) "Calculate the net swap payment." (b) "Design a swap that shares the benefit between two companies."

  1. Net payment: (floating rate received − fixed rate paid) × notional × months ÷ 12. Positive = receive.
  2. Total cost = loan interest ± swap payment. It should equal the fixed rate plus any margin left on the loan.
  3. Swap design, 4 steps: (1) rate table, (2) total saving = difference of the differences, less the bank fee, (3) target rate for each party, (4) the swap flows that reach the targets.
Example data

(a) Loan Tk 200m at benchmark + 2%, interest every 6 months. Swap: pay fixed 9%, receive benchmark. Benchmark is 10% in period 1 and 7% in period 2. (b) Company A can borrow at 8% fixed or benchmark + 1%, and wants floating. Company B can borrow at 10.5% fixed or benchmark + 2%, and wants fixed. A bank arranges the swap for 0.3%; the rest of the saving is shared equally.

(a) Net payment (Tk million)

Period 1 (benchmark 10%) Period 2 (benchmark 7%)
Loan rate (benchmark + 2%) 12% 9%
Loan interest (200 × rate × 6/12) (12.0) (9.0)
Swap: (benchmark − 9%) × 200 × 6/12 1.0 received (2.0) paid
Net interest cost (11.0) (11.0)

Fixed cost = (9% + 2%) × 200m × 6/12 = Tk 11m in every period.

(b) Swap design

Step Company A Company B Difference
1. Fixed rate 8.0% 10.5% 2.5%
Floating rate Benchmark + 1% Benchmark + 2% 1.0%
2. Total saving 2.5% − 1.0% = 1.5%
After bank fee, each 0.6% 0.6% 1.5% − 0.3% = 1.2%
3. Without the swap Benchmark + 1% 10.5%

Traps
  • Annual rates, half-year periods. ICAB's 2026 mock answer mixed yearly and half-yearly amounts.
  • Each company borrows where it has the comparative advantage (the type it does not want), then swaps.
  • Risks to mention: counterparty risk, no benefit if rates fall, hard to exit.
  • Swap v cap v collar: swap = no premium, no upside. Cap = premium, keeps upside. Collar = low premium, limited upside.
P26

Interest rate futures, options and collars

Typical: 6–10 marks = 12–20 minutes. Rarely tested as a calculation, but it is in the manual and in your hedging summary.

When to use: The question gives futures prices like 91.00 (= 100 − interest rate) or option strikes with premiums.

  1. Borrower sells futures (a depositor buys). Contracts = loan ÷ contract size × loan months ÷ 3.
  2. Close out: gain or loss = price movement % × contract size × 3/12 × contracts.
  3. Options on futures: a borrower buys puts (a cap). A depositor buys calls (a floor). Exercise only if it gives a gain.
  4. Collar for a borrower: buy a put and sell a call at a higher strike. Lower premium, but the gain from falling rates is limited.
  5. Net cost = loan interest − futures or option gain + premium.
Example data

A company will borrow Tk 20m for 6 months, starting in 3 months. Three-month futures: contract size Tk 1m, price now 91.00. Put option at strike 91.00 costs 0.40%. Call option at strike 93.00 earns 0.15%. Outcomes: rates rise (borrowing rate 10.3%, futures 89.50) or fall (borrowing rate 5.9%, futures 94.00).

  • Contracts = 20m ÷ 1m × 6 ÷ 3 = 40. Each 1% on 40 contracts = 1% × 1m × 3/12 × 40 = Tk 100,000.
Tk Rates rise (10.3%, futures 89.50) Rates fall (5.9%, futures 94.00)
Loan interest (20m × rate × 6/12) 1,030,000 590,000
1. Futures: sell at 91.00
Futures gain / (loss) (91.00 − 89.50) = 1.5%: 150,000 (91.00 − 94.00) = −3%: (300,000)
Net cost with futures 880,000 (8.8%) 890,000 (8.9%)
2. Option: buy put at 91.00
Exercise? Yes: gain 1.5% = 150,000 No: let it lapse
Premium 0.40% 40,000 40,000
Net cost with option 920,000 (9.2%) 630,000 (6.3%)
3. Collar: buy put 91.00, sell call 93.00
Put Exercise: gain 150,000 Lapses
Call (sold) Not exercised Exercised against us: (94.00 − 93.00) = 1% = (100,000)
Net premium 0.40% − 0.15% 25,000 25,000
Net cost with collar 905,000 (9.05%) 715,000 (7.15%)

Futures fix the cost near 9% (100 − 91.00) in both cases, apart from basis. The option caps the cost and keeps the benefit of a fall. The collar is cheaper than the option but gives up part of the benefit.

Traps
  • Price = 100 − rate. When rates rise, the futures price falls. So a borrower sells first and buys back cheaper.
  • Loan longer than 3 months: scale the number of contracts (6 months = double).
  • Options on FRAs (bank options) are the other way round: a borrower buys a call (cap) and a depositor buys a put (floor).
  • Collar for a depositor: buy a call and sell a put.
