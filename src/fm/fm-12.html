P1

NPV and IRR – the core working format

Typical: 12–20 marks = 24–40 minutes. Tested in almost every paper.

When to use: "Calculate the NPV / IRR of the project" or "Should the company invest?"

  1. Columns t0 to tn. Write the unit (Tk '000) once.
  2. Operating flows: revenue and costs in money terms (inflate each at its own rate). Relevant cash flows only.
  3. Tax on the net operating flow.
  4. Asset: cost, scrap, and tax saved on capital allowances (separate working).
  5. Working capital: show only the change each year. Recover the total at the end.
  6. Discount the net flow at the money cost of capital. Add up for NPV. State accept or reject.
  7. IRR: discount again at a second rate. IRR = L + NPV(L) ÷ [NPV(L) − NPV(H)] × (H − L).
Example data

Machine Tk 10,000k, life 4 years, scrap Tk 2,000k. Revenue Tk 8,000k a year in today's prices, inflation 5%. Cash costs Tk 4,000k in today's prices, inflation 4%. The project cuts contribution from an old product by Tk 400k a year. Working capital = 10% of next year's revenue, in place at the start of each year. Capital allowances 18% reducing balance, balancing allowance in the year of sale. Tax 25%, paid in the same year. Money cost of capital 12%. Market research already paid Tk 300k. Head office overhead allocated Tk 500k a year.

Traps
  • Money v real: inflated flows need the money rate. (1 + money) = (1 + real) × (1 + inflation). Use the real rate only if all flows are in today's prices and inflate at one rate.
  • Tax timing: if tax is paid one year in arrears, move the tax rows one column to the right and add a column.
  • Straight-line allowances: if the question gives straight-line tax depreciation, tax saved = (cost − scrap) ÷ life × tax rate each year.
  • End of project: do not forget scrap value, working capital recovery and any grant. ICAB missed these in Nov-Dec 2024 Q5.
  • Working capital: it is never taxed and never inflated twice. Show the change only.
P2

Sensitivity analysis on NPV

Typical: 4–7 marks = 8–14 minutes. Tested in most years, usually as the last part of the NPV question.

When to use: "How sensitive is the project to…" or "By how much can X change before the project fails?"

  1. Formula: sensitivity = NPV ÷ PV of the cash flows affected by the variable (after tax).
  2. Discount rate: the sensitivity is the IRR. Margin = (IRR − cost of capital) ÷ cost of capital.
  3. Rank the variables. The smallest % is the most critical.
Example data

The P1 project. NPV Tk 1,979k at 12%.

Variable PV of affected flows (Tk '000) Sensitivity = NPV ÷ PV Meaning
Revenue (price) After tax: 20,481 9.7% Price can fall by this % before NPV is nil
Cash costs After tax: 10,007 19.8% Costs can rise by this %
Machine cost Cost less PV of tax saved: 8,212 24.1% Cost can rise by this %
Scrap value After tax: 954 Over 100% Not critical
Discount rate IRR 20.0% v 12% 67% rise Rate can rise to the IRR

PV of after-tax revenue = Σ revenue × 75% × DF. Machine: each Tk 1 of cost gives tax savings with a PV of Tk 0.179, so net PV = 10,000 × (1 − 0.179). Scrap after tax = 2,000 × 75% × 0.636, because a higher scrap value cuts the balancing allowance.

Conclusion: the project is most sensitive to revenue. A 9.7% fall in price removes the whole NPV.

Traps
  • After tax: use after-tax flows when tax is in the question.
  • Discount rate ±x%: if the question says "vary the discount rate by ±4%", just recalculate the NPV at 8% and 16%. ICAB got this wrong in Nov-Dec 2024.
  • Weakness to state: only one variable changes at a time, and it gives no probability.
P3

Capital rationing – decision route

Typical: 7–12 marks = 14–24 minutes. All variants were tested in Nov-Dec 2025 Q6.

When to use: Funds are limited and there are several projects with positive NPVs.

Situation Route
No rationing (perfect market) Accept every project with a positive NPV
One period, divisible projects Rank by PI = NPV ÷ outlay in the rationed period. Take a fraction of the last one
One period, indivisible projects List the combinations that fit. Pick the highest total NPV
Mutually exclusive projects Do the ranking twice, once with each project. Pick the plan with the higher NPV
Rationing at t1 only Rank by NPV ÷ outlay at t1. Projects that need no cash at t1 are taken first
More than one period Set up a linear programme (objective and constraints)

(e) Rationing in both periods – linear programme

  • Let a, b, c, d, e = the share of each project taken.
  • Maximise NPV = 60a + 36b + 90c + 20d + 42e.
  • t0: 200a + 150b + 250c + 100d + 120e ≤ 500. t1: 50a + 100c + 20d + 30e ≤ 120.
  • Limits: 0 ≤ a, b, c, d, e ≤ 1.
Traps
  • PI works only for one rationed period and divisible projects.
  • PI definition: some questions use PV of inflows ÷ outlay. The ranking is the same. Say which one you use.
  • A project that brings in cash at t1 adds to the t1 limit. Put it on the other side of the constraint.
  • Hard v soft rationing: hard = set by the market, soft = set by management. One mark if asked.
P4

Lease v buy – net advantage of leasing

Typical: 6–8 marks = 12–16 minutes. Tested in Jul-Aug 2025.

When to use: The investment decision is already made. The question is how to finance the asset.

  1. Discount rate = after-tax cost of borrowing = interest rate × (1 − tax rate).
  2. Buy: cost, tax saved on allowances, scrap, and any costs only the owner pays.
  3. Lease: rentals and the tax relief on them. Watch the timing (advance or arrears).
  4. Compare the PV of costs. The lower PV wins. Net advantage of leasing = PV cost of buying − PV cost of leasing.
Example data

Asset cost Tk 5,000k, life 4 years, scrap Tk 500k. Allowances 18% reducing balance, balancing allowance in year 4. Owner pays maintenance of Tk 100k a year. Lease: Tk 1,400k a year for 4 years, paid in advance, and the lessor pays maintenance. Tax 25%, same year. Tax relief on a rental comes at the end of the year it covers. Bank loan costs 12% before tax.

Discount rate = 12% × (1 − 0.25) = 9%.

Traps
  • Do not include the loan and its interest in the buy option. The discount rate already covers them.
  • Leave out flows that are the same under both options (revenue, operating costs).
  • Rental timing: in advance = t0 to t3. In arrears = t1 to t4.
  • No tax: if the company pays no tax, use the pre-tax borrowing rate and ignore allowances.
P5

Parent-company (international) NPV

Typical: 12 marks = 24 minutes. Tested in Jul-Aug 2025 Q5.

When to use: A foreign parent invests in another country and asks for the NPV "from the parent's point of view".

  1. Local currency first: project cash flows and local tax, in the normal NPV layout.
  2. Remittance: take off withholding tax and any blocked funds.
  3. Exchange rates: forecast each year. Purchasing power parity: future rate = spot × (1 + local inflation) ÷ (1 + parent inflation), year by year.
  4. Convert to the parent's currency.
  5. Parent-only flows: royalties or fees received (after parent tax), extra parent tax, lost exports.
  6. Discount at the parent's cost of capital.
Example data

A US parent sets up a 3-year project in Bangladesh. Investment Tk 600m, straight-line tax depreciation, no residual value. Working capital Tk 50m, recovered at the end. Pre-tax operating cash flows Tk 340m, 400m and 440m, before a royalty of Tk 20m a year paid to the parent. Bangladesh tax 25%. Withholding tax 10% on remittances of profit (not on capital returned). US tax 30% with full credit for Bangladesh tax, so 5% extra on Bangladesh taxable profit; royalties are taxed at 30% in the US. Spot Tk 120 per USD. Inflation: Bangladesh 8%, US 2%. Parent's cost of capital 12%.

Traps
  • Rate direction: Tk per USD means divide Tk by the rate. The Taka weakens when Bangladesh inflation is higher.
  • Royalty: it is a cost in Bangladesh (saves local tax) but income for the parent. Show both sides.
  • Blocked funds: if profits cannot be remitted until the end, move them to the last year (with any local interest).
  • Discount rate: parent currency flows need the parent's cost of capital.
  • Non-financial points: political risk, exchange controls, tax law changes. Usually 2–3 easy marks.
