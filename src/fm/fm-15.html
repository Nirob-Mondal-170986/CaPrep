P17

Business valuation – several methods

Typical: 9–14 marks = 18–28 minutes. Tested in Jul-Aug 2025 Q6 (14 marks).

When to use: "Value the company / a share using the net asset, P/E and dividend methods."

  1. Net assets: book value, adjusted to realisable (or replacement) values. Gives a floor price.
  2. P/E: maintainable earnings × a suitable P/E. Cut the proxy P/E for an unlisted company.
  3. Dividend yield: dividend ÷ a suitable yield. Dividend growth: D0(1 + g) ÷ (ke − g). For minority holdings.
  4. Summary table and a short comment on which value fits the purpose.
Example data

Unlisted target, 10m shares. Book net assets Tk 800m. Property is worth Tk 300m more than book; inventory of Tk 50m is obsolete; a receivable of Tk 30m is bad. Profit after tax Tk 180m, after a one-off gain of Tk 10m (after tax) and directors' pay that is Tk 20m above market rate. Tax 25%. Listed proxy: P/E 12, dividend yield 4%. Dividend Tk 60m, growth 4%, cost of equity 12%.

Traps
  • Unlisted discount: cut the P/E (or raise the yield) by 25–30% for poor marketability. State your assumption.
  • Net assets ignore goodwill and future earnings. Say this as the weakness.
P18

Mergers and acquisitions – gain, deal NPV and maximum price

Typical: 8–12 marks = 16–24 minutes.

When to use: "What is the most the buyer should pay?", "who gains?", or "cash offer or share exchange?"

  1. Synergy = PV of extra cash flows − integration costs.
  2. Maximum price = target's value alone + synergy.
  3. Cash offer: target gains the premium. Buyer's NPV = synergy − premium.
  4. Share offer: combined value ÷ total shares = new price. Then each side's value after less value before.
  5. EPS after the deal if asked.
Example data

Buyer: 100m shares at Tk 50, earnings Tk 400m. Target: 40m shares at Tk 30, earnings Tk 120m. Synergy: Tk 60m a year after tax for ever, discounted at 12%; one-off integration cost Tk 80m. Offer 1: cash of Tk 36 a share. Offer 2: 3 buyer shares for every 4 target shares.

Traps
  • Share offer: value the new shares at the price after the merger, not the buyer's old price.
  • Target debt: if you value the target's whole cash flows, deduct its debt to get the price for the shares (Jul-Aug 2024 Q6a).
  • Bootstrapping: EPS can rise just because the buyer has a higher P/E. It is not real value.
  • Sale of surplus assets (for example the target's head office) adds to the maximum price.
P19

Free cash flow (value driver) valuation

Typical: 10–12 marks = 20–24 minutes. Tested in Jul-Aug 2024 and the 2026 mock.

When to use: The question gives sales growth, margins and investment rates, and asks for the value of a share.

  1. Sales each year from the growth rates. Note the increase in sales.
  2. Operating profit = sales × margin. Deduct tax.
  3. Investment: extra non-current assets and working capital, as % of the sales increase.
  4. Free cash flow = profit after tax − investment. Discount at the given rate.
  5. Terminal value at the end of the forecast: a multiple of the last cash flow, or FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g).
  6. Equity = total PV + short-term investments − debt. Divide by the number of shares.
Example data

Sales now Tk 400m. Growth 10%, 8%, 6% for 3 years, then 3% for ever. Operating margin 15%. Tax 25%. Extra non-current assets 20% and extra working capital 10% of the sales increase. Depreciation equals replacement spending. Discount rate 10%. Short-term investments Tk 20m. Debt Tk 150m. 20m shares.

Traps
  • Investment is on the increase in sales, not on total sales.
  • Terminal value uses the last year's discount factor, not the next year's.
  • Asset sale proceeds reduce the investment in that year (FG Ltd / Farmgate).
  • Discount rate: WACC values the whole firm (then deduct debt). Cost of equity is for cash flows to shareholders only. Use what the question gives.
